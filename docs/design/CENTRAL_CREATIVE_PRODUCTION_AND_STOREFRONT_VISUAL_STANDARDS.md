# Central Creative Production and Storefront Visual Standards

**Document type:** Operational and design governance standard
**Applies to:** Parent company, creative production team, developers, Store Owners and participating stores
**Status:** Proposed operating standard
**Owner:** Parent company
**Review cycle:** Every six months, or when storefront components and media requirements change

---

## 1. Purpose

The parent company operates the technology, infrastructure and storefront systems used by participating stores. It is therefore responsible for maintaining consistent technical and visual standards across the platform.

Product photography and video production are part of this responsibility. They are not merely optional marketing activities.

The parent company will establish and maintain a central creative production capability to produce, prepare, optimise and manage product images and videos for use across participating storefronts.

The objective is to ensure that:

- Product imagery meets consistent quality requirements.
- Images and videos fit the actual storefront components.
- Products are presented accurately and professionally.
- Storefronts maintain a consistent level of technical quality.
- Store Owners retain meaningful input into how their products and brands are represented.
- Creative assets are managed with clear ownership, approval and usage rights.

This standard applies across all participating stores, regardless of product category.

## 2. Operating principles

The creative production model follows these principles:

1. **Centralised production standards:** The parent company defines and maintains technical requirements for storefront media.
2. **Store Owner creative direction:** Store Owners provide product knowledge, brand direction, references and presentation preferences.
3. **Consistent technical quality:** Final assets must satisfy the relevant image and video requirements of the platform.
4. **Accurate product representation:** Photography and editing must not materially misrepresent the product.
5. **Clear commercial treatment:** Included production allowances and separately chargeable work must be defined.
6. **Documented approval:** Assets requiring Store Owner approval must not be published before approval.
7. **Protected creative rights:** Brand ownership, commissioned content rights and usage permissions must be addressed separately.

## 3. Central creative production function

The parent company may establish a dedicated creative production team serving all participating stores.

The function may include:

### 3.1 Photography

- Product catalogue photography.
- Clothing and apparel photography.
- Product detail and close-up photography.
- Lifestyle and contextual photography.
- Packaging photography.
- Brand-specific product shoots.
- Seasonal and promotional photography.

### 3.2 Videography

- Product demonstrations.
- Product detail videos.
- Short-form promotional videos.
- Lifestyle and campaign footage.
- Product launch content.
- Other approved storefront video formats.

### 3.3 Image and video preparation

- Image selection and editing.
- Cropping and composition.
- Colour and exposure correction.
- Background treatment where appropriate.
- Retouching that does not misrepresent the product.
- Responsive image generation.
- Image compression and format conversion.
- Video editing, encoding and optimisation.
- Asset naming, categorisation and storage.

### 3.4 Visual quality control

The team must check every production asset against the applicable technical and visual requirements before publication.

## 4. Store Owner creative participation

Centralised production does not remove the Store Owner's creative input.

The Store Owner is expected to provide relevant information about:

- The product and its intended audience.
- Brand identity and visual direction.
- Preferred product presentation.
- Styling, colours and background preferences.
- Reference images or examples.
- Important product details that must be visible.
- Any restrictions on how the product may be represented.

The creative team translates this information into a production plan that satisfies the platform's technical standards.

The Store Owner may request reasonable changes during the review process.

The parent company retains responsibility for the final technical quality and compatibility of published assets.

## 5. Standard production workflow

The standard workflow is:

1. **Product submission:** The Store Owner supplies the product and required product information.
2. **Creative brief:** The Store Owner provides brand direction, intended presentation and references.
3. **Production assessment:** The creative team determines the required photography, video, styling, equipment and production effort.
4. **Production scheduling:** The team confirms the production date, location, deliverables and any additional costs.
5. **Capture:** Photography and video are produced using the approved production approach.
6. **Post-production:** Assets are edited, optimised and prepared for the relevant storefront components.
7. **Technical quality assurance:** The team checks dimensions, ratios, formats, file sizes, visual quality and product accuracy.
8. **Store Owner review:** Assets requiring approval are presented for review.
9. **Approval and publication:** Approved assets are uploaded and assigned to the correct products and storefront locations.
10. **Asset archiving:** Originals, approved exports and relevant production records are retained according to the asset retention policy.

The workflow should be recorded in the platform or an approved production-management process as the relevant functionality becomes available.

## 6. Storefront visual and technical standards

The parent company must maintain a central media specification that reflects the actual implementation of the storefront.

The specification must define, where applicable:

| Requirement | Standard to maintain |
|---|---|
| Image aspect ratio | Ratio required by each image container |
| Image dimensions | Minimum and recommended pixel dimensions |
| Image formats | Supported formats such as WebP, JPEG and PNG |
| File size | Upload and delivery limits |
| Composition | Product positioning, framing and safe margins |
| Background | Requirements for each product and presentation type |
| Lighting | Exposure, colour accuracy and consistency |
| Responsive assets | Desktop, mobile and thumbnail variants |
| Video aspect ratio | Ratio required by each video placement |
| Video resolution | Supported and recommended resolution |
| Video duration | Limits appropriate to the placement |
| Video encoding | Supported codec, container and compression |
| Accessibility | Appropriate alternative text and captions where applicable |
| Naming | Consistent asset identification and naming |

### 6.1 Source of technical specifications

Technical specifications must be derived from the actual storefront implementation.

Developers must inspect the relevant components, image containers, responsive breakpoints and media-delivery behaviour before setting or changing production dimensions.

Do not invent universal dimensions or apply one aspect ratio to every image placement.

Where different storefront components use different proportions, the production process must create suitable derivatives rather than stretch or distort the original.

### 6.2 Specification ownership

The parent company's technical team owns the central media specification.

Changes to image containers, responsive layouts or video placements must trigger a review of the corresponding production requirements.

The creative team must be informed when technical changes affect existing production workflows.

## 7. Original files and optimised derivatives

Where practical, the production process must preserve the original high-resolution photograph or original video file.

The system may generate optimised derivatives for:

- Product cards.
- Product detail pages.
- Category listings.
- Promotional banners.
- Mobile storefronts.
- Desktop storefronts.
- Video previews and playback.

Optimised derivatives must not replace the original source file.

The asset-management process should preserve the relationship between the original and its derivatives.

## 8. Product-category production approaches

The creative team must adapt production methods to the product category while maintaining common technical standards.

Examples include:

### 8.1 Clothing and apparel

Possible production requirements:

- Front, back and side views.
- Model photography where appropriate.
- Fabric and stitching details.
- Fit and styling references.
- Lifestyle images.
- Consistent product framing.

### 8.2 Fragrances and beauty products

Possible production requirements:

- Bottle and packaging photography.
- Label and product-detail close-ups.
- Accurate colour representation.
- Texture and material details.
- Lifestyle and promotional compositions.

### 8.3 Home and lifestyle products

Possible production requirements:

- Clear product views.
- Scale and proportion references.
- Contextual lifestyle photography.
- Material and finish details.
- Product-use demonstrations where appropriate.

These are examples, not mandatory shot lists. The production brief must reflect the actual product and its intended presentation.

## 9. Quality assurance and publication

Before an asset is published, the responsible team member must verify:

- The correct product and store are associated with the asset.
- The product is represented accurately.
- The image or video is clear and usable.
- The asset satisfies the applicable technical specification.
- The asset is correctly cropped and positioned.
- Colours and product details have not been materially altered.
- The file is optimised for its intended use.
- Required Store Owner approval has been obtained.
- Required usage rights and permissions are documented.
- Alternative text or captions are supplied where required.

Assets that fail technical or content checks must be corrected before publication.

## 10. Store Owner approval and change requests

The Store Owner should be able to review the proposed presentation before publication.

The review process should support:

- Approval.
- Rejection with a reason.
- Requests for reasonable revisions.
- Clarification of product or brand requirements.

The production team must distinguish between:

- Corrections needed to meet technical standards.
- Corrections needed to represent the product accurately.
- Creative preferences requested by the Store Owner.
- New work outside the agreed production scope.

Additional work may require a separate quotation.

The parent company may reject a requested presentation if it would violate platform standards, legal requirements, third-party rights or product-accuracy requirements. The reason should be communicated.

## 11. Commercial treatment of creative production

Creative production must be commercially sustainable.

The parent company should distinguish between:

### 11.1 Initial onboarding allowance

A defined and limited amount of basic visual production may be included in an approved onboarding or trial package.

The agreement must specify:

- Number of products or assets included.
- Type of production included.
- Number of revisions.
- Production location and scheduling assumptions.
- Any exclusions.
- Whether travel or external production costs are included.

### 11.2 Standard product production

Additional product photography and video production may be charged according to an agreed rate card, production allowance or quotation.

### 11.3 Specialised production

The following may require separate quotations:

- Models and talent.
- External locations.
- Special equipment.
- Complex styling and set construction.
- Large product collections.
- Extensive retouching.
- Additional video editing.
- Urgent production.
- Travel and accommodation.
- Third-party production services.

### 11.4 Shared production days

The parent company may organise shared production days for multiple Store Owners.

The production plan must identify each Store Owner's products, deliverables, costs and approvals.

Shared production may reduce setup and travel costs, but each store's charges and deliverables must remain transparent.

### 11.5 No unlimited production assumption

A standard platform subscription does not automatically include unlimited photography, videography, editing or creative revisions.

Any included production allowance must be expressly defined in the subscription or onboarding terms.

## 12. Content ownership and usage rights

The parties must distinguish between:

- Store Owner brand assets.
- Product ownership and product information.
- Existing photographs and videos supplied by the Store Owner.
- New photographs and videos commissioned by the parent company.
- Third-party creative assets.
- Production templates and internal workflows.
- Platform components and technical systems.

The Store Owner's brand and underlying business rights must not be transferred merely because the parent company operates the storefront or produces content.

The parent company must obtain documented rights sufficient to perform the agreed production and platform services.

For commissioned photographs and videos, the relevant agreement must specify:

- Copyright ownership or licence arrangements.
- Permitted storefront usage.
- Permitted marketing and promotional usage.
- Whether the Store Owner may use the content outside the platform.
- Whether source files are included.
- Whether the parent company may reuse production templates.
- Whether portfolio or promotional use requires consent.
- Applicable third-party licences and model releases.
- Usage after the Store Owner exits the platform.

The parent company must not assume that payment for production automatically grants every possible usage right.

## 13. Asset management and traceability

Each published asset should be associated with relevant records, including:

- Asset identifier.
- Store identifier.
- Product identifier where applicable.
- Original file reference.
- Optimised derivative references.
- Production date.
- Photographer or creator.
- Editing status.
- Approval status.
- Usage rights and licence information.
- Publication status.
- Replacement or retirement history.

The asset-management process should preserve the history of significant changes and approvals.

Assets must not be silently reassigned to unrelated stores or products.

## 14. Roles and responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Parent company | Owns and maintains platform-wide standards and production governance |
| Developers | Maintain media components, technical specifications and delivery behaviour |
| Creative lead | Plans production and enforces visual standards |
| Photographer | Captures product imagery according to the approved brief |
| Videographer | Produces video assets according to the approved brief |
| Editor | Prepares, optimises and exports production assets |
| Store Owner | Provides product knowledge, brand direction and review feedback |
| Platform administrator | Ensures assets are correctly associated with stores and products |

One person may perform multiple roles during early operations. Responsibilities must still be clear.

## 15. Relationship with marketing services

Creative production and marketing are connected but separate functions.

**Creative production** prepares the visual and media assets required for the storefront and approved content placements.

**Marketing services** use approved assets to attract customers, promote products, run campaigns and grow store visibility.

A Store Owner may receive standard production support without purchasing an optional marketing package.

Marketing campaigns, advertising management and additional promotional content must be scoped and priced separately.

## 16. Governance and review

The parent company must review this standard when:

- Storefront components change.
- New product categories are introduced.
- New media formats are supported.
- Production costs materially change.
- New legal or licensing requirements apply.
- Repeated quality problems are identified.
- Store Owners provide feedback that requires a process improvement.

The technical media specification should be maintained as a separate implementation reference so developers and creative staff can use current dimensions and formats without changing this policy every time a component is updated.

## 18. Final standard

The parent company is responsible for maintaining the technical and visual quality of the platform.

Store Owners guide how their products and brands should be presented. The central creative team produces and prepares the content, while the platform team ensures that the assets work correctly across the storefront.

This model protects the quality of the shared platform while respecting the identity, direction and rights of every Store Owner.
