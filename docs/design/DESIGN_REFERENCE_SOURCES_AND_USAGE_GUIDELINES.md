## 1. Purpose

The parent company maintains a collection of external design reference websites to guide design research, inspiration, evaluation and implementation.

These websites provide examples of design approaches, layouts, interactions, visual communication and presentation patterns.

They are research references, not templates that must be copied.

The purpose of this document is to:

- Establish approved external design research sources.
- Explain what each source is useful for.
- Help designers and developers find relevant examples efficiently.
- Encourage deliberate design decisions based on the intended user experience.
- Maintain consistency with the platform's own design system.
- Avoid copying protected designs, content, assets or proprietary work.
- Establish a repeatable design research process.

## 2. Approved design reference websites

The following websites are approved reference sources.

### 2.1 Land-book

**Website:** https://land-book.com/

**Primary use:** Website and landing-page design inspiration.

Use it to research:

- Full website compositions.
- Landing-page structures.
- Hero sections.
- Typography and visual hierarchy.
- Section sequencing.
- Brand presentation.
- Layout patterns.
- Colour and imagery combinations.
- Desktop and mobile presentation where examples are available.

**When to use it:** When planning or reviewing a new storefront, landing page, brand website or major page redesign.

### 2.2 Supahero

**Website:** https://supahero.io/

**Primary use:** Hero-section and above-the-fold inspiration.

- Headline positioning.
- Image and video composition.
- Calls to action.
- Typography.
- Background treatments.
- Visual hierarchy.
- First-screen storytelling.

## 4. Design research workflow

The standard workflow is:

1. Identify the page, component or communication being designed.
2. Identify the user problem and business objective.
3. Select the relevant reference sources.
4. Review several relevant examples.
5. Record useful design patterns and explain why they work.
6. Identify patterns that should not be adopted.
7. Compare the proposed direction with the platform's design system.
8. Produce an implementation proposal or design.
9. Review responsive behaviour, accessibility and usability.
10. Document significant design decisions where necessary.

A reference should support a design decision, not replace the design process.

## 5. How references should be evaluated

Designers and developers should consider:

### 5.1 Visual hierarchy

- Is the most important information immediately clear?
- Is the reading order logical?
- Are headings, body content and actions distinguishable?
- Does the design guide attention appropriately?

### 5.2 Layout and composition

- Is the layout balanced?
- Is spacing consistent?
- Are sections arranged logically?
- Does the composition work at different screen sizes?

### 5.3 Typography

- Is the text readable?
- Are headings appropriately emphasised?
- Are font sizes and line lengths suitable?
- Does the typography support the intended brand identity?

### 5.4 Colour and contrast

- Is the colour palette coherent?
- Is contrast sufficient?
- Are actions and statuses easy to distinguish?
- Does the design remain consistent with the approved platform palette?

### 5.5 Interaction and usability

- Are actions understandable?
- Is navigation predictable?
- Are interactive elements accessible?
- Are loading, empty, error and success states considered?
- Does the design work with touch input and keyboard navigation?

### 5.6 Performance and technical feasibility

- Does the design require large or unnecessary assets?
- Can it be implemented using the existing platform architecture?
- Does it work on mobile devices?
- Are animations and media optimised?
- Does the design introduce avoidable complexity?

## 6. Relationship with the platform design system

The platform's approved design system remains authoritative.

External references must not automatically introduce:

- Unapproved colours.
- Unapproved typography.
- Inconsistent spacing systems.
- Unnecessary visual effects.
- Inaccessible contrast.
- Inconsistent component behaviour.
- Conflicting interaction patterns.
- Unnecessary dependencies.
- Design patterns that compromise performance.

Where a reference conflicts with an approved design-system rule, the design-system rule takes precedence unless an authorised design-system change is approved.

A reference may inspire a new component or design-system improvement, but the proposed change must be reviewed and documented.

## 7. Storefront identity and shared platform consistency

Participating stores may have different brand identities, product categories, audiences and visual directions.

The platform must support these differences without compromising shared usability and technical standards.

The following distinction applies:

**Store-specific identity**

- Brand colours within supported configuration.
- Store logos and approved brand assets.
- Brand-specific photography and content.
- Store voice and messaging.
- Promotional presentation.
- Approved storefront configuration.

**Shared platform standards**

- Accessibility requirements.
- Responsive behaviour.
- Component functionality.
- Technical performance.
- Security.
- Navigation usability.
- Media specifications.
- Shared interaction conventions.

A reference may inspire the visual treatment of a store-specific section, but it must not create inconsistent or unsupported platform behaviour.

## 8. Intellectual property and ethical use

External websites are sources of inspiration and research.

The team must not:

- Copy another company's complete website.
- Reproduce proprietary designs without permission.
- Download and reuse protected images without appropriate rights.
- Copy written content as original platform content.
- Reproduce a competitor's brand identity.
- Assume that publicly visible assets are free for commercial use.

The team may study general design principles and independently implement comparable patterns.

Any third-party assets used in production must have appropriate licences or permissions.

## 9. Design reference records

For significant design work, the team should record:

- Task or component name.
- Reference website.
- Relevant page or example.
- Design pattern observed.
- Reason for selecting the pattern.
- Intended adaptation.
- Design-system considerations.
- Technical considerations.
- Final implementation decision.

A reference record should explain what was learned, not merely contain a link.

## 10. Reference source maintenance

The parent company should periodically verify that each reference website:

- Remains accessible.
- Continues to serve its intended research purpose.
- Has not materially changed its content or ownership.
- Provides relevant examples.
- Remains suitable for professional design research.

New reference sources may be added through the design governance process.

A reference may be removed or replaced if it becomes unavailable, irrelevant or unsuitable.

## 11. Relationship with development and review

Design research must inform implementation, but it must not bypass engineering review.

Before implementation, developers should consider:

- Existing reusable components.
- Current frontend architecture.
- Responsive breakpoints.
- Accessibility.
- Performance.
- Browser support.
- Media requirements.
- Testing requirements.
- Compatibility with existing storefronts.

A visually attractive reference is not automatically an appropriate technical solution.

## 12. Final standard

The ten approved websites form the parent company's initial external design research library.

They should help the team make better decisions across website design, storefronts, pricing, trust, email, presentations, social media, advertising, navigation and social-sharing previews.

The objective is not to make every store look the same or copy existing websites.

The objective is to build a consistent, technically sound platform that gives every Store Owner room to express a distinct brand identity while maintaining a high standard of design and user experience.
