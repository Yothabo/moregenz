## 1. Purpose

This document defines the current operating standards for customer communication, order coordination, payment arrangements and delivery through WhatsApp.

The platform currently follows a WhatsApp-first commerce model. Customers communicate with stores through WhatsApp, while store personnel coordinate payment verification and fulfilment through the store's approved processes.

This document establishes consistent operational expectations without introducing payment gateway or automated delivery integrations that are not currently part of the platform.

## 2. Current operating model

The current customer journey is based on direct communication between the customer and the relevant store.

The expected flow is:

1. Customer discovers products through a store's storefront.
2. Customer contacts the store through WhatsApp.
3. Store personnel respond to enquiries and confirm product availability.
4. Customer and store agree on the order details.
5. Store communicates the accepted payment methods and payment instructions.
6. Customer completes payment outside the platform.
7. Store verifies payment through its approved process.
8. Store prepares the order.
9. Store and customer coordinate delivery or collection through WhatsApp.
10. Store confirms fulfilment and closes the operational order process.

The exact communication and fulfilment arrangements may differ between stores, but each store must follow the platform's minimum operational controls.

## 3. WhatsApp as a communication channel

WhatsApp is the current primary channel for customer-facing order communication.

It may be used for:

- Product enquiries.
- Availability confirmation.
- Order discussions.
- Price and delivery quotations.
- Payment instructions.
- Payment confirmation communication.
- Collection arrangements.
- Delivery coordination.
- Order progress updates.
- Customer complaints and after-sales support.

WhatsApp communication must use the correct store identity and authorised business contact details.

A store must not impersonate another store or communicate using another store's identity without explicit authorisation.

## 4. Store contact information

Each participating store must maintain accurate and approved customer-facing contact information.

This includes, where applicable:

- Store display name.
- WhatsApp business contact number.
- Customer support contact details.
- Operating hours.
- Delivery or collection information.
- Store location information where relevant.

Store contact information displayed on the storefront must be controlled through approved platform configuration.

Changes to business contact details must be reviewed and updated through the authorised process.

## 5. Product enquiry and availability

Store personnel must confirm product availability before accepting an order.

- Storefront product visibility must not be treated as proof of current stock availability.
- Product descriptions, prices and variant details must be checked against current store records.
- Store personnel must confirm the requested variant, quantity and relevant product details.
- If stock is unavailable, the customer must be informed before an order is accepted.
- Any proposed substitute must be agreed with the customer.
- Product availability must not be promised without reasonable verification.

Where stock records are maintained by the platform, store personnel should use the current store-specific inventory information.

## 6. Order agreement

Before accepting an order, the store must confirm the essential order details with the customer.

These should include:

- Products and variants.
- Quantities.
- Agreed prices.
- Applicable discounts or adjustments.
- Delivery or collection method.
- Delivery charges, where applicable.
- Customer delivery information where required.
- Expected fulfilment arrangements.
- Payment method and instructions.

The customer must have an opportunity to confirm the agreed details before the store proceeds.

Material changes to an agreed order must be communicated and accepted by the customer.

## 7. Order record management

Where the platform supports order records, the store should maintain an accurate record of the agreed transaction.

A record should include, where applicable:

- Internal order identifier.
- Store identifier.
- Product and variant references.
- Quantities.
- Agreed prices and totals.
- Customer reference.
- Order creation timestamp.
- Payment arrangement.
- Payment verification status.
- Fulfilment status.
- Delivery or collection information.
- Relevant communication references.

WhatsApp messages must not be treated as a substitute for required financial, inventory or order records.

Where an order is coordinated entirely through WhatsApp and is not yet recorded in the platform, store personnel must follow the approved interim recordkeeping process.

## 8. Payment arrangements

The current platform does not provide integrated online payment processing.

Stores communicate payment instructions through WhatsApp and customers complete payment using the methods accepted by the relevant store.

- Payment instructions must be accurate and authorised.
- Stores must communicate the correct payment recipient and reference details.
- Customers must not be asked to share passwords, PINs, one-time passwords or card security codes.
- Payment evidence must be handled carefully and only where needed for verification.
- A payment notification or customer-provided screenshot is not conclusive proof that funds have been received.
- Store personnel must verify payment through the appropriate payment account or financial record.
- Orders must not be marked as paid solely because a customer says payment has been made.
- Disputed or uncertain payments must be investigated before fulfilment proceeds.

Payment arrangements must follow the platform's financial controls and payment operating model.

## 9. Payment status

The operational process should distinguish the following states where supported by the store's records:

- Awaiting payment.
- Payment reported by customer.
- Payment under verification.
- Payment verified.
- Payment disputed.
- Payment not received.
- Payment refunded or reversed, where applicable.

Only authorised store personnel may confirm payment verification.

A payment status must reflect the evidence available at the time of the update.

## 10. Order preparation

Order preparation begins only after the store has confirmed that the order is eligible for fulfilment.

Before preparing an order:

- Confirm the agreed order details.
- Verify the relevant product availability.
- Confirm payment according to the store's approved process.
- Check any special customer instructions.
- Confirm whether delivery or collection is required.

Products must be checked for correctness, quantity and acceptable condition before dispatch or handover.

Any discrepancy must be resolved with the customer before the order is treated as ready.

## 11. Delivery and collection coordination

Delivery is currently coordinated manually between the store and customer.

WhatsApp may be used to agree on:

- Delivery address.
- Delivery availability.
- Delivery charges.
- Preferred delivery time or window.
- Collection location.
- Collection arrangements.
- Delivery contact information.
- Relevant delivery instructions.

The store is responsible for communicating realistic delivery expectations.

A delivery estimate must not be presented as a guaranteed arrival time unless the store has a reliable basis for that commitment.

Stores must communicate delays or changes as soon as reasonably possible.

## 12. Delivery responsibility and evidence

Each store must establish who is responsible for arranging and completing delivery.

Depending on the store's operating model, delivery may be handled by:

- Store personnel.
- An independently arranged courier.
- A third-party delivery service.
- Customer collection.

Where delivery evidence is available, the store should retain an appropriate reference, such as:

- Courier tracking reference.
- Delivery confirmation.
- Collection acknowledgement.
- Relevant customer communication.
- Internal fulfilment record.

Customer delivery information must only be shared with parties who need it to complete the delivery.

## 13. Fulfilment status

Where order management supports fulfilment statuses, use clear and consistent states.

Suggested states include:

- Not started.
- Preparing.
- Ready for collection.
- Awaiting dispatch.
- Dispatched.
- Out for delivery.
- Delivered.
- Collected.
- Delivery exception.
- Cancelled.

Not every store will use every status.

Statuses must reflect actual operational progress. An order must not be marked delivered merely because it has been dispatched.

## 14. Customer communication standards

Store communication must be clear, accurate and professional.

Stores should:

- Acknowledge customer enquiries within their published operating arrangements.
- Confirm material order details before accepting an order.
- Communicate payment instructions clearly.
- Notify customers of payment verification outcomes.
- Provide reasonable updates during preparation and delivery.
- Communicate delays, stock issues and delivery exceptions promptly.
- Keep customer communication relevant to the order.
- Avoid unsupported promises and misleading statements.

Customers must be informed when an order cannot be fulfilled as agreed.

## 15. Cancellations, changes and returns

Stores must communicate their applicable cancellation, return and exchange terms before finalising an order where relevant.

- Record customer cancellation requests.
- Confirm whether preparation or dispatch has already occurred.
- Assess the request against the store's approved terms and applicable requirements.
- Record agreed changes to products, quantities, delivery or payment.
- Preserve the original order history.
- Process any refund or financial adjustment through authorised procedures.
- Communicate the outcome to the customer.

Stores must not invent or apply inconsistent return conditions.

Applicable consumer protection obligations require appropriate legal review.

## 16. Customer information and privacy

Customer information shared through WhatsApp must be handled responsibly.

- Collect only information required to process the enquiry or order.
- Avoid requesting unnecessary identity documents or sensitive information.
- Do not request account passwords, banking PINs or one-time passwords.
- Restrict access to business communication accounts.
- Avoid forwarding customer details to unrelated people or groups.
- Share delivery information only with authorised delivery parties.
- Protect screenshots and documents containing personal or financial information.
- Follow approved retention and disposal requirements.

WhatsApp access must be removed from personnel who no longer require it for their duties.

## 17. Store personnel responsibilities

Store personnel handling WhatsApp orders must:

- Use authorised store communication accounts.
- Confirm product availability and order details.
- Communicate approved payment instructions.
- Verify payment before confirming paid status.
- Maintain required order and financial records.
- Coordinate fulfilment and delivery.
- Escalate disputes and exceptions.
- Protect customer information.
- Avoid making commitments outside their authority.

Store administrators remain responsible for ensuring that staff access and responsibilities are appropriately assigned.

## 18. Exceptions and escalation

Escalate an issue when:

- Payment cannot be verified.
- The customer disputes a payment or order.
- A suspected fraudulent payment is reported.
- Stock cannot be fulfilled as agreed.
- A delivery is lost, delayed or disputed.
- Customer information may have been exposed.
- A refund or financial adjustment requires approval.
- The store cannot resolve the issue within its authority.
- The issue indicates a wider platform problem.

Security, privacy and financial incidents must follow their respective incident and escalation procedures.

## 19. Platform responsibilities

The platform provides the shared technical environment and approved store configuration.

The platform must not imply that it currently:

- Processes customer payments through integrated gateways.
- Automatically verifies external bank transfers.
- Controls third-party courier operations.
- Guarantees delivery times.
- Monitors or automatically records every WhatsApp conversation.
- Automatically reconciles payments completed outside the platform.

Any future platform capability in these areas must be formally designed, approved, implemented and tested.

## 20. Future operational development

The WhatsApp-first model is the current operating approach, not a permanent limitation on platform growth.

As stores mature, the platform may consider:

- Integrated online payment providers.
- Structured checkout and payment confirmation.
- Automated order notifications.
- Courier and delivery service integrations.
- Delivery tracking.
- Automated customer order updates.
- Centralised customer communication records.
- More advanced fulfilment workflows.

Potential payment providers may include Stripe, PayPal and other suitable providers.

These capabilities remain future considerations. They must not be treated as existing functionality or introduced before the platform and participating stores meet the required operational, financial, security and compliance standards.

Future integrations must preserve store isolation, financial attribution, customer privacy and operational accountability.

## 21. Acceptance criteria

The current WhatsApp-first operating model is acceptable when:

- Every store has accurate customer-facing contact information.
- Customers can communicate with the correct store.
- Product availability is confirmed before order acceptance.
- Agreed order details are recorded appropriately.
- Payment instructions are accurate and authorised.
- Payment is verified before paid status is confirmed.
- Order preparation follows approved checks.
- Delivery or collection arrangements are communicated clearly.
- Fulfilment statuses reflect actual progress.
- Customer information is protected.
- Cancellations, disputes and exceptions follow documented procedures.
- Stores maintain appropriate order and financial records.
- Future integrations are not represented as current capabilities.
