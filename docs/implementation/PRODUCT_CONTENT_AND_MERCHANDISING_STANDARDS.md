## 1. Purpose

This document defines standards for creating, maintaining, presenting and managing product content across the platform and participating stores.

It establishes consistent expectations for product information, images, descriptions, pricing presentation, product categorisation and storefront merchandising.

The objective is to maintain accurate, useful and trustworthy product listings while allowing stores to maintain their own visual identity within approved platform capabilities.

## 2. Scope

These standards apply to:

- Product names and descriptions.
- Product images and media.
- Product categories and attributes.
- Brands and product identifiers.
- Variants and specifications.
- Product availability presentation.
- Store-specific product listings.
- Product pricing presentation.
- Featured products and merchandising.
- Product content review and publication.

These standards complement the product catalogue architecture, inventory architecture, file and object storage standards, accessibility standards and storefront design standards.

## 3. Product information ownership

The platform must distinguish between product source information and store-specific listing information.

### 3.1 Product source information

Product source information describes the underlying product and may include:

- Product name.
- Brand.
- Manufacturer or source.
- General product description.
- Product specifications.
- Product attributes.
- Product identifiers.
- Variant definitions.
- Product media.

Source ownership must be recorded according to the approved catalogue model.

### 3.2 Store listing information

A store listing represents how a store makes a product available to its customers.

It may include:

- Store-specific product description.
- Store-specific imagery where permitted.
- Selling price.
- Promotional price.
- Availability.
- Listing status.
- Store-specific merchandising information.
- Relevant commercial terms.

Store-specific listing changes must not unintentionally overwrite shared source information.

## 4. Product naming

Product names must be clear, accurate and recognisable.

- Use the product's commonly understood name.
- Include a model or variant identifier where necessary.
- Avoid unnecessary promotional language in product names.
- Avoid keyword stuffing.
- Do not use misleading brand or manufacturer references.
- Maintain consistent naming conventions across related products.
- Distinguish variants clearly where customers could otherwise confuse them.

Product names should remain readable on mobile devices and within product cards.

## 5. Product descriptions

Descriptions should help customers understand what they are considering.

A useful product description should:

- Explain what the product is.
- Identify its relevant features.
- Describe practical benefits without unsupported claims.
- Include important specifications where applicable.
- Clarify material, size, capacity or compatibility where relevant.
- Identify limitations or important usage conditions.
- Use natural and understandable language.

Avoid:

- Repetitive promotional statements.
- Unsupported performance claims.
- Misleading guarantees.
- Unverified health, safety or environmental claims.
- Unnecessary technical jargon.
- Copy that does not describe the actual product.

Descriptions should be specific to the product rather than generic text reused across unrelated listings.

## 6. Product images and media

Product imagery must accurately represent the item being offered.

### 6.1 Image quality

Product images should:

- Show the actual product or an accurate representation.
- Have sufficient resolution for supported storefront layouts.
- Use appropriate lighting and exposure.
- Avoid excessive filters or editing.
- Present the product clearly.
- Avoid distracting backgrounds where they reduce product visibility.
- Maintain consistent image treatment within related product collections.

### 6.2 Image accuracy

Images must not materially misrepresent:

- Product colour.
- Product size or proportions.
- Packaging.
- Included accessories.
- Product condition.
- Product features.

If an image is illustrative rather than an exact representation, the listing must make that clear where the distinction could affect a customer's decision.

### 6.3 Image formats and optimisation

- Use approved image formats supported by the platform.
- Optimise images for web delivery.
- Avoid unnecessarily large source files.
- Preserve sufficient quality after compression.
- Use responsive image delivery where supported.
- Provide appropriate alternative text.
- Avoid embedding important product information only within an image.

### 6.4 Image ownership and permissions

Stores must have the right to use uploaded product images.

Do not upload copyrighted material without appropriate permission or a valid basis for use.

Where manufacturer-provided images are used, the store must follow the applicable usage terms.

## 7. Product categorisation

Products must be assigned to meaningful categories.

- Use the approved category structure.
- Avoid creating duplicate categories with equivalent meanings.
- Place products in categories that accurately describe them.
- Use attributes for product characteristics rather than creating unnecessary categories.
- Maintain consistent category naming.
- Review category placement when products change.

Store-specific category presentation must remain within approved platform configuration.

## 8. Product attributes and specifications

Attributes should provide structured information that supports comparison and discovery.

Examples include:

- Size.
- Colour.
- Material.
- Capacity.
- Weight.
- Fragrance family.
- Compatibility.
- Dimensions.
- Model.
- Manufacturer.
- Usage information.

Attribute values must be accurate and consistently formatted.

Use structured fields where the platform supports them rather than placing every specification inside free-text descriptions.

Do not invent missing specifications.

## 9. Variants

Variants must represent meaningful differences in a product.

Examples include size, colour, volume or other selectable product characteristics.

- Each variant must be identifiable.
- Variant names must be clear.
- Variant-specific specifications must be accurate.
- Variant availability must be maintained independently where required.
- Variant SKU and inventory records must follow the approved catalogue and inventory model.
- A customer must be able to identify the selected variant before contacting the store.
- Variant imagery should represent the selected option where available.

A product with multiple variants must not imply that every variant is available merely because one variant is available.

## 10. Pricing presentation

Product prices must be presented clearly and consistently.

- Display the correct store-specific selling price.
- Identify the applicable currency.
- Distinguish regular prices from promotional prices.
- Do not display misleading discounts.
- Do not imply a promotion exists when the reference price is not valid.
- Ensure displayed prices reflect approved store listing data.
- Make relevant delivery charges or additional costs clear before order agreement.
- Avoid presenting estimated prices as confirmed prices.

The storefront must not calculate authoritative order totals independently of the platform's trusted business rules.

## 11. Availability presentation

Product visibility and stock availability must be treated as separate concepts.

A product may be:

- Published and available.
- Published but temporarily unavailable.
- Unpublished.
- Discontinued.
- Awaiting confirmation.

Availability must reflect the relevant store's inventory or approved manual confirmation process.

The platform must not imply that a product is available for immediate fulfilment without sufficient supporting information.

Where stock is managed manually, storefront wording must avoid promising real-time availability.

## 12. Merchandising and product placement

Merchandising determines how products are organised and presented to customers.

Approved merchandising capabilities may include:

- Featured products.
- Category collections.
- New arrivals.
- Promotional listings.
- Related products.
- Store-defined product ordering.
- Seasonal collections.

Merchandising decisions must not alter product ownership, inventory ownership or financial attribution.

### 12.1 Featured products

Featured products should be selected for a clear commercial or informational reason.

- Featured status must be controlled by authorised store personnel.
- Featured products must remain within the store's authorised catalogue.
- Unavailable products must not be presented as immediately purchasable.
- Featured placement must not override product safety, accuracy or publication requirements.

### 12.2 Promotional content

Promotional listings must have accurate conditions.

Where applicable, record:

- Promotion start and end dates.
- Applicable products or variants.
- Discount or promotional price.
- Eligibility conditions.
- Store ownership.
- Relevant approval.

Expired promotions must not continue to appear as active offers.

## 13. Storefront consistency

Storefronts may have different approved branding and visual presentation.

However:

- Product information must remain accurate.
- Core product details must remain discoverable.
- Product selection and variant behaviour must be understandable.
- Pricing and availability information must be presented consistently in meaning.
- Approved platform components and configuration must be used.
- Stores must not introduce unsupported runtime page composition or navigation behaviour.

Visual differences must not compromise essential product information or customer understanding.

## 14. Accessibility and readability

Product content must be accessible across supported devices.

- Use meaningful alternative text for informative product images.
- Avoid text embedded only in product images.
- Maintain sufficient text contrast.
- Use readable font sizes.
- Structure specifications with clear labels.
- Ensure product controls can be operated using supported input methods.
- Do not communicate product availability or promotion status through colour alone.
- Ensure product information remains understandable when images fail to load.

## 15. Content review and publication

Product content must be reviewed before publication.

The review should verify:

- Product identity.
- Product ownership or authorised source.
- Product name and description.
- Image accuracy and usage rights.
- Category and attributes.
- Variant information.
- Store-specific price.
- Availability.
- Promotional conditions.
- Publication status.

Only authorised personnel may publish or materially modify listings within their scope.

Changes to shared source information must follow the catalogue ownership and permission model.

## 16. Product content updates

Product information must be maintained when relevant details change.

Updates may include:

- Correcting inaccurate descriptions.
- Replacing outdated images.
- Updating specifications.
- Adding or removing variants.
- Correcting category placement.
- Updating store-specific prices.
- Changing availability.
- Ending expired promotions.

Changes must preserve the distinction between shared product data and store-specific listing data.

Where changes materially affect an existing customer order, the store must handle the matter through its approved customer communication process.

## 17. Unpublishing and discontinuation

Unpublishing a listing must not automatically delete its underlying product or historical records.

- Unpublishing removes the listing from the relevant customer-facing presentation.
- Discontinuation indicates that the product is no longer offered.
- Historical order and financial references must remain intact.
- Shared product information must not be deleted merely because one store stops selling the product.
- Re-publication must follow the normal content and availability checks.

## 18. Data quality and content integrity

The platform should validate product content wherever practical.

Validation may include:

- Required field checks.
- Duplicate identifier checks.
- Valid image type and size checks.
- Valid price and currency checks.
- Variant consistency checks.
- Category reference validation.
- Publication eligibility checks.
- Promotion date validation.

Validation must occur server-side for authoritative records.

The system should distinguish incomplete content from invalid content and provide useful correction feedback.

## 19. Content security

- Uploaded files must follow the approved file and object storage standards.
- Validate file types and sizes.
- Restrict upload operations to authorised users.
- Do not trust file extensions alone.
- Prevent unauthorised modification of shared product information.
- Apply store-scoped access controls.
- Avoid exposing internal storage paths or private metadata.
- Preserve audit history for important product content changes.

## 20. Acceptance criteria

Product content and merchandising are acceptable when:

- Product source ownership and store listing data remain separate.
- Product names and descriptions are accurate.
- Product images represent the actual offering.
- Image usage rights are respected.
- Categories and attributes are meaningful.
- Variants are clearly identified.
- Prices and promotions are accurate.
- Availability is not misleading.
- Featured products are authorised.
- Product content is accessible and readable.
- Publication and update permissions are enforced.
- Unpublishing preserves historical records.
- Validation and audit controls are implemented.
