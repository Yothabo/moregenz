## 1. Purpose

This document establishes standards for reporting, interpreting and reviewing the business performance of stores operating on the platform.

The objective is to give every Store Owner access to accurate, understandable and actionable information about their store.

Performance reporting must support better business decisions, identify operational problems early and help Store Owners build sustainable businesses.

Reports must be based on reliable records. Estimates, incomplete data and unverified transactions must never be presented as confirmed business results.

## 2. Scope

These standards apply to:

- Store Owners.
- Parent company management.
- Finance and reconciliation teams.
- Central marketing and creative teams.
- Customer support and store operations teams.
- Employees and contractors responsible for reporting or analysing store performance.

They cover operational, commercial, marketing, inventory and customer performance reporting.

## 3. Reporting principles

All performance reporting must follow these principles:

1. Accuracy: figures must come from verified platform records or clearly identified external sources.
2. Transparency: calculations, reporting periods and exclusions must be understandable.
3. Store isolation: Store Owners may only access their authorised store's private business information.
4. Consistency: comparable metrics must use consistent definitions across stores.
5. Context: reports must explain relevant limitations and avoid misleading conclusions.
6. Actionability: reporting should help Store Owners identify practical improvements.
7. Accountability: material adjustments to reported figures must be traceable.
8. Fairness: stores must be assessed with consideration for their age, category, operating model and available data.

## 4. Store performance dashboard

Each Store Owner should have access to a business performance dashboard.

The dashboard should present the following areas where reliable data is available.

### 4.1 Sales performance

- Gross sales.
- Discounts applied.
- Refunds and cancellations.
- Net sales.
- Completed orders.
- Average order value.
- Sales trends over time.
- Sales by product or category.

Gross sales, net sales and cash received must be presented as separate concepts.

An order must not automatically count as completed revenue simply because it was created or submitted.

### 4.2 Order performance

- Total orders received.
- Pending orders.
- Confirmed orders.
- Orders awaiting payment.
- Orders awaiting fulfilment.
- Completed orders.
- Cancelled orders.
- Returned or refunded orders.
- Average fulfilment time.
- Order completion rate.

The platform must define the status transitions used in these calculations.

### 4.3 Product performance

Where sufficient data exists, reports should include:

- Best-selling products.
- Products with low sales.
- Product views.
- Product conversion rate.
- Revenue by product.
- Product returns.
- Product availability.
- Stock movement.
- Products with repeated customer complaints.

Product performance must not be interpreted without considering stock availability, pricing changes and promotional activity.

### 4.4 Inventory performance

Reports should include relevant measures such as:

- Current recorded stock.
- Stock received.
- Stock sold.
- Stock adjustments.
- Stock losses.
- Stockout incidents.
- Inventory turnover.
- Products requiring a stocktake.

Inventory figures must distinguish recorded system quantities from physically verified quantities.

### 4.5 Customer performance

Where lawful and supported by reliable records, reports may include:

- New customers.
- Returning customers.
- Repeat purchase rate.
- Customer purchase frequency.
- Average customer order value.
- Customer feedback trends.
- Customer complaints and resolutions.

Customer identity and personal information must not be unnecessarily exposed through analytics.

Store Owners must not receive private customer information belonging exclusively to another store.

### 4.6 Marketing performance

Where campaigns and tracking data are available, reports may include:

- Campaign reach.
- Impressions.
- Engagement.
- Link clicks.
- Website visits.
- Product views.
- Leads.
- Orders attributed to campaigns.
- Campaign expenditure.
- Cost per lead.
- Cost per acquisition.
- Return on advertising spend.

Marketing attribution must identify its measurement method and limitations.

An attributed order does not necessarily prove that a campaign independently caused the purchase.

Organic traffic, paid advertising, direct visits and other available traffic sources should be distinguished where tracking permits.

## 5. Financial reporting and reconciliation

Performance dashboards must not replace formal financial records.

The platform should distinguish:

- Gross sales.
- Discounts.
- Refunds.
- Net sales.
- Payment amounts received.
- Pending payments.
- Verified payments.
- Payment-provider charges.
- Platform charges.
- Approved service fees.
- Store Owner settlements.
- Outstanding balances.

A sale recorded in the order system must not automatically be treated as money received.

Manual payments must only be included in verified payment totals after the required verification process.

Settlement figures must reconcile with the applicable financial records and the Store Owner Revenue Settlement and Financial Reconciliation Policy.

Where reconciliation is incomplete, the report must clearly show the affected amounts as pending or unverified.

## 6. Reporting periods

The platform should support:

- Daily summaries.
- Weekly summaries.
- Monthly reports.
- Quarterly reviews.
- Annual performance summaries.
- Custom date ranges.

Every report must display:

- Start and end dates.
- Date and time of generation.
- Applicable time zone.
- Currency.
- Whether figures are final, provisional or incomplete.

Comparisons between periods must use equivalent date ranges where possible.

A seven-day period should not be compared with a thirty-day period without clearly explaining the difference.

## 7. Metric definitions

The platform must maintain a central metric dictionary.

Each metric must document:

- Metric name.
- Business meaning.
- Calculation method.
- Required source records.
- Included and excluded records.
- Reporting period.
- Known limitations.
- Responsible owner.

Examples:

### Net sales

Net sales should represent eligible sales after applicable discounts and refunds, using a documented accounting treatment.

### Average order value

Average order value should be calculated using the defined eligible order value divided by the number of eligible orders.

Cancelled and unpaid orders must be handled according to the documented metric definition.

### Order completion rate

The calculation should use completed eligible orders divided by the defined population of eligible orders.

### Product conversion rate

Where reliable tracking exists, product conversion should use eligible product purchases divided by the corresponding measurable product visits.

The platform must not calculate or display a metric when the required underlying data is unavailable or materially incomplete.

## 8. Store comparisons and benchmarking

Store comparisons may help identify opportunities, but must be used carefully.

The parent company may develop benchmarks using appropriately grouped stores.

Comparisons should account for:

- Store age.
- Product category.
- Store size.
- Available inventory.
- Operating model.
- Pricing.
- Marketing expenditure.
- Seasonal demand.
- Reporting period.
- Data completeness.

A new store must not be judged against an established store without relevant context.

Private financial, customer and operational records must not be exposed to other Store Owners through benchmarking.

Where possible, shared benchmarks should be aggregated or anonymised.

## 9. Store Owner performance reviews

The parent company should support periodic performance reviews.

A review should focus on:

1. What has happened during the reporting period.
2. Which results improved or declined.
3. What factors may explain the changes.
4. Which operational issues require attention.
5. What opportunities exist.
6. What actions should be prioritised.

Reviews must be constructive and evidence-based.

A Store Owner should be given an opportunity to explain relevant circumstances before material conclusions are reached.

Performance reviews are intended to support business development, not automatically trigger penalties.

## 10. Recommendations and improvement plans

Recommendations should be practical, measurable and proportionate to the Store Owner's resources.

Examples include:

- Improving product descriptions and images.
- Reviewing product pricing.
- Addressing repeated stockouts.
- Improving fulfilment times.
- Promoting products with strong customer interest.
- Reviewing products with high return rates.
- Improving campaign targeting.
- Following up on abandoned enquiries.
- Strengthening customer retention.

Where an improvement plan is agreed, it should identify:

- The issue being addressed.
- The proposed action.
- The responsible person.
- The expected completion date.
- The measure of success.
- The review date.

Recommendations must not be presented as guaranteed revenue or growth outcomes.

## 11. Data quality and reporting limitations

The platform must identify conditions that could affect reporting accuracy.

Examples include:

- Unverified manual payments.
- Missing order records.
- Delayed payment-provider notifications.
- Incorrect product prices.
- Incomplete stock records.
- Missing campaign tracking.
- Duplicate transactions.
- Pending refunds.
- Integration outages.

Where data is incomplete, the report must disclose the limitation.

Material corrections must be traceable, including the reason for the correction and the responsible authorised person.

Historical reports must not be silently rewritten in a way that hides previous values or adjustments.

## 12. Access and confidentiality

Store Owners may access performance information for stores they are currently authorised to operate.

Parent company personnel may access information only where required by their responsibilities and permissions.

The platform must prevent Store Owners from accessing:

- Other stores' private sales records.
- Other stores' customer lists.
- Other stores' individual order histories.
- Other stores' private financial settlements.
- Unauthorised marketing and supplier records.

Parent company management may access consolidated reporting for legitimate operational, financial and governance purposes.

Access to reports must follow the Platform Data Governance and Information Lifecycle Policy.

## 13. Report exports and sharing

Where supported, Store Owners should be able to export relevant reports in practical formats such as CSV or PDF.

Exports must:

- Respect the user's permissions.
- Identify the reporting period.
- Include currency and metric definitions where necessary.
- Avoid exposing unnecessary personal information.
- Preserve the distinction between verified and provisional figures.

The platform should record sensitive report exports where appropriate.

Store Owners remain responsible for handling exported business information securely.

## 14. Notifications and performance alerts

The platform may notify Store Owners about material business events, including:

- Unusually high cancellation rates.
- Repeated stockouts.
- Significant sales declines.
- Unresolved payment discrepancies.
- Increasing return rates.
- Delayed fulfilment.
- Products receiving repeated complaints.

Alerts must be based on documented thresholds or reliable business rules.

Notifications should avoid unnecessary noise and must provide enough context for the Store Owner to understand the issue.

An alert must not imply misconduct or failure without supporting evidence.

## 15. Parent company reporting

The parent company should maintain consolidated operational reporting covering the platform as a whole.

This may include:

- Number of active stores.
- Store onboarding and retention.
- Total platform order volume.
- Aggregate sales activity.
- Platform availability.
- Payment reconciliation performance.
- Customer support performance.
- Marketing activity.
- Infrastructure costs.
- Store Owner satisfaction.
- Store growth and operational risks.

Consolidated reporting must not be used to conceal individual store problems or misrepresent the performance of the platform.

Internal management reports must distinguish platform revenue from Store Owner sales.

## 16. Reporting disputes and corrections

A Store Owner may dispute a reported figure by submitting:

- The affected report or metric.
- The relevant date or transaction.
- The reason for the dispute.
- Supporting information where available.

The responsible team must investigate against the underlying records.

If a correction is justified:

1. The affected metric must be corrected.
2. The reason must be recorded.
3. The correction must be traceable.
4. Relevant affected reports should be recalculated where appropriate.
5. The Store Owner must receive an explanation.

Disputes must not be dismissed solely because a dashboard displays a calculated value.

## 17. Implementation requirements

Before implementing performance reporting, the platform team must:

- Identify authoritative data sources for each metric.
- Establish consistent metric definitions.
- Confirm order and payment status handling.
- Define reporting-period rules.
- Implement store-level access controls.
- Prevent cross-store data leakage.
- Establish reconciliation dependencies.
- Identify provisional and final reporting states.
- Provide report generation and export capabilities.
- Implement appropriate audit records.
- Test calculations against known transaction scenarios.

Reports must be generated from authoritative records rather than manually maintained dashboard totals.

## 18. Testing requirements

Testing must cover:

- Sales calculations.
- Discounts and refunds.
- Cancelled and unpaid orders.
- Verified and unverified payments.
- Duplicate transactions.
- Store-level reporting permissions.
- Cross-store data isolation.
- Date-range boundaries.
- Currency handling.
- Time-zone handling.
- Missing and delayed data.
- Historical corrections.
- Export permissions.
- Performance under large reporting volumes.

Financial reporting calculations must be tested against independently verified expected results.

## 19. Review and maintenance

These standards should be reviewed annually and whenever material changes affect:

- The commercial model.
- Financial reconciliation.
- Order lifecycle rules.
- Store Owner permissions.
- Data governance.
- Marketing measurement.
- Reporting infrastructure.
- Applicable legal requirements.

Changes to metric definitions must be documented and communicated to affected Store Owners.

## 20. Governing principle

Every Store Owner should be able to understand how their store is performing, where the reported figures come from and what actions may improve the business.

The parent company must provide honest reporting, protect confidential information and use performance data to support sustainable entrepreneurship.

Performance reporting must inform decisions, not manufacture success or punish Store Owners for circumstances outside their reasonable control.
