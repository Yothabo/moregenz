## 1. Purpose

This policy defines how financial records are created, classified, stored, accessed, retained, archived and disposed of across the platform and participating stores.

It establishes controls for financial record integrity, accountability, confidentiality and availability.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to financial records relating to:

- Platform operations.
- Store operations.
- Customer transactions.
- Orders and payments.
- Refunds and reversals.
- Provider fees and settlements.
- Revenue and expenses.
- Commissions and service charges.
- Stock assistance.
- Capital advances and repayments.
- Financial obligations.
- Accounting entries.
- Reconciliation.
- Financial approvals.
- Financial disputes and investigations.

It applies to records in databases, files, reports, provider systems, backups and authorised external accounting systems.

## 3. Policy Principles

Financial records must be:

- Accurate and attributable.
- Protected against unauthorised changes.
- Linked to their relevant business transactions.
- Available to authorised users.
- Retained according to approved requirements.
- Distinguishable by platform and store ownership.
- Supported by appropriate evidence.
- Traceable throughout their lifecycle.
- Disposed of securely when permitted.

## 4. Financial Record Ownership

Financial record ownership must reflect the underlying business relationship and applicable agreements.

Platform financial records must remain distinct from store financial records.

A store's records must not become platform-owned merely because they are stored in a shared database.

Where a transaction involves multiple parties, the record must preserve the relevant relationships and attribution.

Technical database ownership does not independently determine legal ownership of financial information.

## 5. Financial Record Categories

Financial records should be classified into appropriate categories.

### 5.1 Transaction Records

Examples include:

- Orders.
- Payments.
- Refunds.
- Reversals.
- Chargebacks.
- Provider transaction references.
- Settlement transactions.

### 5.2 Accounting Records

Examples include:

- Journal entries.
- Ledger entries.
- Account balances.
- Period-close records.
- Financial adjustments.

### 5.3 Supporting Evidence

Examples include:

- Invoices.
- Receipts.
- Supplier statements.
- Bank statements.
- Provider settlement reports.
- Approved agreements.
- Expense documentation.

### 5.4 Approval and Governance Records

Examples include:

- Payment approvals.
- Refund approvals.
- Expense approvals.
- Financial assistance decisions.
- Adjustment approvals.
- Delegated financial authority.
- Reconciliation sign-offs.

### 5.5 Investigation Records

Examples include:

- Discrepancy reports.
- Dispute evidence.
- Fraud investigation records.
- Control failure reports.
- Corrective action records.

## 6. Record Creation

Financial records must be created through authorised business processes.

Each applicable record should capture:

- Unique reference.
- Record type.
- Date and time.
- Amount and currency where relevant.
- Store or platform attribution.
- Related transaction references.
- Initiating user or system process.
- Relevant status.
- Supporting evidence references.

Records must not rely on free-text descriptions as the only means of establishing transaction identity or ownership.

## 7. Record Integrity

Financial records must be protected against unauthorised alteration or deletion.

Controls should include:

- Backend authorisation.
- Validated business operations.
- Database integrity constraints.
- Transactional consistency.
- Audit logging.
- Restricted administrative access.
- Controlled financial adjustments.
- Backup and recovery controls.

Where a correction is necessary, preserve the original record and create a traceable correction or adjustment.

## 8. Record Attribution

Financial records must retain the correct:

- Platform or store owner.
- Store context.
- Customer or supplier relationship where relevant.
- Order or transaction reference.
- Payment provider reference.
- Financial account.
- Currency.
- Reporting period.

Store attribution must be enforced by trusted backend processes.

A client-supplied store identifier must not be sufficient to establish authority over a financial record.

## 9. Record Classification

Financial records must receive a sensitivity classification appropriate to their content.

Classification should consider:

- Personal information.
- Banking information.
- Commercially confidential information.
- Payment references.
- Provider credentials.
- Investigation evidence.
- Financial performance data.
- Legal or contractual restrictions.

Access and handling requirements must follow the applicable security and data governance controls.

## 10. Access and Use

Financial records may only be accessed for an authorised business purpose.

Access must be based on:

- Verified identity.
- Current account status.
- Assigned permissions.
- Active store membership where applicable.
- Legitimate operational or governance responsibility.

Bulk exports and access to sensitive financial evidence require additional controls appropriate to the risk.

Financial information must not be shared with another store merely because both stores participate in the same platform.

## 11. External Sharing

Financial records may be shared with authorised external parties where there is a valid business, contractual or legal basis.

Before sharing, confirm:

- Recipient identity.
- Purpose.
- Scope of information.
- Authorisation.
- Transfer method.
- Applicable confidentiality requirements.
- Retention expectations.

Only the information necessary for the approved purpose should be disclosed.

## 12. Financial Evidence

Supporting evidence must be linked to the relevant transaction or financial record.

Evidence should retain:

- Source.
- Date received or created.
- Related transaction.
- Responsible party.
- Relevant approval.
- Storage reference.
- Integrity or verification information where supported.

Evidence must not be replaced without preserving the history and reason for replacement.

## 13. Record Retention

Retention periods must be established according to applicable legal obligations, accounting requirements, contractual commitments, operational needs and legitimate business purposes.

This policy does not invent statutory retention periods.

The platform and participating stores must obtain appropriate South African professional advice before finalising retention schedules.

Until approved schedules exist, records must not be deleted merely because they are old or no longer visible in ordinary operational screens.

## 14. Retention Schedule

A formal retention schedule should define, for each record category:

- Record owner.
- Record type.
- Business purpose.
- Applicable legal or contractual basis.
- Retention trigger.
- Retention period.
- Storage location.
- Access restrictions.
- Archive requirements.
- Disposal method.
- Responsible authority.

The schedule must distinguish platform records from store records where their obligations differ.

## 15. Historical Transactions

Historical financial transactions must remain traceable after:

- Store suspension.
- Store deactivation.
- Store ownership changes.
- Storefront replacement.
- Application migration.
- Provider changes.
- Employee departure.
- Account closure.

Historical records must not be removed merely to simplify current operational data.

## 16. Archiving

Records that are no longer required for routine operations may be archived where permitted.

Archiving must preserve:

- Record identity.
- Ownership and attribution.
- Transaction relationships.
- Relevant timestamps.
- Supporting evidence.
- Audit history.
- Required retrieval capability.

Archived records must remain protected and accessible to authorised reviewers.

## 17. Legal Holds and Investigations

Where records are subject to a legal hold, dispute, audit, investigation or other authorised preservation requirement:

- Suspend relevant disposal activities.
- Identify the affected records and systems.
- Record the authority and scope of the hold.
- Restrict unauthorised access.
- Preserve related evidence.
- Track release of the hold.

A legal hold must take precedence over ordinary disposal schedules to the extent required by the applicable authority.

## 18. Deletion and Disposal

Financial records may only be disposed of when:

- The applicable retention period has been satisfied.
- No legal hold or unresolved preservation requirement applies.
- The responsible authority has approved disposal where required.
- Related dependencies have been considered.
- The disposal method is appropriate.

Disposal must be recorded where required.

Historical financial records must not be deleted to conceal losses, disputes, mistakes or unauthorised activity.

## 19. Personal Information in Financial Records

Financial records may contain personal information relating to customers, employees, suppliers and other individuals.

Processing must follow applicable privacy and data governance requirements.

Controls should include:

- Purpose limitation.
- Data minimisation.
- Access restrictions.
- Secure transmission.
- Appropriate retention.
- Controlled disclosure.
- Secure disposal.
- Incident reporting.

Where financial record retention conflicts with a deletion request, the matter must be assessed against applicable legal obligations and professional advice.

## 20. Payment Information

Payment information must be minimised.

The platform should retain provider references, transaction status, amounts and other necessary information rather than unnecessary sensitive payment credentials.

Full card numbers, CVV values and restricted cardholder data must not be stored unless a separately approved architecture and compliance assessment explicitly permits it.

Provider tokens and payment references must be protected according to their sensitivity.

## 21. Backups and Replicas

Financial records contained in backups, replicas and disaster recovery environments remain subject to applicable protection and retention requirements.

Backup restoration must not silently reintroduce records that were lawfully deleted or anonymised.

Where deletion cannot immediately propagate through immutable backup media, the restoration process must apply the approved deletion and suppression controls before restored data becomes operationally accessible.

## 22. Exports and Downloads

Financial exports must be authorised and traceable.

Controls should include:

- Defined export purpose.
- Store and platform scope.
- Permission checks.
- Data minimisation.
- Secure delivery.
- Expiry or access restrictions where supported.
- Export activity logging.

Users must not obtain unrestricted cross-store financial exports through ordinary store permissions.

## 23. External Providers and Accounting Systems

Financial records held by payment providers, banks and accounting systems must be considered in the overall records lifecycle.

The platform must identify:

- Relevant provider.
- Data categories.
- Record ownership and responsibility.
- Synchronisation method.
- Retention arrangements.
- Export and retrieval options.
- Deletion capabilities.
- Contractual limitations.

Provider records must not be assumed to disappear when an internal platform record is deleted.

## 24. Migration and Record Continuity

Financial records must retain their identity and relationships during migration.

Migration controls should verify:

- Record counts.
- Transaction references.
- Amounts and currencies.
- Store attribution.
- Payment and settlement links.
- Refund and reversal links.
- Journal and ledger relationships.
- Supporting evidence.
- Historical timestamps.

Migration must not convert unresolved discrepancies into apparently reconciled balances.

## 25. Monitoring and Compliance

Record management controls should be reviewed for:

- Unauthorised access.
- Unauthorised modification.
- Missing evidence.
- Incomplete attribution.
- Unapproved disposal.
- Retention schedule exceptions.
- Export activity.
- Legal holds.
- Backup restoration behaviour.
- Provider retention gaps.

Material failures must be escalated through the appropriate incident and governance processes.

## 26. Responsibilities

### Platform Owner

Oversees platform-level financial record governance and ensures that ownership, access and retention responsibilities are defined.

### Store Admin

Ensures store financial records and supporting evidence are handled according to approved store procedures.

### Technical Administrators

Maintain technical safeguards, backups, access controls and record retrieval capabilities.

### Financial Reviewers

Verify the completeness and integrity of financial evidence during reviews and reconciliation.

### External Professionals

Advise on applicable accounting, tax, legal and retention requirements within their engagement.

## 27. Exceptions

Exceptions to this policy must:

- Identify the affected records.
- Explain the business or legal reason.
- Assess the risk.
- Identify the responsible owner.
- Define compensating controls.
- Receive approval from the appropriate authority.
- Have an expiry or review condition where practical.

An exception must not authorise unlawful disposal or disclosure.
