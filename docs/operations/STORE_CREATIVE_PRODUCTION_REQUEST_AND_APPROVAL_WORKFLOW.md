## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the operational workflow for requesting, planning, producing, reviewing and delivering creative assets for participating stores.

It covers the central creative production team's work, including:
- Product photography.
- Product videos.
- Brand photography.
- Promotional photography.
- Storefront banners and visual assets.
- Campaign graphics.
- Short-form marketing videos.
- Other approved visual content.

The workflow ensures that every store receives technically suitable, accurately represented and properly approved creative assets while preserving the Store Owner's brand direction and rights.

## 2. Operating Principles

Creative production must follow these principles:

1. The parent company provides central production capability and technical standards.
2. Store Owners contribute product knowledge and creative direction.
3. Each asset must be associated with the correct store and intended use.
4. Technical specifications must come from actual platform requirements.
5. Product representation must be accurate.
6. Creative work must have an agreed scope before production.
7. Store Owners must have a defined review and approval opportunity.
8. Ownership and usage rights must be established before publication.
9. Production costs must be transparent.
10. Original files and approved derivatives must be managed responsibly.

## 3. Roles and Responsibilities

### 3.1 Store Owner

The Store Owner is responsible for:
- Requesting creative work.
- Identifying the products or subjects.
- Providing accurate product information.
- Explaining brand direction and intended audience.
- Supplying relevant references and existing assets.
- Making products available for production.
- Confirming product and brand accuracy.
- Reviewing creative outputs.
- Approving or requesting revisions within the agreed scope.

The Store Owner does not need to determine technical image-processing specifications or platform media requirements.

### 3.2 Central Creative Production Team

The production team is responsible for:
- Reviewing creative requests.
- Planning shoots.
- Determining technical production requirements.
- Photography and videography.
- Editing and post-production.
- Preparing platform-ready assets.
- Quality assurance.
- Asset organisation and delivery.
- Recording production costs and deliverables.

### 3.3 Marketing Team

The marketing team is responsible for:
- Identifying campaign creative requirements.
- Defining campaign objectives.
- Coordinating creative requests linked to campaigns.
- Confirming intended publication channels.
- Ensuring assets match approved campaign messaging.

### 3.4 Parent Company Management

Management is responsible for:
- Approving production budgets where required.
- Defining included production allowances.
- Approving exceptional production work.
- Resolving material scope or rights disputes.
- Ensuring production resources remain sustainable.

## 4. Creative Request Entry Point

Where supported, Store Owners should submit requests through their dashboard.

An alternative approved support channel must be available where dashboard access is unavailable.

Each request should include:

- Store identifier.
- Store Owner identifier.
- Request title.
- Creative category.
- Intended purpose.
- Products or subjects involved.
- Desired deliverables.
- Intended publication channels.
- Preferred completion date.
- Brand direction.
- Target audience.
- Reference images or videos.
- Existing brand assets.
- Product availability.
- Special requirements.
- Requested usage rights, where relevant.

A request must not be considered approved merely because it has been submitted.

## 6. Initial Assessment

The production team must assess each request before scheduling work.

Assessment should establish:

- Whether the request is sufficiently clear.
- Whether the requested deliverables are technically necessary.
- Whether suitable existing assets already exist.
- Whether the products or subjects are available.
- Whether the requested date is achievable.
- Whether specialist equipment or locations are required.
- Whether external suppliers are needed.
- Whether the request falls within an included allowance.
- Whether additional charges apply.
- Whether the proposed use is covered by existing rights.

The team may request clarification before preparing a production plan.

## 7. Production Scope and Quotation

Before production begins, the Store Owner must receive a clear scope.

The scope should identify:

- Production objective.
- Products or subjects.
- Number and type of deliverables.
- Photography or video requirements.
- Production location.
- Editing and post-production work.
- Expected output formats.
- Revision allowance.
- Estimated delivery date.
- Included and excluded work.
- Applicable costs.
- Payment requirements.
- Ownership and usage rights.

The scope must distinguish between:

1. Work included in the subscription or onboarding allowance.
2. Additional paid creative production.
3. Campaign-specific production.
4. External supplier or location costs.
5. Optional marketing services.

No unlimited production commitment should be implied by a subscription.

Additional work must be approved and priced before it begins.

## 8. Production Planning

Once the scope is approved, the production team should prepare a production plan.

The plan may include:

- Shoot date and time.
- Location.
- Product preparation.
- Equipment requirements.
- Lighting and background.
- Shot list.
- Video sequence.
- Styling requirements.
- Props.
- Models or participants.
- Required permissions.
- Production responsibilities.
- Delivery schedule.

The Store Owner must be informed of any preparation required.

Where a shoot involves multiple stores, each store's products, creative direction and deliverables must remain separately identifiable.

## 9. Product Preparation and Accuracy

Before production, the Store Owner must provide accurate information about the products.

Where applicable, this includes:
- Product name.
- Product variant.
- Size or volume.
- Colour.
- Material.
- Packaging.
- Ingredients or composition.
- Relevant product features.
- Correct labels.
- Required warnings.
- Distinguishing characteristics.

The production team must verify that the correct product and variant are being photographed or filmed.

For products where colour accuracy is important, lighting and post-production must not materially misrepresent the actual product.

The team must not digitally add features or alter product characteristics in a way that misleads customers.

## 11. Technical Production Standards

The production team must prepare assets according to the platform's actual requirements.

Technical considerations include:

- Image dimensions.
- Aspect ratios.
- Responsive variants.
- File formats.
- Compression.
- Resolution.
- Colour accuracy.
- Background treatment.
- Cropping and focal positioning.
- Video dimensions.
- Video encoding.
- File-size limits.
- Loading performance.
- Accessibility.
- Alt text and captions where appropriate.

The team must inspect the actual storefront components before selecting output specifications.

A product image may require different derivatives for:
- Product cards.
- Product detail pages.
- Mobile layouts.
- Desktop layouts.
- Category listings.
- Promotional banners.

One image size or aspect ratio must not be assumed suitable for every component.

## 12. Original Files and Derivatives

Where practical, the production team must preserve:

- Original photographs.
- Original video footage.
- Edited master files.
- Approved final exports.
- Optimised storefront derivatives.
- Relevant project files where included in the agreed scope.

Originals should remain separate from compressed web-ready versions.

The team must maintain traceability between:
- Original asset.
- Edited master.
- Approved output.
- Storefront derivative.
- Published location.

The applicable agreement must determine which source and project files are included in the Store Owner's deliverables.

## 13. Production Quality Assurance

Before review by the Store Owner, the production team must verify:

- Correct store.
- Correct product.
- Correct product variant.
- Accurate representation.
- Suitable composition.
- Appropriate lighting.
- Acceptable sharpness.
- Colour consistency.
- Clean editing.
- No unintended visual defects.
- Correct dimensions.
- Correct format.
- Optimised file size.
- Appropriate responsive derivatives.
- Correct file naming and storage.
- Required rights and permissions.

Assets that fail quality checks must be corrected before delivery.

## 14. Internal Review

Where a marketing or creative lead is assigned, the work should receive an internal review before Store Owner approval.

The review should confirm:
- Alignment with the agreed creative direction.
- Technical suitability.
- Brand consistency.
- Product accuracy.
- Intended usage.
- Compliance with campaign or storefront requirements.

Internal approval does not replace the Store Owner's approval of material brand or product presentation.

## 15. Store Owner Review

The Store Owner must receive a reviewable preview of the completed work.

The review should allow the Store Owner to:
- Approve the deliverables.
- Identify factual inaccuracies.
- Request corrections.
- Identify deviations from the approved brief.
- Raise concerns about brand presentation.

Feedback should be consolidated into one review submission where practical.

The agreed scope must define the number of included revision rounds.

Corrections required because the production team failed to follow the approved brief or introduced technical defects should not automatically consume the Store Owner's optional revision allowance.

Requests that materially change the original brief may require a revised scope and quotation.

## 16. Approval and Publication

Assets must not be published until the required approvals are recorded.

Approval must identify:
- Store.
- Asset or deliverable.
- Version.
- Approving Store Owner.
- Approval date.
- Approved usage.
- Any restrictions.

Publication must use the correct approved asset and intended destination.

If an approved asset is materially changed after approval, renewed approval may be required.

## 17. Rights and Permissions

Before publication, the production team must establish the relevant rights.

The production record should identify:
- Asset creator.
- Source of the asset.
- Copyright or other ownership status.
- Commissioning agreement.
- Storefront usage permission.
- Campaign usage permission.
- External platform usage permission.
- Model or participant releases.
- Music and third-party licences.
- Source-file delivery rights.
- Portfolio or promotional usage permissions.

The Store Owner's brand and underlying business rights remain governed by the applicable Store Owner Agreement.

Commissioning or paying for creative work does not automatically settle copyright ownership.

The parent company must not assume that it owns all creative outputs simply because its production team created them.

Rights must be documented in the applicable agreement or asset record.

## 18. Delivery and Asset Handover

After approval, the production team must deliver the agreed assets.

The handover should include:
- Approved files.
- Asset identifiers.
- Store association.
- Product association, where applicable.
- Intended use.
- File formats.
- Relevant dimensions.
- Usage restrictions.
- Approval record.
- Any included source files.

The team must ensure that the files are available to the authorised Store Owner and relevant platform functions.

Assets must not be placed in another store's asset library without an approved shared-use arrangement.

## 19. Production Statuses

Recommended workflow statuses:

- Draft: Request is being prepared.
- Submitted: Request has been submitted.
- Under Review: Production team is assessing it.
- More Information Required: Clarification is needed.
- Awaiting Scope Approval: Deliverables and costs require approval.
- Approved: Production scope has been accepted.
- Awaiting Payment: Required payment is outstanding.
- Scheduled: Production has been scheduled.
- In Production: Photography or video work is underway.
- In Post-Production: Editing and processing are underway.
- Internal Review: Team quality review is underway.
- Awaiting Store Approval: Store Owner must review the work.
- Revision Requested: Approved-scope changes are being addressed.
- Ready for Delivery: Final outputs have passed review.
- Delivered: Assets have been handed over.
- Published: Assets are in approved use.
- Cancelled: Request has been cancelled.
- Archived: Record is retained for reference.

The system must record status changes and relevant timestamps.

## 20. Delays and Rescheduling

Production delays may arise from:
- Product unavailability.
- Store Owner scheduling changes.
- Equipment failure.
- Location issues.
- Weather.
- External supplier delays.
- Additional scope.
- Technical defects.
- Required permissions not being available.

The production team must notify the Store Owner when a material delay becomes known.

The notification should explain:
- What has changed.
- Why it has changed.
- The effect on delivery.
- The revised expected date.
- Any action required from the Store Owner.

Revised dates must be realistic.

## 21. Cancellation and Refunds

Cancellation must follow the agreed production scope and commercial terms.

The production record should identify:
- Who requested cancellation.
- Reason.
- Work already completed.
- Costs already incurred.
- Deliverables already produced.
- Applicable cancellation charges.
- Refund or credit treatment.

The parent company must not charge for work that was not agreed or incurred without an applicable contractual basis.

## 22. Asset Storage and Retention

Creative assets must be stored in an organised and access-controlled structure.

Recommended asset metadata:

- Asset identifier.
- Store identifier.
- Product identifier, where applicable.
- Campaign identifier, where applicable.
- Creator.
- Creation date.
- Version.
- File type.
- Dimensions.
- File size.
- Usage rights.
- Approval status.
- Publication status.
- Retention classification.

Originals, working files and published derivatives must be distinguishable.

Retention and deletion must follow applicable contractual, privacy and operational requirements.

## 23. Production Performance

The parent company should monitor:

- Requests received.
- Production turnaround time.
- Cost per production.
- Deliverables completed.
- Revision frequency.
- Quality defects.
- Store Owner approval time.
- Asset reuse.
- Production capacity.
- Store Owner satisfaction.

Performance data should help improve production efficiency without compromising quality or rights.
