## 1. Purpose

This procedure defines how a participating store may be suspended, how the suspension is managed, and how the store may be restored to active operation.

It establishes safeguards for customers, financial records, inventory, store data, integrations and other participating stores.

## 2. Scope

This procedure covers:

- Voluntary suspension requests.
- Administrative suspension.
- Emergency protective suspension.
- Security-related suspension.
- Financial or operational suspension.
- Suspension reviews.
- Customer and order handling.
- Financial and inventory controls.
- Access and integration restrictions.
- Reactivation assessment.
- Reactivation approval.
- Post-reactivation monitoring.

## 3. Suspension Principles

Suspension must:

- Have a documented reason.
- Be authorised by the appropriate authority.
- Be proportionate to the identified risk or operational need.
- Affect only the necessary store functions.
- Preserve historical records.
- Protect customer commitments.
- Maintain visibility of outstanding obligations.
- Avoid unnecessary disruption to other stores.
- Be reviewed within an appropriate timeframe.
- Include clear conditions for reactivation where applicable.

Suspension is not equivalent to deletion or permanent termination.

## 4. Suspension Types

### 4.1 Voluntary Suspension

A store requests a temporary pause in operations.

The request must be submitted by an authorised store representative and reviewed by the appropriate platform authority.

### 4.2 Administrative Suspension

The platform restricts some or all store operations following an authorised governance or operational decision.

The reason, scope and authority must be recorded.

### 4.3 Emergency Protective Suspension

Immediate restrictions may be applied to contain a credible threat involving security, customer safety, financial integrity, data exposure or platform stability.

Emergency protective action must not be delayed solely because ordinary approval cannot be obtained immediately.

The action must be documented and promptly reviewed by the appropriate authority.

### 4.4 Security Suspension

Access or integrations may be restricted following suspected account compromise, credential exposure, unauthorised activity or another security incident.

### 4.5 Financial Suspension

Financial operations or selected commercial functions may be restricted where an authorised decision identifies a material financial risk or unresolved obligation.

A financial dispute alone must not automatically justify unrelated restrictions without an appropriate assessment.

### 4.6 Operational Suspension

A store may be temporarily restricted because it cannot safely or reliably fulfil its operational responsibilities.

## 5. Suspension Authority

Suspension authority must follow the platform governance framework and applicable store agreements.

The person initiating an emergency protective suspension must have explicitly delegated authority for that action.

Ordinary Store Admin permissions must not automatically include authority to suspend the entire store from the platform.

The system must record the initiating actor and the authority under which the action was taken.

## 6. Suspension Request and Assessment

A suspension request or proposed action should record:

- Store identity.
- Requesting or initiating party.
- Reason.
- Suspension type.
- Affected functions.
- Relevant evidence.
- Customer impact.
- Financial impact.
- Security implications.
- Proposed start time.
- Review date.
- Conditions for lifting restrictions.

Where immediate protective action is required, the assessment may be completed after initial containment, provided the action is reviewed promptly.

## 7. Suspension Scope

Suspension may apply to selected capabilities or the entire store.

Possible restrictions include:

- New customer purchases.
- Checkout.
- Product publication.
- Inventory changes.
- Store employee access.
- Store Admin access.
- API access.
- Storefront access.
- Marketing campaigns.
- Payment operations.
- Selected integrations.

The restriction must be no broader than necessary, unless a broader restriction is justified by the circumstances.

## 8. Customer and Storefront Protection

The platform must determine how customers will be affected.

Possible actions include:

- Disable new purchases.
- Display a clear storefront notice.
- Remove unavailable products from active purchasing.
- Preserve access to order information where appropriate.
- Provide an authorised customer support route.
- Prevent misleading availability or delivery claims.

A suspended store must not continue accepting orders that it cannot fulfil.

## 9. Existing Orders

Suspension must not silently cancel or abandon existing orders.

Review:

- Orders awaiting payment.
- Paid orders awaiting fulfilment.
- Partially fulfilled orders.
- Pending deliveries.
- Open returns.
- Refund requests.
- Customer complaints.

Each affected order must receive an explicit disposition and responsible owner.

## 10. Payments and Financial Operations

Review the effect of suspension on:

- New payment requests.
- Pending provider transactions.
- Captured payments.
- Refunds.
- Chargebacks.
- Provider settlements.
- Outstanding fees.
- Store obligations.

Protect financial records and preserve transaction history.

Restrictions on new financial activity must not prevent necessary reconciliation or authorised resolution of existing transactions.

## 11. Inventory Protection

Review stock reservations, movements and outstanding transfers.

Where necessary:

- Prevent unauthorised stock changes.
- Preserve existing reservations.
- Review stock supplied under assistance arrangements.
- Document stock held by third parties.
- Restrict transfers pending ownership verification.

Suspension must not automatically transfer inventory ownership.

## 12. Product Catalogue Protection

The platform must distinguish between:

- Suspending a store's product listings.
- Restricting changes to source product information.
- Removing products from customer-facing availability.
- Suspending a shared source product.

A store suspension must not unintentionally disable products legitimately available to other stores.

Product ownership and catalogue authority remain unchanged unless separately approved.

## 13. Access Restrictions

Review store-related access according to the suspension scope.

Actions may include:

- Restricting employee access.
- Restricting Store Admin actions.
- Revoking temporary support access.
- Suspending affected API credentials.
- Restricting developer access.
- Revoking compromised sessions.
- Preserving required audit access.

Platform-level access must not be removed merely because one store is suspended, unless the relevant account itself presents a security risk.

## 14. Integration Restrictions

Review integrations connected to the suspended store.

Actions may include:

- Disabling store-specific API credentials.
- Suspending webhook processing.
- Pausing scheduled jobs.
- Disabling marketing integrations.
- Restricting payment operations.
- Preserving provider event records.
- Maintaining safe processing of pending transactions.

Shared integrations must remain operational for unaffected stores.

## 15. Security-Related Suspension

For security-related suspension:

1. Identify the suspected compromise or exposure.
2. Restrict affected access and credentials.
3. Preserve relevant evidence.
4. Review active sessions and tokens.
5. Assess possible cross-store exposure.
6. Notify the appropriate incident authority.
7. Follow the incident response process.
8. Define remediation and reactivation conditions.

Suspension must not replace investigation or incident management.

## 16. Financial or Governance Disputes

Where suspension relates to a financial or governance dispute:

- Record the disputed issue.
- Identify the relevant agreement or policy.
- Preserve supporting evidence.
- Identify the decision authority.
- Record the restriction imposed.
- Provide an appropriate review route.
- Avoid prejudging unresolved allegations.

Restrictions must be proportionate and consistent with applicable agreements.

## 17. Suspension Communication

Communication should identify:

- That the store is suspended.
- Effective date and time.
- General reason where disclosure is appropriate.
- Affected capabilities.
- Impact on existing orders.
- Required actions.
- Contact or escalation route.
- Review arrangements.

Sensitive security or investigation details must only be disclosed to authorised recipients.

## 18. Suspension Records

The suspension record must contain:

- Store identity.
- Suspension reference.
- Suspension type.
- Initiator.
- Authorising authority.
- Reason.
- Evidence references.
- Scope.
- Start date and time.
- Customer and operational impact.
- Financial impact.
- Access and integration changes.
- Review date.
- Reactivation conditions.
- Final outcome.

All material changes must be auditable.

## 19. Suspension Monitoring

During suspension, monitor:

- Attempts to access restricted functions.
- Unexpected API activity.
- Provider callbacks.
- Pending payments and settlements.
- Open orders.
- Customer complaints.
- Financial obligations.
- Inventory exceptions.
- Security incidents.
- Outstanding remediation.

Suspension must not cause pending obligations to become invisible.

## 20. Suspension Review

Suspensions must be reviewed according to their nature, risk and applicable agreements.

The review should determine:

- Whether the original reason remains valid.
- Whether restrictions remain necessary.
- Whether restrictions can be narrowed.
- Whether additional action is required.
- Whether the store may reactivate.
- Whether permanent exit procedures should begin.

A suspension must not remain indefinitely without an identifiable owner and review process.

## 21. Reactivation Request

A store seeking reactivation should submit a request through an authorised representative.

The request should identify:

- Store identity.
- Suspension reference.
- Requesting representative.
- Actions completed.
- Supporting evidence.
- Outstanding issues.
- Requested reactivation date.

The platform must verify the representative's authority.

## 22. Reactivation Assessment

Before reactivation, review:

- Original suspension reason.
- Completion of required remediation.
- Store Admin account security.
- Employee access and permissions.
- API credentials.
- Storefront configuration.
- Product availability.
- Inventory accuracy.
- Open orders.
- Payment and settlement status.
- Financial obligations.
- Provider integrations.
- Customer support readiness.
- Outstanding incidents.
- Required approvals.

Security-related suspensions require verification that the identified weakness has been addressed.

## 23. Reactivation Approval

Reactivation must be approved by the authority responsible for the suspension or another properly delegated authority.

Approval must identify:

- Approved scope.
- Effective date.
- Conditions.
- Remaining restrictions.
- Required monitoring.
- Outstanding actions.

A store must not automatically reactivate merely because a temporary restriction expires.

## 24. Reactivation Execution

Reactivation must follow a controlled sequence:

1. Confirm approval.
2. Verify store and administrator identities.
3. Restore approved access.
4. Reissue or rotate credentials where necessary.
5. Restore eligible integrations.
6. Verify catalogue and inventory availability.
7. Verify checkout and payment processing.
8. Review outstanding orders.
9. Confirm customer communication.
10. Record successful restoration.

Only approved capabilities should be restored.

## 25. Post-Reactivation Monitoring

Monitor the store after reactivation for:

- Authentication failures.
- Unusual API activity.
- Payment failures.
- Duplicate transactions.
- Inventory inconsistencies.
- Order processing errors.
- Provider integration failures.
- Customer complaints.
- Unresolved financial exceptions.

Monitoring intensity should reflect the reason for suspension and the remaining risk.

## 26. Failed Reactivation

If reactivation checks fail:

- Stop the affected restoration step.
- Preserve the current safe state.
- Record the failure.
- Identify the cause.
- Notify the responsible authority.
- Define corrective action.
- Repeat only the necessary verification.

Do not repeatedly restore and disable functions without understanding the failure.

## 27. Suspension-to-Exit Transition

Where a store will not return to active operation, the platform may initiate the store exit procedure.

The transition must preserve:

- Existing suspension records.
- Exit authority.
- Financial obligations.
- Customer commitments.
- Product ownership.
- Inventory records.
- Data retention requirements.
- Historical audit records.

Suspension must not be used to bypass the formal exit process.

## 28. Open Decisions

The following require formal definition:

- Suspension approval authority.
- Emergency protective suspension delegation.
- Suspension review intervals.
- Permitted suspension durations.
- Customer communication requirements.
- Financial restriction thresholds.
- Reactivation approval authority.
- Security remediation acceptance criteria.
- Post-reactivation monitoring periods.
- Suspension appeal and dispute arrangements.
