# Parent Company Performance Management and KPI Framework

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes a framework for measuring the performance of the parent company, its operational teams and the services supporting participating stores.

The objective is to ensure that business goals translate into measurable outcomes, responsibilities are clear and management decisions are based on reliable information.

Performance management must support sustainable growth, service quality, financial discipline and the success of participating Store Owners.

## 2. Scope

This framework applies to:

- Parent company leadership.
- Business operations.
- Technology and platform teams.
- Finance and administration.
- Customer support.
- Store Owner support.
- Marketing and creative production.
- Supplier and partner management.
- Employees and contractors with defined performance responsibilities.

Store Owner business performance is governed separately by the Store Owner Business Performance Reporting and Review Standards.

## 3. Performance management principles

The parent company must follow these principles:

1. Measurability: objectives must have defined outcomes and measurement methods.
2. Accountability: every objective must have an accountable owner.
3. Transparency: performance results must be understandable and supported by evidence.
4. Relevance: metrics must measure outcomes that matter to the business.
5. Fairness: teams must be assessed against factors they can reasonably influence.
6. Balance: performance must consider quality, cost, speed, reliability and customer impact.
7. Continuous improvement: underperformance should lead to investigation and improvement actions.
8. Integrity: metrics must never be manipulated to create an appearance of success.

## 4. Strategic business objectives

The parent company should establish measurable objectives across these areas:

### 4.1 Platform growth

- Increase the number of active participating stores.
- Support sustainable Store Owner growth.
- Improve store retention.
- Expand platform capabilities responsibly.
- Strengthen customer reach across participating stores.

### 4.2 Store Owner success

- Reduce onboarding friction.
- Improve Store Owner support.
- Increase access to useful business information.
- Improve store launch readiness.
- Help Store Owners identify growth opportunities.

### 4.3 Customer experience

- Improve customer satisfaction.
- Reduce unresolved complaints.
- Improve order processing and fulfilment visibility.
- Improve customer support response and resolution.
- Maintain clear and accurate storefront information.

### 4.4 Platform reliability

- Maintain service availability.
- Reduce avoidable incidents.
- Improve recovery times.
- Protect customer and business information.
- Maintain reliable integrations and payment workflows.

### 4.5 Financial sustainability

- Maintain positive operating discipline.
- Understand cost per active store.
- Improve predictable recurring revenue.
- Control infrastructure and service costs.
- Maintain accurate financial reconciliation.

### 4.6 Marketing and brand development

- Improve qualified customer reach.
- Increase campaign effectiveness.
- Maintain consistent creative quality.
- Improve campaign measurement.
- Preserve individual Store Owner brand identities.

## 5. Key performance indicators

Each department should maintain a documented set of relevant KPIs.

A KPI must include:

- Name.
- Business purpose.
- Definition.
- Calculation method.
- Data source.
- Reporting frequency.
- Responsible owner.
- Target or acceptable range.
- Known limitations.
- Escalation threshold.

Targets must be established using actual business capacity and historical evidence rather than arbitrary numbers.

## 6. Core company KPIs

The parent company should consider the following indicators.

### 6.1 Store growth and retention

- Total participating stores.
- Newly onboarded stores.
- Active stores.
- Store launch completion rate.
- Store retention rate.
- Store Owner churn.
- Average onboarding completion time.

A store must have a documented definition of active status.

An account that exists but has no meaningful operational activity should not automatically count as an active store.

### 6.2 Store Owner support

- Support requests received.
- First response time.
- Average resolution time.
- Open support backlog.
- Reopened support requests.
- Escalation rate.
- Store Owner satisfaction.
- Percentage of requests resolved within published service targets.

Response time and resolution time must be measured separately.

A request must not be marked resolved merely to improve a KPI if the underlying issue remains unresolved.

### 6.3 Platform reliability

- Service availability.
- Number of production incidents.
- Mean time to detect.
- Mean time to acknowledge.
- Mean time to recover.
- Failed deployment rate.
- Integration failure rate.
- Backup verification results.
- Critical security findings.
- Recovery test results.

Availability calculations must document planned maintenance and exclusions.

### 6.4 Financial performance

- Monthly recurring revenue.
- Revenue by service category.
- Operating expenditure.
- Gross margin where applicable.
- Cost per active store.
- Customer acquisition expenditure.
- Store Owner acquisition cost.
- Payment reconciliation completion.
- Outstanding settlement discrepancies.
- Cash flow position.

Platform revenue must be distinguished from the sales generated by Store Owners.

### 6.5 Marketing performance

- Campaigns delivered.
- Campaign delivery time.
- Campaign reach.
- Qualified traffic.
- Lead generation.
- Campaign conversion where measurable.
- Cost per acquisition.
- Creative production turnaround.
- Campaign approval time.
- Marketing service satisfaction.

Reach and engagement must not be treated as direct proof of revenue generation.

### 6.6 Customer experience

- Customer complaints.
- Complaint acknowledgement time.
- Complaint resolution time.
- Refund processing time.
- Order fulfilment delays.
- Customer satisfaction.
- Repeat purchase activity where measurable.

Customer experience metrics should be interpreted alongside the responsibilities of the individual Store Owner and the parent company.

## 8. Objectives and key results

The parent company may use an Objectives and Key Results approach.

Each objective should describe a meaningful business outcome.

Each key result must describe measurable evidence that the objective has been achieved.

Example:

Objective: Improve the Store Owner onboarding experience.

Possible key results:

- Reduce incomplete onboarding cases.
- Improve the percentage of stores launched with all required checks completed.
- Reduce avoidable onboarding delays.
- Improve Store Owner onboarding feedback.

Targets must be established after reviewing the baseline.

Objectives should be limited to a manageable number of priorities per review period.

## 9. Performance review cycle

The parent company should use the following review structure:

### Weekly operational review

Focus on:

- Immediate service issues.
- Operational backlogs.
- Critical incidents.
- Overdue actions.
- Near-term delivery commitments.

### Monthly performance review

Focus on:

- KPI results.
- Financial performance.
- Store Owner support.
- Platform reliability.
- Marketing delivery.
- Department-level improvement actions.

### Quarterly business review

Focus on:

- Strategic objectives.
- Growth and retention.
- Financial sustainability.
- Risk exposure.
- Resource requirements.
- Business model performance.

### Annual review

Focus on:

- Overall company performance.
- Strategic progress.
- Commercial model effectiveness.
- Department effectiveness.
- Major operational improvements.
- Objectives for the next year.

## 10. Performance targets

Targets must be realistic, documented and reviewed when business conditions change.

A target should not be introduced without understanding:

- Current performance.
- Available resources.
- Workload.
- Technical dependencies.
- Market conditions.
- Factors outside the responsible team's control.

Where no reliable baseline exists, the first reporting period should establish the baseline.

The parent company must not treat an illustrative target as an established commitment.

## 11. KPI integrity and measurement controls

The company must protect the integrity of its performance data.

Prohibited practices include:

- Manipulating figures.
- Excluding valid failures without justification.
- Marking incomplete work as completed.
- Creating artificial activity.
- Hiding unresolved complaints.
- Changing metric definitions to improve reported results.
- Counting the same outcome more than once.
- Presenting estimates as verified results.

Changes to KPI definitions must be documented.

Where a metric is corrected, the reason and impact must be traceable.

## 12. Performance dashboards and reporting

Management should have access to a consolidated performance dashboard.

The dashboard should show:

- Current reporting period.
- KPI results.
- Targets.
- Previous-period comparisons.
- Trends.
- Data completeness.
- Areas requiring attention.
- Assigned improvement actions.

Reports must distinguish:

- Actual results.
- Forecasts.
- Estimates.
- Provisional results.
- Final verified results.

Access must follow role-based permissions.

Confidential employee, financial and Store Owner information must not be exposed to unauthorised users.

## 13. Underperformance and corrective action

When a KPI falls below its acceptable threshold, the responsible team should investigate.

The review should determine:

1. Whether the result is accurate.
2. Whether the target remains realistic.
3. What caused the deviation.
4. Whether the issue is within the team's control.
5. Whether existing processes or resources are inadequate.
6. What corrective action is required.

An improvement action should identify:

- The issue.
- Root cause where known.
- Corrective action.
- Responsible person.
- Expected completion date.
- Success measure.
- Review date.

Underperformance must not automatically result in disciplinary action.

Where an employee performance issue is involved, the applicable employment policies and labour law must be followed.

## 14. Recognition and improvement

Strong performance should be recognised where it reflects meaningful business outcomes.

Recognition may include:

- Formal acknowledgement.
- Professional development opportunities.
- Increased responsibility.
- Performance-based incentives where approved.
- Team recognition.

Incentives must not encourage manipulation, unsafe shortcuts or conduct that harms customers or Store Owners.

## 15. Resource and capacity planning

Performance reviews must consider whether teams have sufficient resources to meet their responsibilities.

Management should evaluate:

- Staffing capacity.
- Technical capacity.
- Infrastructure costs.
- Supplier dependencies.
- Training requirements.
- Operational workload.
- Competing priorities.

Targets must be adjusted where approved business changes materially affect available resources or responsibilities.

## 16. Performance management and Store Owner support

The parent company's internal KPIs should reflect whether its services genuinely support participating stores.

Relevant measures may include:

- Time taken to launch a store.
- Time taken to resolve Store Owner issues.
- Quality of central creative production.
- Campaign delivery reliability.
- Accuracy of financial reporting.
- Store Owner satisfaction.
- Availability of useful business guidance.

The parent company must not measure its success only through its own revenue or store count while ignoring the quality of service provided to Store Owners.

## 17. Implementation requirements

The parent company should establish:

- A company-level KPI dictionary.
- Department-level objectives.
- KPI owners.
- Data sources.
- Reporting schedules.
- Performance dashboards.
- Review meeting procedures.
- Improvement action tracking.
- Management approval controls.
- Historical performance records.

KPIs should be generated from authoritative operational and financial records wherever possible.

## 18. Testing and assurance

Performance reporting should be checked for:

- Calculation accuracy.
- Data completeness.
- Duplicate records.
- Consistent metric definitions.
- Correct reporting periods.
- Permission enforcement.
- Historical comparison accuracy.
- Traceability of corrections.

Management should periodically verify that reported KPIs reflect actual operational performance.

## 19. Review and maintenance

This framework should be reviewed annually and whenever material changes affect:

- Company strategy.
- Commercial arrangements.
- Department responsibilities.
- Platform capabilities.
- Reporting infrastructure.
- Financial operations.
- Store Owner services.

Changes to core KPIs must be documented and communicated to affected teams.

## 20. Governing principle

The parent company must measure what matters, understand why results change and take responsibility for improving its services.

Performance management must create accountability without encouraging manipulation or sacrificing customer trust, Store Owner success, service quality or long-term business sustainability.
