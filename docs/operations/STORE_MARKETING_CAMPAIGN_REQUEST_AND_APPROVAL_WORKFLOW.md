## 1. Purpose

This document defines the operational workflow for requesting, planning, approving, executing and evaluating marketing campaigns for participating stores.

It translates the Central Marketing Agency and Shared Growth Strategy into a practical process.

The workflow ensures that:
- Store Owners can request marketing support.
- The parent company's marketing team can plan shared campaigns.
- Each store retains its own brand identity.
- Campaigns are approved before publication.
- Advertising budgets and service fees remain transparent.
- Creative assets are properly authorised.
- Campaign results are attributed and reported accurately.

## 2. Scope

This workflow applies to:

- Individual store campaigns.
- Multi-store campaigns.
- Parent-company promotional campaigns featuring participating stores.
- Seasonal and category campaigns.
- Product launches.
- Promotional offers.
- Social media campaigns.
- Paid advertising campaigns.
- Email and messaging campaigns.
- Campaigns using centrally produced photography or video.

It covers marketing campaign operations, not the separate technical support or product photography production workflows.

## 3. Campaign Types

### 3.1 Store-Requested Campaign

A Store Owner requests promotion for their own store, products or services.

Examples:
- New product launch.
- Seasonal promotion.
- Brand awareness campaign.
- Product collection promotion.
- Customer acquisition campaign.

### 3.2 Parent-Initiated Campaign

The parent company identifies a marketing opportunity and proposes a campaign featuring one or more stores.

Examples:
- Introducing a new participating store.
- Promoting a category of products.
- Highlighting local entrepreneurs.
- Seasonal shopping campaigns.
- Multi-store promotional events.

### 3.3 Shared Category Campaign

The parent company coordinates a campaign involving multiple stores operating in a related category.

Each participating store must be identifiable as its own brand.

Campaign participation, costs, deliverables and promotional terms must be agreed in advance.

### 3.4 Parent Company Campaign

The parent company promotes its own platform, services, infrastructure or business-support offering.

Participating stores may be featured as examples or case studies only with appropriate consent and accurate attribution.

A parent-company campaign must not imply that a Store Owner's independent brand is owned by the parent company.

## 4. Campaign Request Entry Point

Where supported, Store Owners should submit campaign requests through their dashboard.

The request process should also be available through an approved alternative channel where dashboard access is unavailable.

A campaign request should capture:

- Store identifier.
- Store Owner identifier.
- Campaign title.
- Campaign objective.
- Products or services to promote.
- Target audience.
- Preferred channels.
- Proposed campaign dates.
- Proposed offer or promotional terms.
- Available creative assets.
- Requested creative production.
- Proposed advertising budget, if applicable.
- Requested marketing services.
- Supporting information.
- Relevant deadlines.

The request must distinguish between a request for basic platform promotion and a request for separately charged marketing services.

Submitting a request does not guarantee approval or implementation.

## 5. Campaign Objectives

Every campaign must have a clearly defined objective.

Possible objectives include:

- Brand awareness.
- Product discovery.
- Website or storefront traffic.
- Customer acquisition.
- Product sales.
- Repeat purchases.
- New product launch.
- Seasonal demand.
- Customer engagement.
- Lead generation.

The marketing team should select measurements that match the objective.

A campaign intended to generate awareness should not be judged only by immediate sales.

A campaign intended to generate sales should include an appropriate conversion measurement where reliable tracking is available.

## 6. Campaign Assessment

The marketing team must assess each request before accepting it.

Assessment should consider:

- Whether the product or service is suitable for promotion.
- Whether the Store Owner has authority to offer the product.
- Whether pricing and promotional terms are accurate.
- Whether sufficient stock or fulfilment capacity exists.
- Whether the target audience is clearly defined.
- Whether the requested timing is realistic.
- Whether suitable creative assets exist.
- Whether the campaign complies with platform policies.
- Whether the proposed budget is sufficient.
- Whether the request conflicts with another campaign.
- Whether the campaign creates legal, reputational or customer risk.

The team may approve, reject, defer or request clarification.

## 7. Campaign Approval Statuses

Each campaign request must have a traceable status.

Recommended statuses:

- Draft: Request is being prepared.
- Submitted: Store Owner has submitted the request.
- Under Review: Marketing team is assessing it.
- More Information Required: Additional information is needed.
- Approved: Campaign scope has been accepted.
- Awaiting Store Approval: Store Owner must approve the final proposal or creative.
- Awaiting Payment: Required service fees or advertising funds have not been received.
- Scheduled: Campaign is approved and scheduled.
- Active: Campaign is running.
- Paused: Campaign has temporarily stopped.
- Completed: Campaign activity has ended.
- Rejected: Request has not been accepted.
- Cancelled: Campaign has been cancelled.
- Archived: Campaign record is retained for historical reference.

A campaign must not move into publication merely because its request was submitted or reviewed.

## 8. Campaign Proposal

Before execution, the marketing team must prepare a campaign proposal.

The proposal should contain:

- Campaign objective.
- Participating store or stores.
- Products or services featured.
- Target audience.
- Marketing channels.
- Campaign dates.
- Creative deliverables.
- Publication schedule.
- Required Store Owner contributions.
- Service fees.
- Advertising budget.
- Third-party costs.
- Expected measurements.
- Approval requirements.
- Important risks or limitations.

The proposal must clearly separate:

1. Marketing and creative service fees.
2. Advertising spend.
3. Production costs.
4. Third-party platform or distribution charges.
5. Any other agreed campaign expense.

Advertising spend must not be presented as the parent company's service revenue.

## 9. Store Owner Approval

The Store Owner must approve material campaign details before execution.

Approval should cover:

- Brand presentation.
- Product selection.
- Product descriptions.
- Prices.
- Discounts and promotional conditions.
- Campaign messaging.
- Creative assets.
- Publication channels.
- Campaign dates.
- Agreed budget contribution.
- Usage rights for supplied materials.

Where a campaign involves multiple stores, each Store Owner must approve the material relating to their own store.

One Store Owner cannot approve another store's participation or brand representation.

Approval must be recorded through the dashboard or another documented channel.

Silence must not be treated as approval.

## 10. Parent Company Approval

The parent company retains authority to approve campaigns for platform-wide and shared marketing purposes.

It may reject or modify a campaign where necessary to protect:

- Legal compliance.
- Customer interests.
- Platform security.
- Brand integrity.
- Campaign quality.
- Other participating stores.
- Commercial sustainability.

Parent-company approval does not remove the need for Store Owner approval where the campaign uses their brand, products, assets or agreed budget.

## 11. Campaign Budget and Payment

Every campaign must have an agreed financial arrangement before paid execution begins.

The campaign record must distinguish:

- Parent-company marketing service fee.
- Creative production fee.
- Advertising platform budget.
- Third-party charges.
- Store Owner contribution.
- Parent-company contribution.
- Shared campaign allocation.

For shared campaigns, the allocation method must be agreed and recorded.

Possible allocation methods include:

- Equal contribution.
- Contribution based on participating stores.
- Contribution based on agreed campaign exposure.
- Individually funded store placements.
- Parent-company-funded promotional activity.

The allocation method must be transparent.

No Store Owner should be charged for another store's campaign participation without prior agreement.

Paid advertising must not begin until the required funds and approvals are confirmed.

## 12. Creative Production and Asset Management

Campaign creative assets must follow the Central Creative Production and Storefront Visual Standards.

The marketing team must confirm:

- Correct store identity.
- Correct product.
- Accurate product representation.
- Approved logo and brand assets.
- Correct pricing and promotional terms.
- Appropriate image and video formats.
- Required usage rights.
- Required platform dimensions.
- Accessibility requirements where applicable.

The Store Owner must be able to review material creative assets before publication.

Where the parent company creates campaign content, the relevant agreement must define permitted usage, ownership and licensing.

Campaign assets must be stored and attributed to the correct store and campaign.

## 13. Publication Readiness Checklist

Before a campaign becomes active, the responsible team must verify:

- Campaign proposal approved.
- Store Owner approval recorded where required.
- Parent-company approval recorded.
- Products and prices verified.
- Promotional conditions verified.
- Creative assets approved.
- Usage rights confirmed.
- Advertising budget confirmed.
- Campaign dates confirmed.
- Destination links tested.
- Tracking configured where available.
- Fulfilment capacity reviewed.
- Customer-facing terms checked.
- Required disclosures included.

Any unresolved material issue must be addressed before publication.

## 14. Campaign Execution

During execution, the marketing team must:

- Follow the approved campaign plan.
- Publish through approved channels.
- Use the correct store attribution.
- Monitor campaign performance.
- Monitor advertising spend.
- Check links and destination pages.
- Identify material errors promptly.
- Record significant changes.
- Notify the Store Owner of relevant issues.

Material changes to pricing, offers, creative direction, audience, budget or campaign duration require renewed approval where they affect agreed terms.

The team may make minor operational adjustments that do not materially change the approved campaign.

## 15. Campaign Pause and Cancellation

A campaign may be paused or cancelled where:

- A product becomes unavailable.
- Pricing or promotional information is incorrect.
- A legal or compliance concern arises.
- A Store Owner withdraws consent.
- Advertising funds are exhausted.
- A platform or channel becomes unavailable.
- Creative assets are found to be unauthorised.
- The campaign creates unexpected customer or reputational risk.

The campaign record must state:

- Reason for the action.
- Person authorising it.
- Date and time.
- Financial implications.
- Required customer communication.
- Conditions for restarting, where applicable.

Any refund or unused advertising-budget treatment must follow the agreed commercial terms.

## 16. Campaign Reporting

Each completed campaign should have a report appropriate to its objective.

Possible measurements include:

- Reach.
- Impressions.
- Engagement.
- Click-through rate.
- Storefront visits.
- Product views.
- Add-to-cart activity.
- Checkout initiation.
- Completed orders.
- Revenue attributed to the campaign.
- Advertising spend.
- Cost per click.
- Cost per acquisition.
- Conversion rate.
- Return on advertising spend, where reliable data exists.

Reports must identify:

- Measurement period.
- Data source.
- Attribution method.
- Known tracking limitations.
- Advertising spend.
- Service fees.
- Results by participating store where applicable.

The parent company must not present estimates as confirmed sales or claim that a campaign caused a purchase without an appropriate attribution basis.

## 17. Multi-Store Campaign Fairness

Shared campaigns must provide clear and fair treatment of participating stores.

The marketing team should document:

- Participating stores.
- Selection criteria.
- Campaign placement.
- Creative exposure.
- Budget contributions.
- Publication schedule.
- Store-specific results.

Participation must not automatically guarantee equal exposure where campaign objectives or commercial arrangements differ.

However, the parent company must not secretly favour a store through undisclosed use of another Store Owner's contribution or private performance information.

Where a campaign uses a selection or ranking process, its relevant criteria should be documented.

## 18. Customer Data and Marketing Consent

Campaigns must follow the platform's customer-data and consent standards.

The marketing team must:
- Use approved audience data.
- Respect marketing consent.
- Honour opt-out requests.
- Avoid unauthorised customer-list sharing.
- Limit access to necessary information.
- Record the purpose and lawful basis for processing.
- Apply store-specific consent and communication rules.

A customer's purchase from one store must not automatically authorise unrelated marketing from every other store.

Cross-store marketing must follow the central customer identity and privacy architecture.

## 19. Campaign Records

Each campaign must retain a structured record containing:

- Campaign identifier.
- Campaign type.
- Participating stores.
- Requesting Store Owner.
- Campaign proposal.
- Approvals.
- Budget and cost allocation.
- Creative assets.
- Publication channels.
- Schedule.
- Execution history.
- Changes.
- Performance reports.
- Pause or cancellation records.
- Final outcome.

Records must be access-controlled and retained according to applicable retention requirements.

## 20. Responsibilities

### Store Owner

Responsible for:
- Submitting accurate campaign information.
- Confirming product availability.
- Approving their store's campaign representation.
- Confirming promotional terms.
- Providing required permissions.
- Meeting agreed financial commitments.
- Communicating operational changes.

### Marketing Team

Responsible for:
- Assessing requests.
- Preparing proposals.
- Coordinating creative work.
- Managing approved campaigns.
- Monitoring execution.
- Reporting results.
- Maintaining campaign records.

### Creative Production Team

Responsible for:
- Producing approved assets.
- Meeting technical media standards.
- Maintaining asset quality.
- Recording production deliverables.
- Respecting agreed creative rights.

### Parent Company Management

Responsible for:
- Commercial approval where required.
- Shared campaign governance.
- Budget oversight.
- Conflict resolution.
- Compliance and risk oversight.
- Approval of exceptions.

## 21. Performance Review

The parent company should periodically review:

- Campaign requests received.
- Approval and rejection rates.
- Campaign delivery timelines.
- Marketing cost per store.
- Advertising spend.
- Campaign performance.
- Store Owner satisfaction.
- Repeat campaign participation.
- Creative production efficiency.
- Shared campaign effectiveness.

The purpose is to improve campaign quality, financial sustainability and value delivered to participating stores.
