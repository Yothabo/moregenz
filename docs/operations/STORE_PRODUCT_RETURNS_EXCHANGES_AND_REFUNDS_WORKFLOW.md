# Store Product Returns, Exchanges and Refunds Workflow

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the operational workflow for customer returns, exchanges, cancellations and refunds across participating stores.

It establishes consistent customer service standards while preserving each store's independent orders, inventory, financial records and commercial responsibilities.

The workflow must support lawful consumer rights, accurate financial reconciliation, traceable decisions and clear communication.

## 2. Core Principles

1. Customers must be treated fairly and consistently.
2. Applicable consumer rights cannot be removed through store policy.
3. Each return or refund must be linked to the original order.
4. Each store remains responsible for its own customer transactions and fulfilment obligations, subject to the parent company's contractual role.
5. Refunds must use the original order's recorded prices and payment information.
6. Returned stock must not automatically become sellable inventory.
7. Every decision and financial adjustment must be recorded.
8. Customer information must only be accessible to authorised parties.
9. Store A must not be financially responsible for Store B's transactions unless an explicit agreement provides otherwise.
10. Disputes must have a clear escalation route.

## 3. Scope

This workflow covers:

- Customer cancellation requests.
- Returns of delivered products.
- Exchanges.
- Refund requests.
- Incorrect products.
- Damaged products.
- Defective products.
- Missing items.
- Products not received.
- Delivery-related disputes.
- Partial refunds.
- Promotional and bundle order adjustments.
- Payment reversals and chargebacks.
- Returned stock inspection and disposition.

It applies to orders placed through supported storefront and assisted-order channels where the platform maintains an order record.

## 4. Roles and Responsibilities

### 4.1 Customer

The customer may:

- Submit a return or refund request.
- Provide the order reference.
- Explain the reason for the request.
- Submit relevant photographs or evidence where appropriate.
- Request an exchange where available.
- Track the request.
- Ask for a review or escalation.

Customers must not be required to provide unnecessary personal information or irrelevant evidence.

### 4.2 Store Owner

The Store Owner is responsible for:

- Receiving and reviewing requests for their store.
- Communicating with customers.
- Verifying order details.
- Assessing the reason for the request.
- Arranging eligible returns or exchanges.
- Coordinating product inspection.
- Approving or escalating requests within their authority.
- Ensuring refunds are properly authorised.
- Maintaining accurate store records.
- Following applicable legal requirements.

### 4.3 Parent Company Support Team

The parent company support team may:

- Assist with customer communication.
- Coordinate escalations.
- Review disputed decisions.
- Monitor service quality.
- Support payment and refund reconciliation.
- Identify recurring product or fulfilment issues.

The support team must not make financial commitments outside its authority.

### 4.4 Finance and Platform Administration

Finance is responsible for:

- Verifying payment status.
- Processing or coordinating authorised refunds.
- Reconciling payment-provider records.
- Recording refund transactions.
- Monitoring failed or delayed refunds.
- Maintaining financial audit records.

Platform administrators maintain workflow permissions, records and system integrity.

## 5. Customer Request Channels

Customers may submit requests through supported channels, including:

- Customer account order history.
- Store contact forms.
- Customer support channels.
- Approved email communication.
- Approved WhatsApp commerce workflows.

Every request must be linked to an existing order where possible.

Requests received outside the platform must be recorded in the relevant order or support record.

A request must not be treated as completed merely because a customer was sent a message.

## 6. Return and Refund Request Information

The platform should capture:

- Return or refund request ID.
- Original order ID.
- Store ID.
- Customer reference.
- Affected order items.
- Quantity affected.
- Request type.
- Reason.
- Customer explanation.
- Supporting evidence where relevant.
- Request timestamp.
- Preferred resolution.
- Assigned Store Owner or support representative.
- Current status.

The platform must not expose another store's order or customer information during request processing.

## 7. Request Categories

The system should distinguish between:

### 7.1 Change-of-Mind Requests

Requests where a customer no longer wants the product.

Eligibility depends on the applicable law, transaction type, product category, disclosures and contractual terms.

The platform must not assume that every change-of-mind request is automatically refundable or automatically excluded.

### 7.2 Defective or Unsafe Products

Requests involving products that are defective, unsafe, damaged or not fit for their intended purpose.

These requests must be assessed under applicable consumer protection requirements.

### 7.3 Incorrect or Incomplete Orders

Requests involving:

- Wrong product.
- Wrong variant.
- Wrong quantity.
- Missing item.
- Product materially different from its description.

### 7.4 Delivery Problems

Requests involving:

- Non-delivery.
- Damaged delivery.
- Incorrect delivery destination.
- Unresolved delivery delays.
- Lost parcels.

### 7.5 Other Disputes

Other requests must be reviewed individually and assigned an appropriate resolution category.

## 8. Request Statuses

The platform should support the following statuses:

- Submitted.
- Under review.
- Awaiting customer information.
- Awaiting return.
- Return in transit.
- Received for inspection.
- Inspection completed.
- Approved.
- Partially approved.
- Rejected.
- Exchange pending.
- Refund pending.
- Refund processing.
- Refund completed.
- Closed.
- Escalated.

Status changes must be recorded with timestamps and the responsible actor.

## 9. Initial Assessment

When a request is received, the responsible Store Owner or support representative must:

1. Confirm the order exists.
2. Confirm the order belongs to the relevant store.
3. Verify the customer and order reference.
4. Identify the affected products and quantities.
5. Confirm payment and fulfilment status.
6. Determine the reason for the request.
7. Identify any applicable legal or contractual requirements.
8. Decide whether the request can be handled directly or requires escalation.

The platform should identify relevant order and payment information without requiring staff to reconstruct the transaction manually.

## 10. Legal Eligibility Assessment

The decision must consider applicable South African law, including the Consumer Protection Act and relevant regulations.

The platform must distinguish between statutory rights, contractual rights and voluntary store policies.

For qualifying electronic transactions, the applicable cooling-off provisions must be considered. The platform must not apply a blanket rule that excludes all online purchases from cooling-off rights.

Certain products or circumstances may be subject to specific statutory exceptions or conditions. These must be assessed against the actual transaction and product.

Where a product is defective, unsafe or fails to meet applicable consumer standards, the relevant statutory remedies and time periods must be considered.

Store policies may provide customers with additional rights but must not reduce mandatory legal protections.

Where the correct legal treatment is uncertain, the request must be escalated for appropriate legal review rather than automatically rejected.

## 11. Return Authorisation

Where a physical return is required, the Store Owner or authorised representative must provide the customer with:

- Return reference.
- Products and quantities to return.
- Return instructions.
- Approved return destination.
- Packaging guidance where appropriate.
- Applicable return transport arrangements.
- Expected next steps.

The platform must record the return authorisation.

Customers must not be instructed to send products to an unverified or unrelated address.

Return transport costs must be allocated according to applicable law, the reason for the return and the agreed transaction terms.

## 12. Return Receipt and Inspection

When a returned product arrives, the receiving party must record:

- Date received.
- Receiving person.
- Return reference.
- Product and variant.
- Quantity.
- Packaging condition.
- Product condition.
- Missing accessories or components.
- Relevant photographs where appropriate.
- Inspection outcome.

Possible inspection outcomes include:

- Suitable for resale.
- Suitable for resale after approved preparation.
- Requires repair or further assessment.
- Defective.
- Damaged.
- Incomplete.
- Not the product expected.
- Unable to verify.

Inspection must be proportionate and must not be used to obstruct legitimate statutory rights.

A customer's legal remedy must not be denied solely because a return inspection process has not been completed within an unreasonable period.

## 13. Exchange Workflow

Where an exchange is appropriate and agreed, the platform must:

1. Link the exchange to the original order.
2. Identify the returned item.
3. Record the replacement product and variant.
4. Confirm replacement stock availability.
5. Calculate any lawful and agreed price difference.
6. Record additional payment or refund requirements.
7. Arrange fulfilment.
8. Notify the customer.
9. Preserve the original order and exchange history.

An exchange must not silently overwrite the original order.

If the replacement product is unavailable, the customer must be informed of available lawful alternatives.

## 14. Refund Calculation

Refund calculations must use the original order's pricing snapshot.

The calculation must account for:

- Original unit price.
- Quantity.
- Discounts.
- Promotional conditions.
- Taxes.
- Delivery charges.
- Amount already paid.
- Previous partial refunds.
- Applicable statutory entitlements.
- Any lawful and agreed adjustments.

The platform must not use the current catalogue price to calculate a historical refund.

Where only part of an order is returned, the system must calculate the affected amount without incorrectly refunding unrelated items.

Delivery charges and other costs must be treated according to applicable law and the circumstances of the return.

## 15. Refund Approval

Refund approval must follow documented financial authority limits.

The workflow should distinguish:

- Store Owner approval.
- Parent company support review.
- Finance verification.
- Additional approval for exceptional or high-value refunds.

The exact approval limits must be configured in the financial governance policy.

No individual should be able to bypass required financial controls by changing a request status manually.

A refund must not be marked completed until payment-provider or other applicable financial evidence confirms completion.

## 16. Refund Processing

Where the original payment method supports a refund, the refund should normally be processed through the appropriate payment channel, subject to applicable legal requirements and provider capabilities.

The platform must record:

- Original payment reference.
- Refund reference.
- Refund amount.
- Currency.
- Payment provider.
- Processing date.
- Processing actor.
- Provider response.
- Current refund status.

For manual payment methods, the platform must require appropriate evidence and independent verification.

The platform must prevent duplicate refunds.

If a refund fails, the customer must be informed and the request must remain open until a valid resolution is recorded.

## 17. Returned Inventory Treatment

A returned product must not automatically increase available stock.

The inventory workflow must distinguish:

- Returned, awaiting inspection.
- Approved for resale.
- Quarantined.
- Damaged.
- Defective.
- Written off.
- Returned to supplier.
- Repaired and released.

Only authorised inspection outcomes may return an item to sellable inventory.

Inventory adjustments must reference the return request and the original order.

Stock changes must remain isolated to the relevant store.

## 18. Partial Refunds

The platform must support partial refunds where justified.

Examples include:

- One item returned from a multi-item order.
- One damaged item in an otherwise fulfilled order.
- A missing component.
- An agreed partial resolution.
- A partial cancellation.

Every partial refund must record:

- Affected order items.
- Quantity.
- Reason.
- Calculation.
- Approval.
- Payment reference.
- Remaining refundable amount.

The platform must prevent cumulative refunds from exceeding the amount legitimately refundable for the transaction.

## 19. Promotional and Bundle Orders

Returns involving discounts, vouchers or bundles must follow the original promotion terms and applicable consumer law.

The platform must retain:

- Original promotion.
- Discount allocation.
- Bundle composition.
- Original line-item prices.
- Refund calculation.
- Any remaining promotion eligibility.

The platform must not invent a new price after the purchase to reduce a customer's valid refund.

Where a bundle is partially returned, the system must apply a documented and legally compliant calculation.

## 20. Cancellations Before Fulfilment

Cancellation requests must be assessed against the order's current fulfilment stage and applicable legal rights.

The platform should distinguish:

- Order not yet accepted.
- Order accepted but not prepared.
- Order being prepared.
- Order dispatched.
- Order delivered.

The system must prevent fulfilment from proceeding when a valid cancellation has been approved and the order can still be stopped.

Where dispatch has already occurred, the request must follow the appropriate cancellation, delivery interception or return process.

## 21. Chargebacks and Payment Disputes

A payment dispute or chargeback must be recorded separately from an ordinary customer refund request.

The responsible team must:

- Identify the original transaction.
- Confirm the disputed amount.
- Review payment and fulfilment evidence.
- Preserve relevant customer communication.
- Preserve delivery evidence.
- Record response deadlines.
- Coordinate the response with the payment provider.
- Record the final outcome.

The platform must prevent an accidental duplicate refund where a chargeback has already reversed the payment.

## 22. Customer Communication

Customers must receive clear updates when:

- Their request is received.
- Additional information is required.
- A return is authorised.
- A returned product is received.
- An inspection is completed.
- A request is approved or rejected.
- An exchange is arranged.
- A refund is initiated.
- A refund is completed.
- A request is escalated.

Rejections must include a clear reason and explain the available review or escalation route.

Customer communications must avoid disclosing internal financial information or another customer's data.

## 23. Disputes and Escalation

A customer may request a review where they disagree with a decision.

The review should be handled by an authorised person who was not solely responsible for the original disputed decision where practical.

Escalation may involve:

1. Store Owner.
2. Parent company customer support.
3. Parent company operations or compliance.
4. Finance for payment disputes.
5. Legal adviser where required.

The platform must preserve the original decision and the subsequent review history.

Customers must not be prevented from exercising external statutory or regulatory remedies.

## 24. Financial Reconciliation

Every completed refund must reconcile against:

- Original order.
- Original payment.
- Refund transaction.
- Payment-provider settlement.
- Store financial ledger.
- Applicable parent-company fees or adjustments.

Refunds must be attributed to the correct store.

The platform must distinguish:

- Refund requested.
- Refund approved.
- Refund submitted.
- Refund confirmed.
- Refund reconciled.

A refund must not be treated as financially settled merely because it has been approved internally.

Any mismatch must be recorded and investigated.

## 25. Permissions and Store Isolation

Store Owners may only access return and refund records belonging to their assigned store, subject to their permissions.

The platform must enforce store scope server-side.

Store Owners must not be able to:

- View another store's customer return requests.
- Approve another store's refunds.
- Change another store's inventory.
- Access another store's payment references.
- Modify another store's order history.

Parent company finance and support access must be governed by explicit roles and audit records.

## 26. Audit and Activity Records

The platform must record:

- Request creation.
- Request classification.
- Eligibility assessment.
- Return authorisation.
- Return receipt.
- Inspection outcome.
- Approval or rejection.
- Exchange creation.
- Refund calculation.
- Refund approval.
- Refund submission.
- Refund confirmation.
- Inventory adjustment.
- Customer notification.
- Escalation.
- Final closure.

Each record should include:

- Event ID.
- Request ID.
- Store ID.
- Order ID.
- Actor ID.
- Actor role.
- Action.
- Previous status.
- New status.
- Reason.
- Amount where applicable.
- Timestamp.
- Supporting reference.

Audit history must be protected against unauthorised alteration.

## 27. Data Protection

Return and refund records may contain customer information, photographs, payment references and other personal information.

The platform must:

- Collect only information necessary for the request.
- Restrict access by role and store.
- Protect supporting evidence.
- Avoid storing full payment card details.
- Apply appropriate retention rules.
- Securely dispose of information when lawful retention is no longer required.
- Preserve records that must be retained for legal, financial or dispute purposes.

Processing must comply with applicable privacy requirements, including POPIA.

## 28. Reporting and Monitoring

The parent company should be able to monitor:

- Return request volume.
- Refund volume and value.
- Exchange volume.
- Common return reasons.
- Defective product rates.
- Fulfilment-related complaints.
- Average resolution time.
- Outstanding refunds.
- Failed refund transactions.
- Dispute and chargeback outcomes.
- Inventory write-offs.
- Store-level return trends.

Store Owners may view their own store's return and refund performance.

Platform-wide reports must preserve the confidentiality of individual store records.

## 29. Error Handling and Recovery

The platform must handle:

- Invalid order references.
- Duplicate requests.
- Duplicate refund attempts.
- Failed payment-provider refunds.
- Partial refund failures.
- Missing return evidence.
- Incorrect store assignment.
- Inventory adjustment failures.
- Concurrent request updates.
- Disputed payment transactions.
- Network interruptions.

Refund and inventory operations must be designed to prevent duplicate financial or stock movements.

Retries must be idempotent where possible.

A failed refund must not be represented as completed.

A failed inventory update must not create a false available-stock balance.

## 30. Testing Requirements

The implementation must test:

1. Customers can submit return requests for valid orders.
2. Requests are linked to the correct store and order.
3. Store Owners cannot access another store's requests.
4. Return eligibility follows configured legal and contractual rules.
5. Existing order prices are preserved.
6. Partial refunds calculate correctly.
7. Promotional refunds preserve the original discount calculation.
8. Duplicate refund attempts are prevented.
9. Failed refunds remain unresolved.
10. Completed refunds reconcile with payment records.
11. Returned stock is quarantined until approved.
12. Exchanges preserve original order history.
13. Cancellation prevents eligible orders from progressing.
14. Chargebacks are distinguished from ordinary refunds.
15. Customer notifications reflect actual request status.
16. Audit records capture every significant action.
17. Inventory changes affect only the correct store.
18. Refunds cannot exceed the legitimate refundable amount.
19. Customer data is protected by role and store scope.
20. Failed operations can be retried without duplicate financial or inventory movements.

## 31. Implementation Guidance

Before implementation:

- Review existing order lifecycle functionality.
- Review payment-provider refund capabilities.
- Review manual payment verification.
- Review financial reconciliation procedures.
- Review inventory movement and stocktake records.
- Review customer account and order history functionality.
- Review Store Owner permissions.
- Review existing support escalation workflows.

Do not introduce a separate refund ledger if the existing financial architecture already provides the required transaction and reconciliation records.

Any schema changes must include migrations, integrity constraints, appropriate indexes, rollback considerations and automated tests.

## 32. Governing Rule

Every return, exchange and refund must be handled fairly, lawfully and transparently.

The original order must remain historically accurate, the correct store must carry the correct financial responsibility, and returned inventory must only become available for sale after authorised assessment.

The platform must provide the infrastructure and controls necessary to make these decisions traceable and accountable.
