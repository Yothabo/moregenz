# Store Owner Onboarding and Store Launch Procedure

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the operational procedure for onboarding an approved Store Owner and preparing their assigned store for public launch.

It establishes a consistent process for collecting business information, establishing the store identity, preparing products, producing storefront media, configuring platform services, testing operational workflows and approving launch.

The objective is to ensure that every store launches with accurate information, a recognisable brand identity, properly configured commerce functionality and a Store Owner who understands how to operate it.

## 2. Onboarding Principles

Every onboarding process must follow these principles:

1. A store must have an approved Store Owner before public launch.
2. The Store Owner's business identity must be kept distinct from the parent company.
3. The parent company must provide the agreed infrastructure and onboarding support.
4. The Store Owner must participate in business, product and creative decisions.
5. Product information must be verified before publication.
6. Media must meet the platform's technical and visual requirements.
7. Payment, order and fulfilment workflows must be tested.
8. Security and privacy requirements must be completed.
9. Launch approval must be documented.
10. The onboarding history must remain associated with the store.

## 3. Onboarding Lifecycle

The recommended onboarding lifecycle is:

1. Admission approval.
2. Agreement and commercial acceptance.
3. Store creation or assignment.
4. Business and brand information collection.
5. Store identity configuration.
6. Product catalogue preparation.
7. Creative production.
8. Storefront configuration.
9. Payment and fulfilment configuration.
10. Dashboard access and training.
11. Quality assurance.
12. Launch approval.
13. Public launch.
14. Initial operational review.

Each stage must have an identifiable status and responsible party.

## 4. Stage 1: Admission Approval

Before onboarding begins, confirm that the applicant has completed the recruitment and assessment process.

Verify:

- Applicant identity.
- Authority to represent the business.
- Admission decision.
- Mandatory admission gates.
- Outstanding conditions.
- Approved business category.
- Product or service suitability.
- Relevant compliance requirements.

The admission record must reference the assessment and approval.

Applicants with unresolved mandatory conditions must not proceed to public launch.

## 5. Stage 2: Agreement and Commercial Acceptance

Before activating paid services, obtain the required agreement acceptance.

This may include:

- Store Owner Agreement.
- Subscription Agreement.
- Trial Agreement, where applicable.
- Brand and Intellectual Property Terms.
- Privacy and Data Processing Terms.
- Creative Production Terms.
- Marketing Services Agreement, if purchased.
- Other applicable schedules.

Record:

- Agreement version.
- Acceptance date.
- Accepting person.
- Subscription plan.
- Trial start and end dates.
- Included services.
- Approved additional services.
- Applicable payment terms.

A trial must not automatically convert into a paid subscription without explicit acceptance.

## 6. Stage 3: Store Creation or Assignment

The parent company creates a new store or assigns the approved Store Owner to an existing store through an authorised succession process.

Capture:

- Store ID.
- Store name, where approved.
- Store category.
- Store creation method.
- Assigned Store Owner.
- Assignment start date.
- Agreement reference.
- Store status.
- Proposed storefront address.
- Initial configuration.

If the store is created through cloning, follow the Store Cloning Architecture and ensure that no private operational records are copied.

The new store must receive independent operational data.

## 7. Stage 4: Business Information Collection

Collect the information needed to configure the store and accurately represent the business.

### 7.1 Business identity

- Business or trading name.
- Business description.
- Contact information.
- Location or service area.
- Customer support details.
- Business operating hours, where applicable.
- Business registration information, where applicable.

### 7.2 Business model

- Products or services offered.
- Target customers.
- Product sourcing or production method.
- Pricing approach.
- Fulfilment method.
- Delivery coverage.
- Collection arrangements.
- Return and refund approach.
- Customer service process.

### 7.3 Legal and compliance information

Identify relevant obligations based on the business and product category.

Do not assume every Store Owner requires identical registration, permits or licences.

Record any required verification and outstanding compliance actions.

## 8. Stage 5: Brand Identity Configuration

The Store Owner must provide or approve the identity used for their store.

Collect, where available:

- Store name.
- Logo.
- Brand colours.
- Typography preferences.
- Brand voice.
- Brand description.
- Tagline.
- Product positioning.
- Target audience.
- Reference imagery.
- Existing brand guidelines.

### 8.1 Brand review

The parent company should review submitted material for:

- Technical suitability.
- Readability.
- Accessibility.
- Consistency.
- Potential intellectual property conflicts.
- Suitability for the platform.

If improvements are recommended, the Store Owner must be involved in the decision.

### 8.2 Brand approval

Record:

- Submitted assets.
- Asset source.
- Ownership or licence information.
- Proposed changes.
- Store Owner feedback.
- Final approved assets.
- Approval date.

Do not publish assets with unresolved ownership or usage rights.

## 9. Stage 6: Product Catalogue Preparation

The Store Owner supplies the product or service information required for the catalogue.

Each product should be reviewed for:

- Product name.
- Description.
- Category.
- Price.
- Product attributes.
- Variants.
- Availability.
- Inventory arrangement.
- Delivery or collection requirements.
- Product-specific legal information.
- Accurate product claims.

### 9.1 Product content quality

Descriptions must be accurate, understandable and useful to customers.

The parent company may assist with copywriting and presentation, but it must not invent product specifications, ingredients, certifications or performance claims.

### 9.2 Product verification

Before publication, confirm:

- The product exists or can reasonably be supplied.
- The Store Owner has the right to sell it.
- Pricing has been approved.
- Product information is accurate.
- Required disclosures are present.
- Product images represent the actual product.

### 9.3 Catalogue readiness

A store should have enough approved product content to provide a coherent customer experience.

The required catalogue size may differ by business type.

A service business may not require the same catalogue structure as a clothing retailer.

## 10. Stage 7: Central Creative Production

The central creative team prepares platform-ready visual assets.

The production process should include:

1. Review product and brand information.
2. Identify the required visual assets.
3. Confirm the production scope.
4. Agree any additional costs.
5. Schedule the production session.
6. Capture photographs or video.
7. Edit and prepare the media.
8. Generate required responsive variants.
9. Optimise file sizes.
10. Review visual quality.
11. Obtain Store Owner approval where required.
12. Store the approved assets in the correct location.

The Store Owner contributes product knowledge, styling preferences, references and creative direction.

The parent company controls technical media standards and platform compatibility.

The included subscription production allowance must be respected.

Additional production requires approval and agreed pricing.

## 11. Stage 8: Storefront Configuration

Configure the storefront using the approved brand and business information.

Configuration may include:

- Store name.
- Logo.
- Brand colours.
- Navigation.
- Homepage sections.
- Product categories.
- Product listings.
- Banners.
- Store information.
- Contact details.
- Delivery information.
- Returns information.
- Legal disclosures.
- Search metadata.
- Social sharing metadata.
- Structured product data.

The storefront must follow the approved platform design system and responsive standards.

Store Owners may request supported configuration changes through the dashboard.

Platform code and shared infrastructure remain under parent-company control.

## 12. Stage 9: Payment and Fulfilment Configuration

Before launch, establish the supported payment and fulfilment workflows.

### 12.1 Payment configuration

Confirm:

- Enabled payment methods.
- Payment provider configuration, where applicable.
- Merchant or account ownership.
- Payment confirmation workflow.
- Manual payment verification, where supported.
- Refund handling.
- Payment reconciliation.
- Customer payment instructions.

Payment processing must not be represented as operational until the relevant integration or manual procedure has been tested.

### 12.2 Fulfilment configuration

Confirm:

- Delivery methods.
- Delivery coverage.
- Delivery charges.
- Collection options.
- Processing times.
- Dispatch procedures.
- Order tracking arrangements.
- Customer communication.
- Returns and exchanges.
- Refund responsibilities.

The Store Owner and parent company must understand their respective responsibilities.

## 13. Stage 10: Dashboard Access and Training

Each Store Owner must receive an individual account.

Do not share personal login credentials between Store Owners.

Provide access according to the approved permissions model.

Training should cover:

- Dashboard navigation.
- Product management.
- Product availability.
- Order monitoring.
- Inventory workflows.
- Customer communication.
- Store performance information.
- Creative requests.
- Marketing requests.
- Store change requests.
- Support requests.
- Security practices.
- Password and account recovery.

The Store Owner should understand which decisions they can make directly and which require parent-company approval.

Record training completion and any outstanding support needs.

## 14. Stage 11: Quality Assurance

The parent company must conduct a launch-readiness review.

### 14.1 Brand and presentation

Verify:

- Correct store name.
- Correct logo.
- Approved colours.
- Correct product presentation.
- Consistent typography.
- Responsive layouts.
- Accessible navigation.
- Correct store attribution.

### 14.2 Product content

Verify:

- Accurate descriptions.
- Correct prices.
- Correct variants.
- Correct images.
- Correct availability.
- No misleading claims.
- Required disclosures.

### 14.3 Technical functionality

Test:

- Homepage loading.
- Navigation.
- Product pages.
- Search.
- Cart.
- Checkout.
- Payment workflow.
- Order confirmation.
- Order management.
- Customer account functionality.
- Mobile presentation.
- Desktop presentation.
- Error handling.

### 14.4 Operational readiness

Verify:

- Store Owner access.
- Support contacts.
- Fulfilment procedures.
- Delivery settings.
- Refund procedures.
- Inventory arrangements.
- Customer service readiness.
- Security settings.

### 14.5 Privacy and security

Verify:

- Appropriate account permissions.
- No shared credentials.
- Store-level data isolation.
- Customer information protection.
- Privacy notices.
- Marketing consent handling.
- Required third-party permissions.

## 15. Stage 12: Launch Approval

A store must not be launched publicly until all mandatory launch checks have passed.

The launch approval record should include:

- Store ID.
- Store Owner.
- Launch checklist.
- Outstanding issues.
- Compliance status.
- Technical test results.
- Product approval status.
- Media approval status.
- Payment readiness.
- Fulfilment readiness.
- Store Owner acceptance.
- Approving person.
- Approval date.
- Planned launch date.

Minor non-blocking issues may be recorded for post-launch correction.

Critical security, payment, legal, product accuracy or customer-impacting issues must be resolved before launch.

## 16. Stage 13: Public Launch

Once approved:

- Activate the storefront.
- Confirm the public domain or storefront address.
- Verify the live experience.
- Test key customer journeys.
- Confirm product availability.
- Confirm order notifications.
- Confirm support channels.
- Publish approved launch communications.
- Record the actual launch date.

The Store Owner should receive confirmation that the store is live and operational.

## 17. Stage 14: Initial Operational Review

The parent company should review the store after launch.

The first review should assess:

- Storefront performance.
- Product presentation.
- Customer experience.
- Order activity.
- Payment workflow.
- Fulfilment performance.
- Customer enquiries.
- Store Owner dashboard usage.
- Support requirements.
- Technical issues.
- Marketing opportunities.

The purpose is to identify and resolve operational problems early.

Early sales volume must not be treated as the only measure of launch success.

## 18. Onboarding Status Model

The platform should support a traceable onboarding status.

Suggested statuses include:

- Application approved.
- Agreement pending.
- Agreement accepted.
- Store creation pending.
- Business information pending.
- Brand review.
- Catalogue preparation.
- Creative production.
- Storefront configuration.
- Payment configuration.
- Fulfilment configuration.
- Training pending.
- Quality assurance.
- Launch approval pending.
- Approved for launch.
- Live.
- Onboarding paused.
- Onboarding cancelled.

The final status model must align with the existing application architecture.

Every material status change should record the actor and timestamp.

## 19. Responsibility Matrix

| Activity | Store Owner | Parent company |
|---|---|---|
| Business information | Supplies accurate information | Reviews and configures |
| Brand direction | Leads and approves | Advises and implements |
| Product information | Supplies and verifies | Reviews and publishes |
| Product photography | Provides products and direction | Produces and prepares media |
| Technical media standards | Provides feedback | Defines and enforces |
| Storefront infrastructure | Uses assigned store | Owns and maintains platform |
| Payment setup | Provides required business information | Configures supported systems |
| Fulfilment | Operates agreed fulfilment process | Provides platform workflows |
| Dashboard training | Participates | Provides training |
| Launch approval | Confirms readiness | Conducts final checks |
| Platform security | Follows account policies | Maintains controls |

Specific responsibilities must be confirmed in the applicable agreements.

## 20. Onboarding Records

The platform should maintain an onboarding record containing:

- Onboarding ID.
- Store ID.
- Store Owner assignment.
- Assigned onboarding coordinator.
- Admission reference.
- Agreement references.
- Onboarding status.
- Checklist results.
- Supporting documents.
- Brand approval records.
- Product readiness.
- Creative production records.
- Payment configuration status.
- Fulfilment configuration status.
- Training completion.
- Quality assurance results.
- Launch approval.
- Actual launch date.

Sensitive documents must be protected through appropriate access controls and retention policies.

## 21. Implementation Sequence

### Phase 1: Operational procedure

- Establish the onboarding checklist.
- Assign responsibilities.
- Define required documents.
- Define approval gates.
- Establish launch criteria.

### Phase 2: Dashboard support

- Introduce onboarding status tracking.
- Add document and asset submission workflows.
- Add approval records.
- Add onboarding task ownership.
- Add progress visibility.

### Phase 3: Creative and catalogue integration

- Connect product preparation to creative production.
- Record production scope and costs.
- Record Store Owner approvals.
- Track asset readiness.

### Phase 4: Launch controls

- Introduce launch-readiness checks.
- Record technical test results.
- Enforce required approvals.
- Record launch events.

### Phase 5: Continuous improvement

- Measure onboarding time.
- Identify common delays.
- Review support requirements.
- Improve training.
- Refine the onboarding process.

## 22. Final Standard

Every Store Owner must enter the platform through a documented and controlled onboarding process.

The parent company must provide the infrastructure, technical standards, creative production and agreed support.

The Store Owner must contribute the business information, product knowledge, brand direction and operational commitment required to establish a functioning store.

No store should be launched merely because its storefront has been created. It must be commercially, technically and operationally ready to serve customers.
