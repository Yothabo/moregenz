# Store Supplier Onboarding and Procurement Management Standards

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes how suppliers are identified, assessed, approved and managed across participating stores.

It also defines how Store Owners source products, place purchase orders, receive stock, verify supplier deliveries and maintain procurement records.

The framework must support existing entrepreneurs with established supplier relationships, informal businesses with local sourcing arrangements and new ventures that require assistance identifying reliable suppliers.

## 2. Core Principles

1. Store Owners retain meaningful control over their product sourcing and supplier relationships, subject to applicable agreements and platform requirements.
2. Supplier records must distinguish independently engaged suppliers from centrally approved or shared suppliers.
3. A supplier used by one store must not automatically become an approved supplier for every other store.
4. Supplier verification must be proportionate to the product, transaction value and associated risk.
5. Procurement decisions must be traceable.
6. Supplier costs and commercial terms must remain confidential.
7. Stock must only be recorded as received after appropriate verification.
8. Supplier approval does not guarantee product quality or legal compliance.
9. Parent-company involvement in procurement must be transparent.
10. Supplier relationships and purchasing records must survive Store Owner changes where necessary for business continuity.

## 3. Supplier Relationship Types

The platform must distinguish the following supplier relationships.

### 3.1 Store-Specific Supplier

A supplier engaged directly by an individual Store Owner.

The supplier relationship belongs to the relevant store's procurement records.

Other stores must not automatically gain access to its private pricing, negotiated terms or purchasing history.

### 3.2 Parent-Company Approved Supplier

A supplier assessed and approved by the parent company for potential use across participating stores.

Approval means the supplier has passed the defined assessment process. It does not mean every store must use that supplier.

Individual stores may still need to complete their own commercial arrangements.

### 3.3 Shared Supplier

A supplier used by multiple stores under an approved shared arrangement.

The agreement must define:

- Participating stores.
- Contracting party.
- Pricing arrangements.
- Ordering responsibilities.
- Payment responsibilities.
- Delivery arrangements.
- Quality standards.
- Returns and dispute procedures.
- Confidentiality requirements.

### 3.4 Supplier Introduced by a Store Owner

A Store Owner may recommend a supplier for possible inclusion in the parent company's approved supplier network.

The parent company must not assume ownership of the Store Owner's existing supplier relationship merely because the supplier is introduced through the platform.

Any shared commercial arrangement must be agreed explicitly.

## 4. Roles and Responsibilities

### 4.1 Store Owner

The Store Owner is responsible for:

- Identifying suitable suppliers.
- Assessing product suitability.
- Negotiating store-specific terms.
- Confirming prices and availability.
- Placing authorised purchase orders.
- Monitoring supplier performance.
- Verifying received stock.
- Maintaining procurement records.
- Reporting supplier problems.
- Ensuring products can lawfully be offered for sale.

### 4.2 Parent Company

The parent company is responsible for:

- Maintaining supplier governance standards.
- Reviewing suppliers proposed for shared approval.
- Supporting supplier discovery where appropriate.
- Negotiating shared arrangements where authorised.
- Monitoring material supplier risks.
- Maintaining centrally managed supplier agreements.
- Providing procurement infrastructure.
- Escalating significant supplier disputes.

The parent company must not represent a supplier as centrally approved when the required assessment has not been completed.

### 4.3 Finance

Finance is responsible for:

- Verifying supplier invoices.
- Recording supplier payments.
- Reconciling procurement expenditure.
- Monitoring outstanding supplier balances.
- Reviewing payment exceptions.
- Maintaining financial records.

### 4.4 Platform Administration

Platform administrators maintain:

- Supplier records.
- Procurement permissions.
- Purchase-order workflows.
- Goods-received records.
- Supplier status controls.
- Audit history.
- Store-level data isolation.

## 5. Supplier Onboarding

Before a supplier is used for material or recurring procurement, the responsible Store Owner or parent company representative should assess the supplier.

The assessment must be proportionate to the risk.

### 5.1 Supplier Information

Where applicable, capture:

- Supplier legal or trading name.
- Contact person.
- Business address.
- Contact details.
- Registration information where applicable.
- Tax information where legally required.
- Product categories supplied.
- Product origin.
- Payment terms.
- Delivery arrangements.
- Return and warranty conditions.
- Relevant licences or permits.
- Bank-account verification status.
- References or trading history where appropriate.

A small informal supplier must not automatically be rejected solely because they lack company registration if the law does not require it for their circumstances.

The platform must distinguish mandatory legal requirements from internal preferences.

### 5.2 Supplier Verification

Verification may include:

- Confirming the supplier's identity.
- Checking business details where available.
- Confirming contact information.
- Verifying bank-account details through an appropriate process.
- Reviewing product samples.
- Checking product authenticity.
- Confirming distribution or resale authority where relevant.
- Reviewing references.
- Assessing delivery reliability.
- Reviewing relevant compliance documentation.

The level of verification must reflect the risk of the supplier and the products involved.

### 5.3 Supplier Approval Status

The platform should support:

- Draft.
- Under review.
- Approved.
- Approved with conditions.
- Restricted.
- Suspended.
- Rejected.
- Archived.

Approval status must include the responsible reviewer, decision date and reason.

## 6. Product and Supplier Suitability

Supplier approval must not automatically approve every product supplied by that supplier.

Product assessment must consider:

- Product identity.
- Quality.
- Safety.
- Authenticity.
- Labelling.
- Product specifications.
- Intellectual property rights.
- Applicable permits or restrictions.
- Consumer disclosure requirements.
- Storage and handling requirements.
- Warranty or after-sales support.

Products that are unlawful, unsafe, counterfeit or materially misrepresented must not be offered for sale.

Where product legitimacy cannot be established, the listing must remain restricted pending verification.

## 7. Supplier Agreements

Supplier relationships should have clear written terms proportionate to their commercial importance.

Where applicable, agreements should address:

- Products supplied.
- Pricing.
- Minimum order quantities.
- Payment terms.
- Delivery times.
- Delivery costs.
- Product quality.
- Product authenticity.
- Warranties.
- Returns.
- Defective goods.
- Replacement obligations.
- Confidentiality.
- Intellectual property.
- Data protection.
- Termination.
- Dispute resolution.

The agreement must identify the actual contracting parties.

The parent company must not be represented as the buyer or guarantor where the Store Owner is independently purchasing the products.

## 8. Procurement Planning

Before placing a purchase order, the Store Owner should review:

- Current stock.
- Reserved stock.
- Available stock.
- Expected demand.
- Outstanding purchase orders.
- Supplier lead times.
- Minimum order quantities.
- Available budget.
- Product profitability.
- Storage capacity.
- Product expiry or shelf-life requirements where applicable.

The platform may provide reorder suggestions based on stock thresholds and sales history.

Automated suggestions must not place purchase orders without the required authority.

## 9. Purchase Order Creation

A purchase order should contain:

- Purchase order number.
- Store ID.
- Supplier ID.
- Order date.
- Expected delivery date.
- Product references.
- Product variants.
- Quantities.
- Agreed unit costs.
- Applicable taxes.
- Delivery charges.
- Total order value.
- Payment terms.
- Delivery destination.
- Special instructions.
- Authorising person.
- Purchase-order status.

The purchase order must clearly identify which store is making the purchase.

## 10. Purchase Order Approval

Approval requirements should be based on documented authority limits.

Possible controls include:

- Store Owner approval for ordinary purchases within their authority.
- Additional approval for purchases exceeding configured limits.
- Finance review for unusual payment arrangements.
- Parent company approval for shared procurement commitments.
- Additional review for high-risk or regulated products.

Approval limits must be configured rather than embedded as unexplained hard-coded assumptions.

The platform must record approval decisions and reasons.

## 11. Purchase Order Lifecycle

The platform should support:

1. Draft.
2. Submitted for approval.
3. Approved.
4. Sent to supplier.
5. Supplier acknowledged.
6. Partially delivered.
7. Fully delivered.
8. Partially invoiced.
9. Fully invoiced.
10. Partially paid.
11. Fully paid.
12. Closed.
13. Cancelled.
14. Disputed.

Status transitions must follow documented rules.

A purchase order must not be marked fully received merely because the supplier says it has been dispatched.

## 12. Goods Received Procedure

When stock arrives, the receiving person must compare the delivery against the purchase order and supplier documentation.

The receiving process should record:

- Purchase order reference.
- Supplier delivery note.
- Date received.
- Receiving person.
- Product and variant.
- Quantity ordered.
- Quantity received.
- Quantity accepted.
- Quantity rejected.
- Product condition.
- Packaging condition.
- Batch or serial number where relevant.
- Expiry date where applicable.
- Photographic evidence where appropriate.
- Discrepancies.
- Inspection outcome.

The platform must support partial deliveries.

Only accepted quantities may be added to sellable stock, subject to any required inspection or quarantine process.

## 13. Supplier Delivery Discrepancies

Discrepancies may include:

- Short delivery.
- Excess delivery.
- Wrong product.
- Wrong variant.
- Damaged goods.
- Defective goods.
- Missing documentation.
- Incorrect pricing.
- Unauthorised substitutions.

The Store Owner must record the discrepancy and notify the supplier where required.

The platform should support:

- Accepted quantity.
- Rejected quantity.
- Outstanding quantity.
- Replacement requested.
- Credit requested.
- Supplier dispute.
- Resolution status.

Unverified excess stock must not silently become available inventory.

## 14. Supplier Invoice Verification

Supplier invoices must be compared against:

- Approved purchase order.
- Goods-received record.
- Agreed supplier pricing.
- Delivery charges.
- Applicable taxes.
- Credit notes.
- Approved variations.

The platform should identify mismatches for review.

A payment must not be approved solely because an invoice was uploaded.

The person approving payment must have the appropriate authority.

## 15. Supplier Payments

Supplier payments must follow the agreed payment terms and financial approval process.

The platform should record:

- Supplier invoice reference.
- Purchase order.
- Store ID.
- Amount due.
- Amount paid.
- Payment date.
- Payment method.
- Payment reference.
- Outstanding balance.
- Payment status.
- Authorising person.

Store-specific procurement expenditure must remain attributed to the correct store.

Where the parent company pays a supplier on behalf of a store, the arrangement must be recorded with the correct financial treatment and supporting agreement.

## 16. Supplier Performance Management

Supplier performance should be assessed using relevant indicators, including:

- Delivery reliability.
- Product quality.
- Order accuracy.
- Pricing consistency.
- Communication.
- Dispute resolution.
- Product authenticity.
- Documentation quality.
- Return handling.
- Compliance.

The platform may record performance reviews and supporting evidence.

Supplier ratings must be based on actual recorded experience and must not be presented as verified facts without adequate evidence.

A supplier may receive:

- Normal monitoring.
- Improvement request.
- Conditional approval.
- Restricted status.
- Suspension.
- Removal from the approved supplier network.

A supplier's status in one store must not automatically change another store's independent supplier relationship unless a documented risk or legal restriction requires wider action.

## 17. Supplier Disputes

Supplier disputes may involve:

- Non-delivery.
- Late delivery.
- Incorrect invoices.
- Defective goods.
- Product authenticity.
- Breach of agreement.
- Unfulfilled warranties.
- Unauthorised price changes.
- Payment disagreements.

The responsible party must:

1. Record the dispute.
2. Preserve purchase orders and invoices.
3. Preserve delivery and inspection evidence.
4. Contact the supplier.
5. Record the supplier's response.
6. Agree or escalate a resolution.
7. Record any credit, replacement or financial adjustment.
8. Close the dispute only when the outcome is documented.

Material disputes may require parent company or legal review.

## 18. Supplier Changes and Store Owner Succession

Supplier records must distinguish between the supplier entity and the Store Owner's relationship with that supplier.

When a Store Owner leaves:

- Existing supplier records must remain historically accurate.
- Outstanding purchase orders must be identified.
- Outstanding invoices must be reconciled.
- Supplier disputes must remain open until resolved.
- Supplier access and permissions must be reviewed.
- A successor must receive only the access required to continue legitimate store operations.

A Store Owner's personal supplier relationships must not automatically be transferred to a successor without an appropriate legal or commercial basis.

## 19. Shared Procurement

Where multiple stores participate in a shared purchase, the arrangement must define:

- Participating stores.
- Total order quantity.
- Allocation per store.
- Unit cost.
- Shared transport costs.
- Payment responsibility.
- Receiving responsibility.
- Quality inspection.
- Dispute ownership.
- Returns and credit allocation.

The platform must create store-specific allocation records.

Shared procurement must not result in one store's stock or expenditure being incorrectly attributed to another store.

## 20. Supplier Data Confidentiality

Supplier records may contain commercially sensitive information.

The platform must restrict access to:

- Negotiated prices.
- Supplier costs.
- Bank details.
- Contracts.
- Payment records.
- Private supplier communications.
- Procurement history.

Store Owners may access their own supplier relationships and authorised shared suppliers.

Parent company personnel may access supplier information according to their responsibilities and permissions.

## 21. Audit and Activity Records

The platform must record:

- Supplier creation.
- Supplier verification.
- Approval or rejection.
- Supplier status changes.
- Purchase order creation.
- Purchase order approval.
- Purchase order amendment.
- Supplier acknowledgement.
- Goods received.
- Goods rejected.
- Invoice submission.
- Invoice approval.
- Supplier payment.
- Supplier dispute.
- Supplier credit.
- Supplier performance review.
- Supplier suspension or removal.

Each record should include:

- Event ID.
- Supplier ID.
- Store ID where applicable.
- Purchase order or invoice reference.
- Actor ID.
- Actor role.
- Action.
- Previous value where appropriate.
- New value where appropriate.
- Reason.
- Timestamp.
- Supporting evidence reference.

Audit records must be protected against unauthorised modification.

## 22. Reporting and Monitoring

Store Owners should be able to review:

- Their suppliers.
- Active purchase orders.
- Outstanding deliveries.
- Supplier invoices.
- Procurement expenditure.
- Supplier payment status.
- Delivery discrepancies.
- Supplier performance.
- Open supplier disputes.

The parent company may review:

- Approved supplier network.
- Shared procurement activity.
- Supplier concentration risks.
- Recurring supplier failures.
- Procurement policy compliance.
- Shared supplier performance.

Reports must respect store-level confidentiality.

## 23. Error Handling and Recovery

The platform must handle:

- Duplicate suppliers.
- Invalid supplier details.
- Duplicate purchase orders.
- Unauthorised purchase approvals.
- Partial deliveries.
- Duplicate goods-received records.
- Invoice mismatches.
- Failed payments.
- Incorrect stock allocation.
- Supplier status changes during active orders.
- Concurrent purchase-order updates.

Operations that affect financial or inventory records must be designed to prevent duplicate transactions.

Retrying a failed operation must not create duplicate purchase orders, stock movements or payments.

## 24. Testing Requirements

The implementation must test:

1. Store Owners can create store-specific supplier records.
2. Approved shared suppliers can be used by authorised stores.
3. Store-specific supplier information remains isolated.
4. Supplier verification status is recorded.
5. Restricted suppliers cannot be used where prohibited.
6. Purchase orders require the appropriate approval.
7. Purchase orders are linked to the correct store.
8. Partial deliveries are recorded accurately.
9. Rejected stock does not increase sellable inventory.
10. Goods received are linked to the correct purchase order.
11. Invoice mismatches are identified.
12. Supplier payments are recorded against the correct obligations.
13. Shared procurement allocations remain accurate.
14. Supplier disputes preserve supporting evidence.
15. Store Owner succession preserves historical records.
16. Store Owners cannot access another store's private procurement records.
17. Duplicate purchase-order and stock movements are prevented.
18. Audit history records material procurement actions.

## 25. Implementation Guidance

Before implementation:

- Review the existing supplier and vendor data model.
- Review product catalogue relationships.
- Review inventory receiving and stock movement procedures.
- Review financial reconciliation.
- Review payment permissions.
- Review Store Owner access controls.
- Review existing purchase-order functionality, if present.

Do not create a parallel supplier or procurement system where the existing architecture already supports the required records.

Any schema changes must include migrations, integrity constraints, appropriate indexes, rollback considerations and automated tests.

## 26. Governing Rule

Store Owners must be able to source and procure products through reliable, transparent processes while retaining control of their own commercial relationships.

The parent company provides supplier governance, shared infrastructure and oversight without automatically taking ownership of independent supplier relationships.

Every purchase, delivery, invoice, payment and dispute must remain traceable to the correct supplier and store.
