## 1. Purpose

This procedure defines how a participating store exits the platform, how its operational access and integrations are handled, and how its data and outstanding obligations are managed.

It aims to protect the store, the platform, customers, employees and other participating stores during an orderly exit.

## 2. Scope

This procedure covers:

- Voluntary store exit.
- Platform-initiated termination or suspension followed by exit.
- Store deactivation.
- Outstanding orders and customer commitments.
- Financial reconciliation and obligations.
- Product ownership and catalogue availability.
- Inventory and stock assistance.
- Customer and employee information.
- Storefronts and domains.
- API credentials and integrations.
- Data exports and handover.
- Historical records and retention.
- Final access revocation.

## 3. Exit Principles

A store exit must:

- Be authorised and documented.
- Preserve historical business records.
- Protect customer and employee information.
- Identify and resolve outstanding obligations.
- Prevent unauthorised access after exit.
- Preserve product ownership and source information.
- Avoid disrupting other participating stores.
- Distinguish suspension, deactivation and deletion.
- Follow applicable agreements and legal requirements.
- Provide a clear record of completion.

Store exit does not automatically mean that all associated data may be deleted.

## 4. Exit Types

### 4.1 Voluntary Exit

A store requests to discontinue participation.

The request must be submitted by an authorised representative and verified before processing.

### 4.2 Platform-Initiated Exit

The platform initiates termination according to applicable agreements, policies and authorised decisions.

The reason, authority and supporting evidence must be recorded.

### 4.3 Emergency Suspension

The platform may apply an authorised protective suspension where immediate action is necessary to protect customers, data, funds or platform operations.

Emergency suspension is not itself a completed store exit.

### 4.4 Store Deactivation

Deactivation removes the store from active platform operations while preserving records and obligations that must remain available.

## 5. Exit Authority

The authority to approve an exit must follow the applicable platform governance framework and store participation agreements.

A Store Admin may submit an exit request but must not automatically be treated as having authority to delete historical platform records or waive outstanding obligations.

Platform personnel must not use the exit process to bypass contractual, legal or dispute-resolution requirements.

## 6. Exit Request

The exit request should record:

- Store identity.
- Requesting representative.
- Verification of authority.
- Requested exit date.
- Reason, where provided.
- Outstanding operational concerns.
- Relevant agreements.
- Known financial obligations.
- Proposed handover arrangements.

The platform must acknowledge receipt and identify the responsible exit coordinator.

## 7. Exit Assessment

Before approving an exit, assess:

- Current store status.
- Active orders.
- Pending payments and refunds.
- Provider settlements.
- Customer commitments.
- Open complaints and disputes.
- Inventory and stock assistance.
- Outstanding financial obligations.
- Product ownership.
- Shared catalogue dependencies.
- Active integrations.
- Employee access.
- Data export requests.
- Legal holds and retention requirements.

Material unresolved matters must be assigned an owner and documented.

## 8. Exit Plan

An exit plan must define:

- Approved exit date.
- Responsible coordinator.
- Store communication.
- Customer communication where required.
- Order and fulfilment arrangements.
- Financial reconciliation.
- Inventory handling.
- Product catalogue treatment.
- Data export and handover.
- Integration shutdown.
- Access revocation.
- Historical record preservation.
- Final verification.

The plan must identify dependencies and actions that must occur before deactivation.

## 9. Storefront Deactivation

The store storefront must be deactivated according to the approved exit plan.

Actions may include:

- Preventing new purchases.
- Updating storefront availability.
- Removing active promotional placements.
- Disabling checkout.
- Updating store status.
- Displaying an approved customer notice.
- Preserving historical order access for authorised users.

The platform must prevent new orders from being accepted after the effective shutdown point.

Existing orders must continue through an approved completion, cancellation or resolution process.

## 10. Outstanding Orders

Every open order must receive an explicit disposition.

Possible outcomes include:

- Fulfilment completed.
- Fulfilment transferred through an authorised arrangement.
- Order cancelled.
- Refund initiated.
- Customer resolution pending.

Each outcome must preserve the original order and transaction history.

A store must not abandon customer commitments merely because its storefront has been deactivated.

## 11. Payments and Settlements

Payment and settlement records must be reconciled before final closure where possible.

Review:

- Uncaptured payments.
- Pending provider transactions.
- Unsettled payments.
- Refunds.
- Reversals.
- Chargebacks.
- Provider fees.
- Bank receipts.
- Outstanding amounts.

Uncertain provider outcomes must be investigated before retrying or closing the related transaction.

## 12. Financial Obligations

Identify all outstanding financial obligations involving the store and platform.

These may include:

- Platform fees or agreed commissions.
- Supplier obligations.
- Approved capital advances.
- Stock assistance.
- Repayment obligations.
- Refund liabilities.
- Provider fees.
- Approved expense reimbursements.
- Disputed balances.

The exit process must not silently waive, transfer or settle obligations without appropriate authority and supporting agreements.

## 13. Final Financial Reconciliation

Prepare a final financial reconciliation covering the relevant exit period.

The reconciliation must distinguish:

- Confirmed balances.
- Pending settlements.
- Outstanding refunds.
- Disputed amounts.
- Approved adjustments.
- Amounts owed to the store.
- Amounts owed by the store.
- Unresolved obligations.

The final reconciliation must be reviewed and approved through the applicable financial controls.

Unresolved items must remain recorded with assigned owners and follow-up arrangements.

## 14. Inventory and Stock Assistance

Inventory must be reviewed independently of storefront deactivation.

Identify:

- Store-owned stock.
- Platform-owned stock.
- Supplier-owned stock.
- Stock supplied under assistance arrangements.
- Reserved stock.
- Damaged or disputed stock.
- Stock held at external locations.

Any return, transfer, sale or other disposition must follow ownership rights and applicable agreements.

Inventory movements must be recorded with references, quantities, dates and responsible parties.

## 15. Product Catalogue Treatment

Store exit must not automatically delete shared catalogue products.

Product treatment depends on ownership and other stores' legitimate availability.

The process must distinguish:

- Platform-owned source products.
- Store-owned source products.
- Shared products available to other stores.
- Store-specific listings.
- Store-specific prices and commercial terms.
- Store-specific product media.

The exiting store's listing may be deactivated without deleting a source product used by other stores.

Store-owned product information must not be transferred to another store without appropriate authority.

## 16. Customer Information

Customer information must be handled according to the applicable data governance and privacy requirements.

The exit assessment must determine:

- Which customer records are store-scoped.
- Which records are needed for open orders.
- Which information must be retained.
- Which information may be exported.
- Which information must be deleted or anonymised.
- Which records are subject to legal holds.
- Which information remains necessary for platform operations.

The exiting store must not receive unrestricted customer information belonging to other stores.

Customer data must not be retained or shared merely because it is technically available.

## 17. Employee and User Access

Review all users associated with the exiting store.

Actions may include:

- Revoking store membership.
- Disabling employee accounts where appropriate.
- Removing store-specific permissions.
- Revoking temporary support access.
- Revoking developer access.
- Reviewing privileged accounts.
- Preserving required audit records.

An individual account may remain valid for other legitimate platform responsibilities, but its access to the exiting store must be removed.

## 18. API Credentials and Integrations

All store-specific integration credentials must be reviewed.

Actions may include:

- Revoking store-scoped public identifiers where supported.
- Revoking private API credentials.
- Disabling webhooks.
- Removing provider account mappings.
- Disabling scheduled integrations.
- Revoking third-party access.
- Rotating shared credentials where exposure is suspected.
- Disabling obsolete API access.

Private credentials must remain protected throughout the exit process.

Shared platform integrations must not be disabled if doing so would disrupt other stores.

## 19. Storefront, Domain and Hosting Handover

Storefront ownership and domain arrangements must be reviewed against the applicable agreements.

The handover plan should identify:

- Storefront source code ownership.
- Hosting account ownership.
- Domain registration ownership.
- DNS administration.
- Deployment credentials.
- Environment configuration.
- Analytics and marketing accounts.
- Third-party service dependencies.

Where a domain or hosting account belongs to the store, transfer or continued ownership must follow the applicable agreement.

Platform-owned infrastructure must not be transferred without explicit approval.

## 20. Data Export and Handover

An authorised store representative may request an export of eligible store data.

The request must identify:

- Requesting representative.
- Authority verification.
- Data categories.
- Relevant date range.
- Intended purpose.
- Required format.
- Delivery method.

Exports must be limited to information the store is entitled to receive.

Exclude or appropriately protect:

- Other stores' information.
- Platform confidential information.
- Privileged credentials.
- Personal information without an appropriate basis.
- Records restricted by legal hold or other obligations.

Export activity must be logged.

## 21. Data Preservation and Retention

Historical records must remain subject to approved retention schedules.

Preserve relevant:

- Orders.
- Payments.
- Refunds.
- Financial records.
- Reconciliation evidence.
- Audit records.
- Customer commitments.
- Dispute records.
- Assistance agreements.
- Inventory movements.
- Approval records.

Store deactivation must not bypass retention requirements.

Deletion or anonymisation must follow the Data Governance Policy and applicable legal advice.

## 22. External Provider Closure

Review provider relationships connected to the store.

These may include:

- Payment providers.
- Email and SMS services.
- Analytics platforms.
- Advertising platforms.
- Shipping and fulfilment services.
- Storage providers.
- Accounting systems.
- Domain and hosting services.

Confirm which accounts belong to the platform and which belong to the store.

Do not close a shared provider account if other stores depend on it.

## 23. Security and Risk Review

Before final closure, assess:

- Remaining access paths.
- Active credentials.
- Outstanding provider callbacks.
- Pending background jobs.
- Unresolved security incidents.
- Data export exposure.
- Domain and DNS control.
- Shared infrastructure dependencies.
- Financial and contractual disputes.

Preserve evidence and apply authorised protective measures where necessary.

## 24. Final Store Deactivation

Final deactivation may proceed when:

- Exit authority is verified.
- The exit plan is approved.
- New purchases are disabled.
- Open orders have documented outcomes.
- Financial reconciliation is completed or outstanding items are formally assigned.
- Inventory ownership and disposition are documented.
- Customer data responsibilities are established.
- User access is reviewed.
- Integrations are disabled or transferred.
- Required data exports are completed.
- Historical records are preserved.
- Remaining obligations are documented.

Where an unresolved matter prevents final closure, record the exception and obtain approval for the appropriate restricted or provisional status.

## 25. Post-Exit Monitoring

After deactivation, monitor for:

- Unexpected API requests.
- Provider callbacks.
- Delayed settlements.
- Refund outcomes.
- Chargebacks.
- Unresolved customer complaints.
- Outstanding obligations.
- Attempts to use revoked credentials.
- Data restoration or migration issues.

Assign responsibility for each outstanding item.

## 26. Exit Completion Record

The final exit record must contain:

- Store identity.
- Exit type.
- Approval authority.
- Effective exit date.
- Exit coordinator.
- Order disposition summary.
- Financial reconciliation status.
- Inventory disposition.
- Product catalogue treatment.
- Data export details.
- Retention and legal hold status.
- Access revocation status.
- Integration and provider status.
- Outstanding obligations.
- Exceptions.
- Final verification.
- Closure approval.

## 27. Exceptions and Disputes

Disputes involving ownership, finances, customer data, intellectual property or contractual obligations must be escalated through the applicable governance and dispute-resolution processes.

The platform must preserve relevant records while the matter remains unresolved.

An exit must not be used to avoid an existing investigation or obligation.

## 28. Open Decisions

The following require formal definition:

- Store exit notice requirements.
- Exit approval authority.
- Contractual termination conditions.
- Financial settlement deadlines.
- Data export formats and delivery arrangements.
- Storefront and domain transfer rules.
- Treatment of store-owned product information.
- Post-exit support responsibilities.
- Provider account closure responsibilities.
- Final retention and deletion schedules.
- Outstanding obligation management.
- Legal and accounting review requirements.
