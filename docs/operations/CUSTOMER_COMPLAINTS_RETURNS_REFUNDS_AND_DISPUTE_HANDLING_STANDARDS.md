## 1. Purpose

This document establishes a consistent framework for handling customer complaints, returns, refunds, exchanges and disputes across all participating stores.

It protects customers, gives Store Owners clear operational responsibilities and ensures that the parent company can monitor service quality without taking ownership of each Store Owner's independent brand identity.

These standards must be implemented in accordance with applicable South African consumer protection, e-commerce, privacy and payment laws.

## 2. Core Principles

All participating stores must:

1. Treat customers fairly and respectfully.
2. Provide accurate product descriptions and pricing.
3. Honour applicable consumer rights.
4. Publish clear delivery, return, refund and cancellation information.
5. Investigate complaints objectively.
6. Keep records of complaints and their outcomes.
7. Avoid misleading customers about their rights.
8. Protect customer information throughout the process.
9. Escalate serious, repeated or unresolved disputes.
10. Never use store policies to remove rights provided by law.

The platform must make it possible to identify which store is responsible for a transaction and which Store Owner is responsible for handling the associated operational matter.

## 3. Responsibility Structure

### 3.1 Store Owner Responsibilities

The Store Owner is responsible for the day-to-day handling of customer matters relating to their store, including:

- Product-related complaints.
- Incorrect or damaged products.
- Product quality concerns.
- Order preparation and fulfilment.
- Store-specific delivery arrangements.
- Exchanges and returns.
- Refund requests.
- Customer communication.
- Resolution of store-specific service disputes.

The Store Owner must follow the platform's approved procedures and applicable law.

### 3.2 Parent Company Responsibilities

The parent company is responsible for:

- Providing the platform and complaint-management functionality.
- Maintaining appropriate transaction and support records.
- Providing documented operating procedures.
- Monitoring compliance with platform standards.
- Escalating serious or repeated problems.
- Supporting payment reconciliation where the platform is involved.
- Maintaining platform-level security and privacy controls.
- Addressing defects in parent-company infrastructure.
- Suspending or restricting activity where required to protect customers or the platform.

The parent company must not misrepresent itself as the seller where the Store Owner is the actual seller.

The legal seller, contracting party and responsible entity must be clearly identified in the applicable storefront terms and transaction records.

## 4. Customer-Facing Information

Each storefront must make the following information reasonably accessible:

- Legal seller or responsible business identity.
- Contact details for customer support.
- Product descriptions and material characteristics.
- Total price and applicable additional charges.
- Delivery methods and estimated delivery periods.
- Cancellation terms.
- Return and refund procedures.
- Applicable warranty information.
- Privacy information.
- Complaint submission procedure.
- Any relevant product restrictions or conditions.

The information must be understandable and must not contradict applicable consumer law.

## 5. Complaint Categories

Complaints should be classified using consistent categories.

### 5.1 Product Complaints

Examples:
- Product differs materially from its description.
- Product is defective.
- Product is damaged.
- Incorrect product supplied.
- Product authenticity or quality concern.
- Missing components or accessories.

### 5.2 Order Complaints

Examples:
- Order not received.
- Incorrect quantity.
- Order status is inaccurate.
- Order was cancelled unexpectedly.
- Duplicate order or charge.
- Fulfilment delay.

### 5.3 Delivery Complaints

Examples:
- Delivery delayed.
- Parcel damaged in transit.
- Parcel delivered to the wrong address.
- Missing parcel.
- Delivery fee dispute.
- Courier conduct complaint.

### 5.4 Payment Complaints

Examples:
- Payment made but not recognised.
- Duplicate payment.
- Incorrect amount charged.
- Refund not received.
- Payment status incorrectly displayed.

### 5.5 Service Complaints

Examples:
- Poor customer communication.
- Unreasonable handling of a return.
- Misleading product information.
- Unprofessional conduct.
- Failure to follow an agreed resolution.

## 6. Complaint Submission and Recording

Customers must have a clear way to submit complaints.

A complaint record should include:

- Unique complaint reference.
- Store identifier.
- Order reference, where applicable.
- Customer identifier.
- Complaint category.
- Date and time received.
- Customer's description.
- Relevant product information.
- Supporting photographs or documents.
- Assigned responsible person.
- Priority and status.
- Communication history.
- Investigation findings.
- Proposed resolution.
- Final outcome.
- Closure date.

Customers should receive acknowledgement that their complaint has been received.

The system must not require customers to disclose unnecessary personal information to submit a complaint.

## 7. Complaint Priority

### Critical

Examples:
- Suspected fraud.
- Product safety concern.
- Serious privacy or security incident.
- Multiple customers affected by the same material defect.
- Suspected unlawful activity.

Action:
- Escalate immediately.
- Preserve relevant evidence.
- Restrict affected activity where reasonably necessary.
- Notify the appropriate responsible parties.
- Follow applicable legal reporting requirements.

### High

Examples:
- Customer paid but has not received an order.
- Significant product defect.
- Incorrect or duplicate charge.
- Repeated failure to honour a valid return or refund.
- Material delivery failure.

Action:
- Prioritise investigation.
- Keep the customer informed.
- Escalate unresolved matters.

### Normal

Examples:
- Routine return request.
- Product exchange request.
- Minor order discrepancy.
- Delivery status clarification.

Action:
- Handle through the normal complaint workflow.
- Record the outcome.

### Low

Examples:
- General product questions.
- Non-urgent feedback.
- Suggestions for improvement.

Action:
- Respond through normal customer support.

## 8. Complaint Handling Workflow

The standard workflow is:

1. Receive the complaint.
2. Create or update the complaint record.
3. Acknowledge receipt.
4. Identify the responsible store and seller.
5. Classify the complaint.
6. Review the order, payment and fulfilment records.
7. Request reasonable additional evidence if required.
8. Determine the applicable legal and contractual position.
9. Propose an appropriate resolution.
10. Obtain any required approval.
11. Communicate the decision.
12. Execute the agreed resolution.
13. Record the outcome.
14. Close the complaint after completion or documented escalation.

A complaint must not be marked resolved merely because a response was sent.

## 9. Returns and Exchanges

Each store must publish a return and exchange policy that is consistent with applicable law.

The policy must distinguish:

- Statutory cancellation rights.
- Returns for defective or unsafe goods.
- Goods that differ from their description.
- Voluntary change-of-mind returns.
- Exchanges offered as a commercial option.
- Product-specific hygiene or safety restrictions.
- Special-order or personalised products, where legally relevant.

A store's voluntary return policy must not remove statutory rights.

### 9.1 Return Request Information

A return request should capture:

- Order reference.
- Product and quantity.
- Reason for return.
- Date the customer received the product, where relevant.
- Condition of the product.
- Supporting evidence, where appropriate.
- Requested outcome.

### 9.2 Return Authorisation

Where a return authorisation process is used, it must be simple and must not create an unlawful barrier to exercising consumer rights.

Customers should receive clear instructions about:

- Where to send or deliver the product.
- Packaging requirements.
- Collection arrangements, if available.
- Who is responsible for return costs.
- Expected next steps.

### 9.3 Inspection

Returned goods may be inspected where reasonably necessary.

Inspection must not be used to unfairly deny a valid statutory remedy.

The inspection record should include:
- Date received.
- Product condition.
- Missing items or accessories.
- Photographs where useful.
- Inspection outcome.
- Recommended resolution.

## 10. Refund Standards

Refunds must be handled accurately, transparently and within applicable legal timeframes.

The responsible seller must determine whether a refund is required under the circumstances.

Refund records should include:

- Original order reference.
- Original payment reference.
- Refund reason.
- Approved amount.
- Approval date.
- Refund method.
- Processing date.
- Payment-provider reference, where available.
- Final refund status.

Where a payment provider is involved, the platform must distinguish between:

- Refund requested.
- Refund approved.
- Refund submitted to provider.
- Refund processing.
- Refund completed.
- Refund failed.

A refund must not be marked completed solely because an internal administrator approved it.

The customer must be informed if processing depends on a bank or payment provider.

## 11. South African Consumer Protection Requirements

The operating procedures and customer-facing terms must be reviewed against applicable South African legislation.

Relevant legislation includes:

- Consumer Protection Act 68 of 2008.
- Electronic Communications and Transactions Act 25 of 2002.
- Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013.
- Applicable payment, tax and sector-specific legislation.

The Consumer Protection Act includes requirements concerning quality, warranties, returns, refunds and cancellation rights.

The Electronic Communications and Transactions Act provides additional rules for certain electronic transactions, including cooling-off rights and supplier disclosure requirements.

The precise application of these rights depends on the transaction, product, seller and relevant statutory exceptions.

The platform must not assume that every transaction has the same cancellation period or that every product can be returned under identical conditions.

### 11.1 Legal Review

Before launch, a South African consumer-law practitioner should review:

- Storefront terms and conditions.
- Return and refund policies.
- Cancellation workflows.
- Product-specific restrictions.
- Seller identification.
- Distance-selling disclosures.
- Complaint escalation procedures.
- Customer communication templates.

Store Owners must receive approved policy templates and guidance for adapting store-specific details without contradicting mandatory legal requirements.

## 12. Product-Specific Considerations

Different product categories may require different operational handling.

Examples include:

### Fragrances and Personal-Care Products

Consider:
- Product integrity.
- Packaging condition.
- Leakage or damage.
- Authenticity.
- Hygiene and safety considerations.
- Whether the product has been opened or used.

Any restriction on returns must be legally reviewed and must not override applicable rights for defective, unsafe or incorrectly described goods.

### Clothing and Accessories

Consider:
- Correct size and colour.
- Product description accuracy.
- Manufacturing defects.
- Missing components.
- Condition and evidence of use.
- Applicable change-of-mind rights.

### Custom or Personalised Products

Consider:
- Approved specifications.
- Customer-supplied information.
- Proof or design approval.
- Production defects.
- Differences between approved and delivered work.

Personalisation does not automatically remove all consumer rights.

## 13. Delivery and Fulfilment Disputes

For delivery-related complaints, review:

- Order confirmation.
- Dispatch date.
- Courier or delivery provider.
- Tracking information.
- Delivery address.
- Proof of delivery.
- Customer communication.
- Reported damage or loss.

The responsible seller must investigate the matter and communicate the outcome.

The Store Owner must not simply redirect every customer to a courier without taking responsibility for the seller's obligations.

Where a third-party delivery provider is responsible for an operational failure, the Store Owner may pursue the provider separately while continuing to address the customer's rights.

## 14. Payment Disputes and Chargebacks

Payment disputes must be handled using verified transaction records.

The responsible team should review:

- Payment provider reference.
- Order reference.
- Amount paid.
- Payment status.
- Settlement records.
- Refund history.
- Customer communication.
- Delivery or collection evidence.
- Dispute deadlines.

Store Owners must not mark an order paid based only on an unverified screenshot or customer statement.

Where a chargeback or payment dispute occurs, preserve relevant evidence and follow the payment provider's process.

A customer complaint and a payment-provider dispute must be tracked separately but linked where they relate to the same transaction.

## 15. Escalation and Dispute Resolution

A complaint must be escalated when:

- The Store Owner cannot resolve it.
- The customer disputes the proposed outcome.
- The matter involves possible fraud.
- The issue may affect multiple customers.
- A safety concern is raised.
- A privacy or security incident is suspected.
- A statutory deadline may be at risk.
- The complaint involves significant financial or reputational exposure.

The parent company may review the handling of a complaint to ensure platform standards are followed.

Where a dispute cannot be resolved internally, the customer must be informed of applicable external complaint or dispute-resolution channels.

Depending on the matter, these may include the National Consumer Commission, an applicable accredited industry ombud, the National Consumer Tribunal or a court with jurisdiction.

The correct forum depends on the nature of the complaint and the applicable legal process.

## 16. Customer Communication Standards

Customer communication must be:

- Respectful.
- Clear.
- Accurate.
- Timely.
- Specific to the complaint.
- Free from intimidation or retaliation.
- Consistent with applicable law.

A response should explain:
- What was reviewed.
- What was established.
- What decision was made.
- What happens next.
- Any action required from the customer.
- Relevant timeframes.

Store Owners must not:
- Blame customers without evidence.
- Make misleading legal claims.
- Threaten customers for raising complaints.
- Publicly expose customer information.
- Promise refunds or replacements that have not been authorised.
- Delete complaint records to conceal poor service.

## 17. Complaint Trends and Quality Monitoring

The parent company should periodically review complaint data to identify:

- Products generating repeated complaints.
- Frequent delivery problems.
- Recurring payment disputes.
- Product-description inaccuracies.
- Store-specific service issues.
- Supplier quality concerns.
- Defects affecting multiple stores.
- Repeated failure to meet customer expectations.

Where a pattern is identified, corrective action may include:
- Product listing review.
- Supplier investigation.
- Additional Store Owner training.
- Fulfilment process changes.
- Product suspension.
- Platform improvements.
- Customer notification where appropriate.

Performance comparisons between stores must account for order volume, product category and operating history.

## 18. Record Retention and Privacy

Complaint, return, refund and dispute records must be retained according to the platform's approved retention schedule and applicable legal requirements.

Access must be restricted according to role and business need.

Records must:
- Be accurate.
- Be protected against unauthorised modification.
- Have an identifiable audit history.
- Avoid unnecessary duplication of personal information.
- Be deleted or anonymised when retention is no longer justified, subject to legal obligations.

A Store Owner must not have unrestricted access to another store's complaints, orders or customer records.

## 19. Prohibited Practices

The following practices are prohibited:

- Misrepresenting statutory consumer rights.
- Refusing a valid remedy solely because a store policy says otherwise.
- Deliberately delaying refunds.
- Falsifying delivery or payment evidence.
- Deleting complaint records to avoid accountability.
- Retaliating against customers for complaints.
- Publishing customer personal information without lawful authority.
- Misrepresenting the legal seller.
- Making unsupported claims about product safety or authenticity.
- Treating every customer dispute as a chargeback or fraud attempt.

Serious or repeated violations may result in corrective action under the Store Owner Agreement.

## 20. Implementation Requirements

Before this standard becomes operational, the parent company must:

1. Approve standard complaint categories.
2. Configure complaint and return records.
3. Define Store Owner and support-team permissions.
4. Publish approved customer-facing policy templates.
5. Confirm refund approval responsibilities.
6. Integrate refund status tracking where supported.
7. Define escalation ownership.
8. Establish complaint reporting.
9. Train Store Owners on the procedures.
10. Obtain South African legal review of customer-facing policies.
