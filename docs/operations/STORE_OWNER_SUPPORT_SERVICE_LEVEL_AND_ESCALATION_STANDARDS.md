# Store Owner Support, Service Levels and Escalation Standards

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes how the parent company provides operational, technical and business-platform support to Store Owners.

It defines:
- Support channels and responsibilities.
- Issue classification and priority levels.
- Response and resolution targets.
- Escalation procedures.
- Incident communication.
- Support records and accountability.
- Boundaries between included support and optional paid services.

The objective is to give every Store Owner a clear, predictable support experience while allowing the parent company to operate the platform sustainably.

## 2. Support Principles

Support must follow these principles:

1. Every Store Owner must have access to a documented support process.
2. Issues must be acknowledged, classified and tracked.
3. Critical platform incidents take priority over routine requests.
4. Store Owners must receive understandable updates.
5. Support decisions must be consistent and documented.
6. Store-specific problems must be distinguished from platform-wide problems.
7. Support must not expose another store's confidential information.
8. Support must not promise a resolution time that cannot reasonably be met.
9. Repeated incidents must lead to investigation and corrective action.
10. Commercially excluded services must be identified before work begins.

## 3. Support Categories

### 3.1 Platform and Technical Support

Includes assistance with:
- Storefront availability.
- Storefront errors.
- Product display problems.
- Product publishing issues.
- Checkout and order-processing problems.
- Customer account issues.
- Payment integration issues.
- Dashboard access.
- Supported store configuration.
- Platform-generated notifications.
- Security-related concerns.
- Performance and loading problems.

### 3.2 Store Operations Support

Includes guidance on:
- Product management.
- Order handling.
- Inventory procedures.
- Manual payment verification.
- Fulfilment workflows.
- Customer communication features.
- Store reporting.
- Supported dashboard functions.

### 3.3 Creative Production Support

Includes coordination of approved creative production:
- Product photography.
- Product video.
- Image preparation.
- Media replacement.
- Storefront visual quality checks.
- Technical media requirements.

Production quantities, turnaround times and revision allowances must follow the applicable subscription, production allowance or separate quotation.

### 3.4 Marketing Support

Includes support for agreed marketing activities:
- Campaign coordination.
- Campaign approval.
- Content scheduling.
- Advertising coordination.
- Campaign reporting.
- Brand presentation.

Marketing strategy, campaign management, paid advertising and additional creative work are not automatically included in core technical support.

### 3.5 Commercial and Account Support

Includes:
- Subscription questions.
- Invoices and payment records.
- Plan changes.
- Service quotations.
- Transaction-fee questions, where applicable.
- Agreement and renewal queries.
- Store Owner assignment changes.

## 4. Support Channels

The parent company must publish its approved support channels.

Possible channels include:
- A support function within the Store Owner dashboard.
- An official support email address.
- An approved business messaging channel.
- A documented emergency contact route for critical incidents.

The parent company must identify:
- Which channels are monitored.
- Operating hours.
- How critical incidents should be reported.
- What information Store Owners should include.
- Which channels are suitable for confidential information.

Personal employee contact details must not become the default support infrastructure.

## 5. Support Ticket Requirements

Each support request should create a traceable record.

A support record should include:

- Unique ticket reference.
- Store identifier.
- Store Owner identifier.
- Date and time received.
- Reporting channel.
- Issue category.
- Description of the problem.
- Affected functionality.
- Screenshots or relevant evidence, where available.
- Severity and priority.
- Assigned support person or team.
- Current status.
- Actions taken.
- Communication history.
- Resolution details.
- Closure date.
- Store Owner confirmation or closure reason.

Sensitive information must be handled according to the platform's privacy and security standards.

Store Owners must never be asked to submit passwords, authentication codes or full payment-card details through ordinary support channels.

## 6. Priority Classification

Every issue must be classified according to business impact, urgency and the number of affected users or stores.

### P1: Critical

Examples:
- Platform-wide storefront outage.
- Checkout unavailable across multiple stores.
- Confirmed security compromise.
- Unauthorised access to customer or business data.
- Major payment-processing failure affecting multiple stores.
- Data corruption or loss affecting essential operations.

Expected handling:
- Immediate triage during monitored support hours.
- Escalation to the responsible technical or security lead.
- Regular incident updates.
- Workaround or mitigation prioritised.
- Root-cause investigation after service restoration.

### P2: High

Examples:
- One store's storefront is unavailable.
- A major store-specific checkout failure.
- Orders cannot be processed through an essential workflow.
- Dashboard functionality prevents essential operations.
- A payment integration problem affects a specific store.

Expected handling:
- Prioritised investigation.
- Escalation where the issue requires specialist intervention.
- Regular progress updates.
- A workaround considered where a permanent fix is not immediately available.

### P3: Normal

Examples:
- Product images displaying incorrectly.
- A non-critical dashboard feature malfunction.
- Product publishing errors with an available workaround.
- Reporting discrepancies that do not affect financial settlement.
- Store configuration problems.

Expected handling:
- Scheduled investigation.
- Progress updates when meaningful.
- Resolution through the normal support or development workflow.

### P4: Low Priority

Examples:
- General usage questions.
- Minor visual inconsistencies.
- Non-urgent content adjustments.
- Feature suggestions.
- Requests for additional functionality.

Expected handling:
- Guidance or review through the normal support process.
- Feature requests evaluated separately from defect reports.
- No automatic commitment to implementation.

## 7. Proposed Service-Level Targets

The following are proposed initial targets, not existing guarantees. They must be approved against actual staffing, operating hours, infrastructure and support capacity before being published in a Store Owner Agreement.

| Priority | Initial response target | Target for next meaningful update |
|---|---:|---:|
| P1 Critical | Within 1 monitored support hour | Within 2 support hours |
| P2 High | Within 4 support hours | Within 1 business day |
| P3 Normal | Within 1 business day | Within 2 business days |
| P4 Low | Within 2 business days | Within 5 business days |

These targets measure acknowledgement and communication, not guaranteed resolution.

A response should:
- Confirm that the request was received.
- Provide a ticket reference.
- Confirm the assigned priority.
- Explain the next action.
- Identify any information still required.

Resolution estimates must be communicated separately and updated when circumstances change.

## 8. Support Hours and Availability

The parent company must publish its normal support hours and applicable public-holiday arrangements.

The following must be distinguished:

- Support hours: when routine requests are monitored.
- Incident monitoring: when critical incidents can be detected or escalated.
- Platform availability: whether the storefront infrastructure is operational.
- Development availability: when planned engineering work is scheduled.

A platform operating continuously does not automatically mean that human support is available continuously.

If the parent company does not operate a 24-hour support team, this limitation must be communicated clearly.

Critical incident procedures should identify the available escalation route outside ordinary support hours, if one is provided.

## 9. Incident Escalation

### 9.1 First-Level Support

Responsible for:
- Receiving requests.
- Creating support records.
- Gathering evidence.
- Classifying issues.
- Resolving documented routine problems.
- Communicating progress.

### 9.2 Technical Escalation

Required when an issue involves:
- Application defects.
- Database problems.
- Infrastructure failures.
- Deployment problems.
- Payment integration failures.
- Security vulnerabilities.
- Data integrity concerns.

The technical team must document investigation findings and any corrective changes.

### 9.3 Security Escalation

Security incidents must follow the security incident response procedure.

Examples include:
- Suspected account compromise.
- Unauthorised data access.
- Exposure of confidential information.
- Suspicious administrative activity.
- Vulnerability reports.
- Malware or malicious activity.

Security incidents must not be treated as ordinary feature requests.

### 9.4 Management Escalation

Management involvement is required where:
- A serious incident remains unresolved.
- A Store Owner disputes a material support decision.
- An incident creates significant financial or reputational exposure.
- A service commitment cannot be met.
- A commercial or contractual dispute arises.
- A decision requires an exception to established policy.

## 10. Incident Communication

During significant incidents, the parent company should communicate:

1. What is affected.
2. Which stores or functions are affected, where known.
3. What customers or Store Owners may experience.
4. Whether a workaround exists.
5. What action is being taken.
6. When the next update is expected.
7. When normal service has been restored.

Communication must distinguish confirmed facts from preliminary investigation findings.

The parent company must not disclose:
- Another store's private information.
- Internal credentials or security-sensitive details.
- Confidential infrastructure information.
- Personal information unrelated to the incident.

After a significant incident, a post-incident report should record:
- Incident timeline.
- Root cause, where established.
- Scope and impact.
- Mitigation and recovery actions.
- Preventive measures.
- Outstanding work.

## 11. Feature Requests and Store Change Requests

A feature request is not automatically a defect.

Store Owners may submit requests for:
- Additional storefront sections.
- Layout improvements.
- New supported configuration options.
- Product presentation changes.
- Dashboard improvements.
- Additional reports.
- New integrations.
- Operational workflow improvements.

Requests must follow the Store Change Request process.

Each request should be evaluated for:
- Business value.
- Store Owner benefit.
- Security implications.
- Accessibility.
- Performance.
- Technical feasibility.
- Compatibility with other stores.
- Maintenance cost.
- Whether the feature should be shared or store-specific.

The parent company must record whether a request is:
- Approved.
- Rejected.
- Deferred.
- Requiring clarification.
- Requiring a separate quotation.

A rejection should include a reasonable explanation.

## 12. Support Boundaries

Core subscription support must be clearly defined.

It may include:
- Assistance with supported platform functionality.
- Routine technical troubleshooting.
- Basic product and order workflow guidance.
- Standard incident handling.
- Access and account assistance.
- Defined maintenance and platform updates.

It does not automatically include:
- Unlimited custom development.
- Unlimited product uploads.
- Unlimited photography or video production.
- Unlimited graphic design.
- Full social media management.
- Paid advertising budgets.
- Professional legal or tax advice.
- Third-party service charges.
- Custom integrations outside the agreed scope.
- Work caused by unauthorised modifications or misuse.

Additional work must be scoped and priced before it begins.

The parent company must not use support boundaries to avoid responsibility for defects or failures in services it has agreed to provide.

## 13. Store Owner Responsibilities

Store Owners are expected to:
- Use the published support channels.
- Provide accurate information.
- Include relevant screenshots or examples.
- Report suspected security issues promptly.
- Avoid sharing passwords or authentication codes.
- Cooperate with reasonable troubleshooting.
- Keep their contact details current.
- Follow documented platform procedures.
- Avoid repeatedly submitting the same issue through unrelated channels unless escalation is necessary.

Store Owners must not be penalised for reporting legitimate defects or security concerns.

## 14. Service Credits and Remedies

The parent company must define any service credits, refunds or other remedies in the applicable agreement.

No service credit should be assumed unless the contract explicitly provides for it.

Where the parent company materially fails to provide an agreed service, the issue must be assessed under the applicable agreement and relevant law.

Any remedy must distinguish:
- Platform outage.
- Third-party provider failure.
- Scheduled maintenance.
- Store Owner configuration or misuse.
- Events outside reasonable control.
- Failure of an optional separately contracted service.

## 15. Support Performance Reporting

The parent company should periodically review:

- Tickets received.
- Tickets by priority.
- First-response performance.
- Resolution time.
- Reopened tickets.
- Repeat incidents.
- Escalation frequency.
- Store Owner satisfaction.
- Common product or workflow problems.
- Support cost per store.
- Support workload by category.

Reports should help identify systemic problems rather than merely measure ticket volume.

Store-specific reports must not reveal another Store Owner's confidential information.

## 16. Knowledge Base and Self-Service

The parent company should maintain a knowledge base covering:

- Store Owner dashboard usage.
- Product creation and publishing.
- Product image requirements.
- Order processing.
- Inventory management.
- Payment verification.
- Fulfilment.
- Customer communication.
- Store settings.
- Common errors.
- Security and account practices.

Articles must be:
- Accurate.
- Version-controlled.
- Reviewed when platform functionality changes.
- Written in clear language.
- Accessible to Store Owners with different levels of technical experience.

Self-service documentation supplements human support. It must not be used as a substitute for handling genuine incidents.

## 17. Support Access and Auditability

Support personnel must receive only the access necessary to investigate and resolve an issue.

Where support requires access to a Store Owner's data or dashboard:
- The purpose must be legitimate.
- Access must be authorised under the platform's access-control rules.
- Sensitive actions must be logged.
- Support personnel must not browse unrelated customer or business information.
- Temporary elevated access must be removed when no longer required.

Support activity must comply with the platform's access-control, privacy and security standards.

## 18. Review and Continuous Improvement

This policy must be reviewed:
- When support staffing changes.
- When subscription plans change.
- When new support channels are introduced.
- When platform architecture changes materially.
- After serious incidents.
- When service-level performance repeatedly misses targets.
- At least annually.

Service-level targets must be adjusted to match actual operational capacity.

The parent company must not advertise support guarantees that it cannot consistently deliver.

- Support channels.
- Staffing and escalation ownership.
- Final response targets.
- Included support scope.
- Optional service boundaries.
- Any contractual service credits or remedies.

All approved changes must be reflected in the Store Owner Agreement and applicable service schedules.
