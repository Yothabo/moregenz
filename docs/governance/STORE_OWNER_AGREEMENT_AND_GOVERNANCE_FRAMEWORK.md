# Store Owner Agreement and Governance Framework

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the governance framework for the relationship between the parent company and each Store Owner.

It defines the principles, rights, responsibilities, authority boundaries and procedures that should form the basis of the formal Store Owner Agreement.

The objective is to create a relationship in which Store Owners have meaningful control over their assigned store identities and day-to-day business operations, while the parent company retains responsibility for the shared platform, infrastructure, security, central governance and overall business structure.

This framework is an internal policy and contract-design standard. It is not a substitute for a legally reviewed agreement.

## 2. Nature of the Relationship

### 2.1 The parent company

The parent company operates the central business platform and provides participating Store Owners with the infrastructure and services required to establish and operate digital storefronts.

Its responsibilities include:

- Owning and governing the shared platform and infrastructure.
- Maintaining the central software and technical environment.
- Managing platform security and access controls.
- Establishing shared operational standards.
- Providing agreed hosting, maintenance and support.
- Coordinating central creative production.
- Operating the shared marketing and growth function.
- Managing platform-level financial and administrative controls.
- Maintaining central governance and compliance procedures.
- Approving platform-wide changes and shared capabilities.

### 2.2 The Store Owner

A Store Owner is the person or approved business entity assigned to operate a participating store.

The Store Owner has meaningful authority over the identity, positioning and commercial direction of that assigned store, subject to the agreement and platform-wide requirements.

Their responsibilities may include:

- Developing and maintaining the store's brand identity.
- Selecting products and determining the store's product direction.
- Setting product prices within applicable platform and legal requirements.
- Providing accurate product and business information.
- Directing merchandising and product presentation.
- Participating in creative decisions.
- Approving agreed marketing campaigns.
- Managing day-to-day store operations.
- Monitoring orders, customer service and fulfilment.
- Maintaining product quality and business standards.
- Communicating operational issues and improvement requests.
- Meeting agreed subscription and service obligations.

### 2.3 Distinction between store operation and platform ownership

The Store Owner operates and develops the assigned store as their business identity within the parent company's structure.

This operational and brand authority does not automatically transfer ownership of the parent company's platform, software, infrastructure, central systems or other shared assets.

The parent company must not use this distinction to misrepresent the Store Owner as having no meaningful rights or control over their store.

The agreement must clearly identify which rights belong to the Store Owner, which assets belong to the parent company and which rights are licensed or shared.

## 3. Store Owner Appointment

### 3.1 Appointment process

A person or business may become a Store Owner after completing the applicable admission, verification and onboarding process.

The process should establish:

- The applicant's identity.
- Their authority to operate the proposed business.
- The business or venture being represented.
- The proposed products or services.
- The proposed brand identity.
- Relevant trading or business-development experience.
- Operational capacity.
- Product sourcing and fulfilment arrangements.
- Applicable legal and regulatory requirements.
- Agreement to platform standards.
- Acceptance of the commercial arrangement.

Admission must follow the parent company's documented Store Owner recruitment and assessment policy.

### 3.2 Different business stages

The parent company may appoint Store Owners from different business stages:

1. Existing informal businesses.
2. Existing registered businesses.
3. New ventures and idea-stage founders.

The absence of company registration, historical revenue or an established social media following must not automatically disqualify an otherwise suitable applicant.

The admission process must evaluate applicants according to their actual stage of development.

### 3.3 Appointment records

Each appointment must be recorded with:

- Store ID.
- Store Owner identity.
- Appointment date.
- Agreement version.
- Subscription plan.
- Trial status, where applicable.
- Approved business and product categories.
- Assigned permissions.
- Approved brand identity.
- Relevant compliance records.
- Appointment status.
- Review dates.

The appointment record must remain part of the store's governance history.

## 4. Store Owner Authority

The Store Owner should have authority over the commercial and brand direction of their assigned store.

Subject to platform capabilities and applicable policies, this includes:

### 4.1 Brand identity

- Store name, subject to approval and availability.
- Logo and visual identity.
- Brand colours.
- Brand voice and messaging.
- Brand positioning.
- Target audience.
- Brand storytelling.
- Approved promotional identity.

### 4.2 Product and merchandising direction

- Product selection.
- Product categories.
- Product descriptions and product information.
- Product pricing.
- Product presentation.
- Merchandising priorities.
- Promotional product selection.
- Product availability decisions.

### 4.3 Marketing direction

- Store-level marketing objectives.
- Campaign themes.
- Promotional priorities.
- Brand messaging.
- Creative references.
- Target audience recommendations.
- Campaign approval.
- Participation in shared marketing initiatives.

### 4.4 Day-to-day operations

- Monitoring store activity.
- Managing supported product and order workflows.
- Coordinating fulfilment.
- Responding to customer enquiries through approved channels.
- Reporting operational problems.
- Requesting improvements.
- Reviewing store performance.

The exact permissions available through the dashboard must reflect the agreed authority model.

## 5. Reserved Parent Company Authority

The parent company retains authority over matters that affect the shared platform, infrastructure, security, financial governance and the wider business.

Reserved matters include:

- Platform architecture and core software.
- Infrastructure and hosting.
- Security controls.
- Authentication and access policies.
- Database architecture and shared data models.
- Platform-wide design standards.
- Technical deployment and release management.
- Shared integrations.
- Platform availability and maintenance.
- Central financial controls.
- Platform-wide legal and compliance requirements.
- Appointment and removal procedures.
- Shared marketing governance.
- Platform-wide service and subscription policies.

The parent company may reject or defer a request that would compromise security, stability, compliance, financial controls or the experience of other stores.

Where practical, the parent company should explain the decision and offer an alternative.

Reserved authority must not be used to arbitrarily interfere with a Store Owner's agreed brand identity or ordinary store decisions.

## 6. Store Identity and Brand Rights

### 6.1 Store identity

Each participating store must maintain a distinct identity.

The Store Owner should be credited for developing and operating that identity.

The parent company may provide infrastructure, design assistance, production resources and marketing support without claiming that the Store Owner's brand or work belongs to the parent company.

### 6.2 Brand ownership

The Store Owner retains the rights to their brand and underlying intellectual property, subject to the actual ownership records and any separate written agreement.

The parent company receives only the permissions required to operate the storefront and provide agreed services.

The agreement must distinguish:

- Brand name.
- Logo.
- Slogans.
- Product designs.
- Product descriptions.
- Photography and video.
- Marketing content.
- Social media accounts.
- Customer relationships.
- Third-party licensed assets.

### 6.3 Parent company use of brand assets

The parent company may use approved Store Owner brand assets only for agreed purposes.

These may include:

- Operating the assigned storefront.
- Producing approved marketing material.
- Promoting the store through central channels.
- Creating platform listings.
- Producing reports and business documentation.

Use beyond the agreed purposes requires additional permission.

### 6.4 Brand registration

Where formal brand registration is required, the parties must agree in writing on:

- The intended proprietor.
- The reason for the registration structure.
- The beneficial ownership arrangement.
- The costs.
- Renewal responsibilities.
- Licence or nominee arrangements, if applicable.
- Transfer and release obligations.
- Exit procedures.

No brand should be transferred or registered in the parent company's name merely because the Store Owner joined the platform.

Any nominee, agency or alternative registration arrangement must be reviewed by a South African intellectual property attorney.

## 7. Creative Production and Approval

The parent company operates a central creative production team.

The team is responsible for producing and preparing platform-ready media in accordance with the technical and visual standards of the platform.

The Store Owner contributes:

- Product knowledge.
- Brand references.
- Creative direction.
- Styling preferences.
- Product details.
- Review feedback.
- Approval of final presentation.

The parent company controls technical production standards, including image dimensions, aspect ratios, formats, compression, responsive variants and quality assurance.

The Store Owner retains meaningful input into the creative presentation of their products.

The scope, cost, usage rights and included production allowance must follow the applicable subscription and creative production agreements.

Neither party may assume ownership of commissioned content without an explicit written agreement.

## 8. Marketing and Promotion

The parent company may promote participating stores through its own marketing channels and campaigns.

Every campaign must preserve the individual identity of the participating store.

The Store Owner should be consulted and obtain the required approval opportunity before the parent company:

- Uses their brand in a new campaign.
- Publishes promotional claims on their behalf.
- Uses product imagery outside agreed purposes.
- Commits advertising expenditure.
- Makes material changes to approved brand messaging.
- Offers discounts or promotions that affect their commercial position.

Campaign approvals, advertising budgets, service fees and results must be recorded.

The parent company must not guarantee commercial outcomes.

## 9. Store Owner Dashboard and Change Requests

The Store Owner dashboard is the primary operational interface for supported store activities.

It may provide access to:

- Store information.
- Product management.
- Order management.
- Inventory information.
- Customer-facing operational workflows.
- Store performance reporting.
- Marketing and creative requests.
- Support requests.
- Store change requests.
- Commercial and subscription information.

### 9.1 Change request rights

A Store Owner may submit requests for improvements to their assigned store.

Examples include:

- Changing the position of a button.
- Adjusting product presentation.
- Adding a supported storefront section.
- Updating banners.
- Requesting a new promotional component.
- Improving navigation.
- Requesting a new supported feature.

### 9.2 Review and implementation

The parent company must assess requests against:

- Technical feasibility.
- Security.
- Accessibility.
- Platform consistency.
- Performance.
- Cost.
- Impact on other stores.
- Maintenance requirements.

Requests may be approved, rejected, deferred or returned for clarification.

Approved changes must follow the normal development, testing and release process.

A Store Owner may configure supported options but may not directly modify shared infrastructure or arbitrary platform code.

### 9.3 Request history

The platform should record:

- Request ID.
- Store ID.
- Requesting Store Owner.
- Description.
- Supporting screenshots or files.
- Business justification.
- Submission date.
- Review status.
- Decision.
- Reason.
- Assigned implementation work.
- Release reference.
- Completion date.

This history must remain associated with the store.

## 10. Operational Responsibilities

### 10.1 Store Owner responsibilities

The Store Owner is responsible for:

- Accurate product information.
- Lawful products and services.
- Product quality.
- Pricing decisions.
- Supplier and production arrangements.
- Stock accuracy within assigned workflows.
- Fulfilment arrangements.
- Customer communication.
- Order-related cooperation.
- Approved promotional commitments.
- Subscription and service payments.
- Prompt reporting of operational problems.

### 10.2 Parent company responsibilities

The parent company is responsible for:

- Maintaining the shared platform.
- Providing agreed technical support.
- Maintaining security controls.
- Managing platform-wide changes.
- Providing agreed onboarding.
- Operating central creative production.
- Managing approved marketing services.
- Maintaining platform-level records.
- Providing transparent commercial reporting.
- Supporting orderly store transitions.

### 10.3 Shared responsibilities

Certain matters require cooperation.

These include:

- Product launches.
- Campaign planning.
- Customer complaints involving platform issues.
- Payment discrepancies.
- Refund and cancellation coordination.
- Data correction.
- Product recalls or safety issues.
- Legal or regulatory enquiries.
- Store handovers.

The agreement must identify the responsible party for each workflow.

## 11. Customer, Order and Business Data

The platform may use a central customer identity system across multiple stores.

This does not mean that every Store Owner may access every customer's complete cross-store history.

Store Owners should only access customer and order information required for their own legitimate store operations and authorised purposes.

The parent company must establish clear rules for:

- Customer account ownership and access.
- Order visibility.
- Cross-store purchase history.
- Marketing consent.
- Customer communication.
- Data correction.
- Data export.
- Data retention.
- Security incidents.
- Data subject requests.

The parent company and Store Owner must have clearly defined responsibilities under the Protection of Personal Information Act.

The agreement must not imply that a Store Owner owns all personal information merely because a customer purchased from their store.

## 12. Financial and Commercial Obligations

The Store Owner must comply with the accepted commercial arrangement.

This may include:

- Monthly subscription fees.
- Approved additional creative production.
- Approved marketing services.
- Approved advertising expenditure.
- A future transaction fee, but only after separate written acceptance.

The parent company must provide clear billing and supporting records.

No optional service should be charged without an agreed scope and price.

Any future transaction fee must follow the separate transaction fee schedule and reconciliation requirements.

The commercial relationship is governed by the applicable subscription and service agreements.

## 13. Performance and Business Development

The parent company may review store performance to identify support needs, operational risks and growth opportunities.

Performance reviews may consider:

- Store activation.
- Product catalogue quality.
- Order activity.
- Fulfilment reliability.
- Customer feedback.
- Product availability.
- Store Owner participation.
- Marketing activity.
- Customer retention.
- Financial sustainability.

Performance data must be interpreted in context.

A new store must not be judged by the same historical sales expectations as an established business.

Reviews should account for:

- Store age.
- Starting condition.
- Product category.
- Market size.
- Location.
- Marketing resources.
- Seasonality.
- Operational capacity.
- Subscription level.
- Available support.

The purpose of performance review is to support improvement and responsible governance, not to create arbitrary pressure or automatic removal.

## 14. Conduct and Compliance

Store Owners must operate lawfully and honestly.

Prohibited conduct includes:

- Fraud.
- Deliberate misrepresentation.
- Counterfeit or infringing products.
- Unlawful products or services.
- Manipulation of orders or payment records.
- Abuse of customer information.
- Harassment or discrimination.
- Misuse of platform access.
- Deliberate security interference.
- False advertising.
- Unauthorised use of third-party intellectual property.

The parent company must investigate reported breaches fairly and maintain records of material decisions.

The agreement must identify serious breaches that may justify immediate protective action, subject to applicable law and contractual requirements.

## 15. Store Owner Reviews and Dispute Resolution

The parent company should conduct periodic reviews with Store Owners.

Reviews may cover:

- Store performance.
- Operational challenges.
- Product quality.
- Marketing opportunities.
- Subscription suitability.
- Creative requirements.
- Support quality.
- Proposed improvements.
- Compliance concerns.

Store Owners must have a clear channel to raise complaints and dispute decisions.

The dispute process should include:

1. Informal discussion.
2. Written escalation.
3. Review by an authorised decision-maker.
4. A documented outcome and reasons.
5. Mediation or other agreed dispute resolution where appropriate.
6. Legal remedies available under applicable law.

A Store Owner must not be penalised merely for raising a genuine complaint or challenging a decision in good faith.

## 16. Appointment Changes and Store Owner Departure

A Store Owner's assignment may end because of:

- Voluntary resignation.
- Mutual agreement.
- Expiry or termination of the agreement.
- Material breach.
- Inability to continue operating the store.
- Business closure.
- Other agreed contractual circumstances.

The process must distinguish between:

- Ending the Store Owner's assignment.
- Terminating the commercial agreement.
- Suspending access.
- Closing a store.
- Transferring the store to a successor.

These are not necessarily the same event.

### 16.1 Store continuity

Where a Store Owner leaves, the store may continue operating under the parent company's structure.

The store's identity, operational history and historical records should remain intact, subject to the applicable brand rights and agreements.

### 16.2 Successor appointment

A successor must be properly assessed and appointed.

The handover should identify:

- Current store status.
- Existing products.
- Inventory position.
- Outstanding orders.
- Customer service matters.
- Supplier and fulfilment arrangements.
- Active campaigns.
- Financial matters.
- Outstanding support requests.
- Brand and creative permissions.
- Access permissions.
- Historical records.

### 16.3 Attribution

A previous Store Owner's contributions must remain accurately attributed.

A successor must not be credited with work, improvements or achievements performed by a previous Store Owner.

The parent company must preserve the store's lineage and assignment history.

## 17. Suspension and Termination

The agreement must define fair and proportionate procedures for:

- Non-payment.
- Security risks.
- Serious misconduct.
- Unlawful trading.
- Material contractual breaches.
- Extended inactivity.
- Voluntary termination.

Where immediate action is required to protect customers, the platform or other stores, the parent company may take temporary protective measures.

Where appropriate, the Store Owner should receive notice, reasons and an opportunity to respond or remedy the issue.

Termination must follow the agreed contractual process and applicable law.

## 18. Confidentiality and Non-Disclosure

Both parties must protect confidential information obtained through the relationship.

Confidential information may include:

- Platform architecture.
- Security procedures.
- Internal financial information.
- Commercial terms.
- Supplier information.
- Customer information.
- Unreleased products.
- Campaign plans.
- Internal business strategies.
- Non-public technical documentation.

Confidentiality obligations must not prevent a Store Owner from using their own brand knowledge, experience, skills or independently developed business information.

The agreement should define permitted disclosures, exceptions and the duration of confidentiality obligations.

## 19. Agreement Package

The Store Owner Agreement should operate alongside the following documents:

1. Subscription Agreement and Service Schedule.
2. Trial and Pilot Agreement.
3. Brand and Intellectual Property Terms.
4. Creative Production Terms.
5. Marketing Services Agreement.
6. Advertising Budget and Campaign Approval Terms.
7. Data Processing and Privacy Terms.
8. Transaction Fee Schedule, if introduced.
9. Store Owner Conduct and Compliance Policy.
10. Store Change Request Procedure.
11. Exit, Data Export and Handover Terms.

Where documents conflict, the final agreement must specify which document takes precedence.

## 20. South African Legal Review

Before execution, the agreement must be reviewed by a South African commercial attorney.

Review must address:

- The legal nature of the Store Owner relationship.
- Whether any employment, partnership, agency or franchise implications arise.
- Contractual authority and delegation.
- Consumer protection.
- Electronic contracting.
- Privacy and data processing.
- Intellectual property.
- Payment and financial obligations.
- Liability and indemnities.
- Dispute resolution.
- Termination and restraint provisions, if any.
- Applicable tax obligations.

The agreement must accurately reflect the actual business relationship.

The parent company must not rely on labels alone to determine the legal nature of the relationship.

## 21. Final Governance Standard

The Store Owner relationship must be built on meaningful operational authority, clear commercial terms, transparent responsibilities and respect for individual business identity.

The Store Owner develops and operates their assigned store within the parent company's platform and governance structure.

The parent company provides and protects the shared infrastructure, central services and platform-wide standards.

Neither party should be misled about the rights, responsibilities or assets of the other.

Every appointment, material decision, change request, handover and termination must be documented so that the store's history and the contributions of its Store Owners remain traceable.
