# Store Owner Revenue Settlement and Financial Reconciliation Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes the financial controls governing sales revenue, payment collection, refunds, deductions, settlement and reconciliation for participating stores.

It defines how the parent company and Store Owners account for transactions processed through the shared platform.

The objectives are to:
- Maintain accurate financial records for every store.
- Distinguish store revenue from parent-company revenue.
- Ensure transparent settlement calculations.
- Prevent unauthorised deductions.
- Provide traceable payment and refund records.
- Establish clear dispute and reconciliation procedures.
- Protect customer funds and financial information.
- Support accurate financial reporting.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to:
- Online card payments.
- Payment gateway transactions.
- EFT payments.
- Manual payment verification.
- Cash transactions recorded through approved workflows.
- Refunds and cancellations.
- Delivery and other customer charges.
- Parent-company subscription fees.
- Optional creative and marketing service fees.
- Any agreed transaction-based platform fee.

It applies to all participating stores and authorised personnel involved in financial operations.

## 3. Financial Separation Principles

The platform must maintain a clear distinction between:

1. Store sales revenue.
2. Customer payments collected.
3. Payment-provider fees.
4. Refunds and chargebacks.
5. Delivery or fulfilment charges.
6. Store-level operating costs.
7. Parent-company subscription revenue.
8. Optional service revenue.
9. Agreed platform transaction fees.
10. Amounts payable to or by each party.

The parent company's ownership and operation of the platform does not make every store sale parent-company revenue.

Store-level financial activity must remain attributable to the relevant store.

## 4. Store Financial Records

Each store must have its own financial ledger or equivalent auditable financial record.

The record should capture:

- Store identifier.
- Order identifier.
- Payment transaction identifier.
- Transaction date.
- Gross order value.
- Discounts.
- Delivery charges.
- Tax amounts where applicable.
- Payment method.
- Payment-provider fees.
- Refunds.
- Chargebacks.
- Reversals.
- Agreed platform fees.
- Other authorised deductions.
- Net settlement amount.
- Settlement reference.
- Settlement date.
- Reconciliation status.

Records must preserve the original transaction and subsequent adjustments rather than overwriting history.

## 5. Gross Sales and Net Settlement

Gross sales must not be confused with the amount available for settlement.

The settlement calculation must identify each component separately.

A general calculation is:

Net settlement =
Gross collected customer payments
- Valid refunds
- Chargebacks and reversals
- Agreed payment-processing charges
- Agreed platform transaction fees
- Other explicitly authorised deductions
- Applicable adjustments

This calculation must be adapted to the actual payment-provider arrangement and the contractual relationship between the parent company and Store Owner.

Uncollected orders must not be recorded as collected cash revenue.

## 6. Payment Collection Models

The parent company must document which payment collection model applies to each payment method.

Possible arrangements include:

### 6.1 Direct Store Collection

The customer pays directly into an account controlled by the Store Owner.

The platform records the payment and relevant order information.

The Store Owner remains responsible for the receipt and reconciliation of funds, subject to the agreed platform reporting requirements.

### 6.2 Parent Company Collection

The customer pays through a payment arrangement controlled by the parent company.

The parent company records the transaction and accounts for the amount attributable to the relevant store.

The settlement process must identify the legal basis for collecting and holding funds, the responsible payment entity and the applicable contractual obligations.

### 6.3 Payment Provider Managed Settlement

A payment provider collects and distributes funds according to its approved marketplace or connected-account arrangement.

The platform must reconcile provider reports against internal order and payment records.

The actual payment flow must be confirmed with the provider before this model is implemented.

The parent company must not assume that a payment gateway automatically supports marketplace settlement or split payments.

## 7. Payment Verification

Payment status must be based on reliable evidence.

For integrated payments:
- Use verified provider transaction records.
- Validate provider callbacks or webhooks.
- Prevent duplicate payment processing.
- Record transaction references.
- Reconcile provider settlement reports.

For manual payments:
- Follow the Manual Payment Verification and Reconciliation Standards.
- Confirm the funds have actually been received.
- Record the verifier and verification time.
- Preserve relevant evidence.
- Do not rely solely on customer-submitted screenshots.

An order must not be marked paid merely because a customer claims to have made payment.

## 8. Payment Statuses

Financial and order statuses must remain distinguishable.

Recommended payment statuses include:

- Pending: Payment has not been confirmed.
- Awaiting Verification: Manual evidence requires review.
- Paid: Payment has been confirmed.
- Partially Paid: Only part of the amount has been received.
- Failed: Payment attempt was unsuccessful.
- Cancelled: Payment attempt or obligation was cancelled.
- Refunded: Payment has been refunded.
- Partially Refunded: Part of the payment has been returned.
- Disputed: Payment is under dispute.
- Chargeback: A chargeback has been recorded.
- Reversed: A payment has been reversed.
- Reconciliation Required: Records do not match.
- Reconciled: Financial records have been matched and reviewed.

A paid order is not automatically settled to the Store Owner.

## 9. Refunds and Cancellations

Refunds must follow the applicable customer-facing policy and legal requirements.

Every refund record should include:

- Order identifier.
- Payment identifier.
- Store identifier.
- Refund amount.
- Refund reason.
- Initiating person.
- Approval where required.
- Payment-provider reference.
- Refund date.
- Refund status.

Refunds must be recorded against the original transaction.

A refund must not be counted as a new sale or used to erase the original transaction.

Where a refund occurs after settlement, the resulting adjustment must be recorded transparently and handled according to the agreement.

The parent company must not make arbitrary deductions from unrelated Store Owner revenue to recover a disputed amount.

## 10. Chargebacks and Payment Disputes

Chargebacks must be recorded separately from ordinary refunds.

The financial record should identify:
- Original transaction.
- Disputed amount.
- Payment-provider reference.
- Dispute reason.
- Supporting evidence.
- Response deadline.
- Outcome.
- Applicable fees.
- Final adjustment.

Responsibility for chargeback losses and provider fees must follow the contractual agreement and actual cause of the dispute.

The parent company must provide relevant evidence to the responsible Store Owner where appropriate and lawful.

## 11. Platform Transaction Fees

A transaction-based platform fee may be introduced only under a separately disclosed and accepted agreement.

Before implementation, the agreement must define:

- Fee percentage or fixed amount.
- Effective date.
- Qualifying transaction types.
- Calculation basis.
- Treatment of discounts.
- Treatment of delivery charges.
- Treatment of taxes.
- Refund adjustments.
- Cancelled orders.
- Chargebacks.
- Cash and EFT transactions.
- WhatsApp-assisted orders.
- Reporting requirements.
- Dispute procedure.

The fee must not be applied retrospectively without a valid contractual basis.

The platform must be able to identify and reconcile qualifying completed transactions before charging the fee.

A fee must not be calculated from an unverified order, failed payment or cancelled transaction.

## 12. Subscription and Optional Service Fees

Subscription fees must be recorded separately from sales revenue.

Optional marketing, photography, video and creative production charges must also be recorded separately.

Each charge must have:
- An applicable agreement or approved quotation.
- A description.
- Amount.
- Currency.
- Issue date.
- Due date.
- Payment status.
- Applicable tax treatment.
- Relevant store identifier.

A Store Owner must be able to distinguish a platform subscription from a separately purchased service.

Advertising spend must remain separate from marketing service fees.

## 13. Settlement Schedule

The parent company must publish an approved settlement schedule where it controls or coordinates settlement.

The schedule must define:
- Settlement frequency.
- Cut-off time.
- Processing period.
- Expected settlement date.
- Minimum settlement amount, if any.
- Treatment of weekends and public holidays.
- Treatment of provider delays.
- Treatment of disputed transactions.
- Treatment of pending refunds.
- Applicable settlement fees.

A settlement date must not be promised before the payment provider's actual processing conditions are understood.

If a settlement is delayed, the responsible party must communicate the reason and revised expected date.

## 14. Settlement Statement

Every settlement should produce a statement that the Store Owner can review.

The statement should include:

- Store identity.
- Settlement period.
- Opening balance, where applicable.
- Gross collected payments.
- Discounts.
- Refunds.
- Chargebacks.
- Payment-provider charges.
- Agreed platform fees.
- Other authorised deductions.
- Adjustments.
- Net settlement amount.
- Amount previously settled.
- Outstanding balance.
- Settlement reference.
- Settlement date.

The Store Owner must be able to trace statement totals back to individual transactions, subject to appropriate access controls.

## 15. Reconciliation Process

Reconciliation must compare three principal records:

1. Internal order and payment records.
2. Payment-provider transaction and settlement records.
3. Bank or receiving-account records, where applicable.

The reconciliation process should:

- Identify the settlement period.
- Import or obtain provider records.
- Match transactions using reliable identifiers.
- Match refunds and reversals.
- Identify missing or duplicated transactions.
- Identify unmatched amounts.
- Investigate differences.
- Record adjustments with reasons.
- Obtain appropriate review.
- Mark the period as reconciled.

Unresolved differences must remain visible.

A reconciliation process must never silently force records to balance by changing historical transaction values.

## 16. Reconciliation Exceptions

Common exceptions include:

- Payment received but order not marked paid.
- Order marked paid without matching provider evidence.
- Duplicate payment.
- Missing provider transaction.
- Settlement amount differs from internal records.
- Refund not reflected by the provider.
- Chargeback not recorded internally.
- Payment received into an unexpected account.
- Incorrect store attribution.
- Incorrect fee calculation.
- Duplicate settlement.
- Bank deposit without a matching transaction.

Each exception must have:
- A unique reference.
- A description.
- An assigned reviewer.
- Supporting evidence.
- Investigation notes.
- Resolution status.
- Final adjustment, if required.

## 17. Store Owner Financial Visibility

Store Owners should have access to their own permitted financial information.

The dashboard should support:
- Sales summaries.
- Payment status.
- Refund history.
- Settlement statements.
- Fee breakdowns.
- Reconciliation exceptions relevant to their store.
- Exportable financial records where supported.

A Store Owner must not be able to access another store's private financial records.

Central management access must follow role-based access controls and audit logging.

## 18. Financial Disputes

A Store Owner must be able to dispute a financial entry or settlement statement.

The dispute should identify:
- Relevant transaction or settlement.
- Amount disputed.
- Reason.
- Supporting evidence.
- Requested correction.

The parent company should:
1. Acknowledge the dispute.
2. Record it.
3. Review the underlying evidence.
4. Reconcile relevant provider and bank records.
5. Communicate findings.
6. Correct confirmed errors.
7. Record the final outcome.

Undisputed amounts should not automatically be withheld because another amount is under investigation, unless a valid contractual or legal basis requires otherwise.

## 19. Financial Controls and Approval

Financial controls should include:

- Separation of payment verification and settlement approval where practical.
- Restricted access to payment configuration.
- Approval for exceptional adjustments.
- Audit logging of financial changes.
- Unique transaction references.
- Duplicate detection.
- Reconciliation review.
- Controlled refund permissions.
- Regular review of outstanding balances.

No individual should be able to create, approve and conceal an unauthorised financial adjustment without detection.

## 20. Security and Fraud Prevention

The parent company must protect financial records and payment systems.

Controls should include:
- Role-based access.
- Multi-factor authentication where supported.
- Secure provider integrations.
- Verified webhook handling.
- Idempotent payment processing.
- Audit logs.
- Restricted financial exports.
- Monitoring of unusual activity.
- Secure handling of bank information.
- Incident reporting.

Full payment-card information must not be stored in ordinary application records unless the platform has a properly assessed and compliant payment architecture.

## 21. Financial Reporting

The parent company should maintain separate reporting for:

### Store-Level Reporting
- Gross sales.
- Collected payments.
- Refunds.
- Chargebacks.
- Payment fees.
- Platform fees.
- Net settlement.
- Outstanding balances.

### Parent-Company Reporting
- Subscription revenue.
- Optional service revenue.
- Transaction-fee revenue, where applicable.
- Infrastructure costs.
- Creative production costs.
- Marketing costs.
- Support costs.
- Payment-related costs.
- Platform operating expenses.

Store sales must not be combined with parent-company revenue in a way that misrepresents the parent company's financial performance.

Accounting treatment must be confirmed with a qualified South African accountant.

## 22. Records and Retention

Financial records must be retained according to applicable legal, tax, accounting and contractual requirements.

Records must:
- Be protected against unauthorised alteration.
- Preserve transaction history.
- Identify the responsible actor for adjustments.
- Support audit and reconciliation.
- Be retrievable for legitimate disputes.
- Follow approved retention and deletion procedures.

Financial records must not be deleted merely because a Store Owner leaves the platform.

## 23. Store Owner Responsibilities

Store Owners must:
- Provide accurate settlement details.
- Keep banking information current.
- Review settlement statements.
- Report discrepancies promptly.
- Maintain required sales and financial records.
- Follow payment-verification procedures.
- Avoid manipulating order or payment statuses.
- Cooperate with reasonable reconciliation requests.
- Inform the parent company of suspected financial fraud.

Changes to settlement bank details must follow a verified process.

## 24. Parent Company Responsibilities

The parent company must:
- Maintain accurate platform financial records.
- Explain settlement calculations.
- Apply only authorised deductions.
- Reconcile payment-provider records.
- Protect financial information.
- Investigate discrepancies.
- Communicate settlement delays.
- Preserve an auditable adjustment history.
- Maintain clear separation of store and parent-company revenue.
- Follow applicable contractual and legal obligations.

## 25. Implementation Dependencies

Before this policy is operational, the parent company must establish:

- The legal payment collection model.
- The payment provider's settlement capabilities.
- The responsible entity for receiving customer funds.
- The store-level ledger structure.
- Settlement calculation rules.
- Fee schedules.
- Refund and chargeback procedures.
- Reconciliation ownership.
- Financial approval roles.
- Dashboard reporting.
- Accounting integration requirements.

The platform must not introduce automated settlement deductions until these dependencies have been implemented and tested.
