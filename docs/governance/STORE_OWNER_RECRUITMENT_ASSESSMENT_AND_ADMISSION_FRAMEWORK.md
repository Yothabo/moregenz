## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the process for identifying, assessing, selecting and onboarding Store Owners into the parent company's multi-store platform.

The parent company exists to help entrepreneurs and small businesses access the digital infrastructure, technical capabilities, creative resources and commercial support they may not be able to build independently.

Recruitment must therefore identify more than established businesses. It must also recognise informal businesses with real trading activity and founders who have a credible idea but have not yet launched.

The objective is to select Store Owners who demonstrate potential, commitment, integrity and the ability to develop a sustainable business.

## 2. Recruitment Philosophy

The parent company should be accessible to entrepreneurs at different stages of business development.

A candidate must not automatically be rejected because they:

- Do not have a registered company.
- Do not have a website.
- Do not have a large social media following.
- Do not have a formal business office.
- Do not have audited financial statements.
- Have not yet generated revenue.
- Operate informally.
- Are still developing their first product.

These factors may be relevant to a particular assessment, but they are not universal admission requirements.

The assessment must consider the applicant's actual stage, circumstances, product or service, market opportunity and ability to execute.

The parent company must distinguish between a candidate who lacks infrastructure and one who lacks a credible business proposition or willingness to operate responsibly.

## 3. Candidate Categories

Applications should be classified into three primary pathways.

### 3.1 Existing informal businesses

This pathway is for entrepreneurs who are already selling products or services but may not have formal company registration or professional digital infrastructure.

Examples include:

- Clothing sellers.
- T-shirt printers.
- Home-based bakeries.
- Beauty and personal-care businesses.
- Independent designers.
- Local manufacturers.
- Craft producers.
- WhatsApp-based businesses.
- Social media sellers.
- Small-scale distributors.
- Other informal trading operations.

The assessment should focus on evidence of genuine business activity, product quality, customer relationships, fulfilment ability and growth potential.

Useful evidence may include:

- Product photographs.
- Existing product listings.
- Social media profiles.
- WhatsApp Business presence.
- Customer testimonials.
- Previous orders.
- Proof of completed work.
- Repeat customers.
- Supplier or production arrangements.
- Current prices.
- Basic sales or order records.
- Customer feedback.
- Evidence of reliable delivery or collection arrangements.

The parent company should not require audited financial statements from an informal micro-business unless a specific risk or legal requirement makes them necessary.

### 3.2 Existing registered businesses

This pathway is for businesses that have already established a formal operating structure.

The assessment should consider:

- Business registration details, where applicable.
- The applicant's authority to represent the business.
- Existing products or services.
- Trading history.
- Customer base.
- Brand identity.
- Intellectual property ownership or usage rights.
- Supplier relationships.
- Production capacity.
- Fulfilment capability.
- Existing marketing activities.
- Business objectives.
- Digital growth requirements.
- Relevant industry-specific compliance.

Company registration is evidence of formal establishment, not proof of product quality, commercial viability or operational reliability.

A registered business must undergo the same substantive assessment of its products, market and ability to operate.

### 3.3 New ventures and idea-stage founders

This pathway is for applicants who have an idea or proposed business but have not yet launched.

They may not have:

- Existing sales.
- Previous customers.
- A registered company.
- A website.
- A social media following.
- A complete product catalogue.
- Established supplier relationships.

Their assessment must focus on the founder and the proposed business.

Relevant considerations include:

- The problem or customer need being addressed.
- The proposed product or service.
- Target market.
- Market understanding.
- Differentiation.
- Product sourcing or production plan.
- Initial pricing assumptions.
- Estimated costs and margins.
- Ability to test demand.
- Founder commitment.
- Time available to develop the venture.
- Willingness to learn and respond to feedback.
- Ability to execute a realistic launch plan.

Idea-stage applicants may be admitted to a pre-launch or development pathway rather than receiving an immediate full storefront launch.

## 4. Recruitment Sources

The parent company may identify candidates through:

- Direct applications.
- Referrals.
- Existing business networks.
- Social media.
- Local entrepreneur communities.
- Small business events.
- Business support organisations.
- Industry associations.
- Existing customers and suppliers.
- Local producers and manufacturers.
- Entrepreneurship programmes.
- Community business initiatives.

Recruitment should not depend exclusively on online applications.

The parent company should actively identify entrepreneurs who have established customer relationships and trading activity but lack access to professional e-commerce infrastructure.

## 5. Application Information

The application process should collect only information necessary to assess the candidate.

### 5.1 Basic applicant information

- Full legal name.
- Contact details.
- Location.
- Preferred communication method.
- Identity verification information through an appropriate secure process.
- Business name, if one exists.
- Applicant's relationship to the proposed business.

### 5.2 Business information

- Business stage.
- Business description.
- Product or service categories.
- Target customers.
- Current trading status.
- Existing sales channels.
- Current customer base.
- Product sourcing or production method.
- Fulfilment arrangements.
- Main business challenges.
- Growth objectives.

### 5.3 Supporting material

Depending on the applicant's business stage, supporting material may include:

- Product photographs.
- Brand assets.
- Social media links.
- Product samples.
- Customer testimonials.
- Previous work.
- Order records.
- Supplier information.
- Business registration records, where applicable.
- A business concept or launch plan.

Applicants should not be required to provide sensitive financial or identity documents through ordinary unsecured communication channels.

## 6. Verification and Due Diligence

The parent company must verify the information necessary to make a responsible appointment.

Verification must be proportionate to the applicant's business stage and risk profile.

### 6.1 Identity and authority

Confirm that:

- The applicant is a real and identifiable person.
- The applicant has authority to represent the business.
- Any required business ownership or authorisation information is credible.
- There are no material identity inconsistencies.

### 6.2 Product and service verification

Review:

- Whether products or services genuinely exist or can reasonably be produced.
- Whether the applicant has the right to sell them.
- Whether product claims are accurate.
- Whether products meet applicable legal and safety requirements.
- Whether the proposed product category is supported by the platform.

### 6.3 Business activity

For established businesses, assess available evidence of trading.

For new ventures, assess the credibility of the proposed operating plan rather than demanding historical sales.

### 6.4 Brand and intellectual property

Determine:

- Who created the brand.
- Who owns or controls the logo.
- Whether the business name is already in use.
- Whether product images may legally be used.
- Whether third-party assets are properly licensed.
- Whether the applicant has permission to use supplier or manufacturer materials.

Unverified or potentially infringing material must not be published until the rights issue is resolved.

### 6.5 Operational capability

Assess whether the applicant can:

- Source or produce the advertised products.
- Maintain reasonable product quality.
- Keep product availability accurate.
- Fulfil orders.
- Handle customer enquiries.
- Resolve operational problems.
- Meet commitments made to customers.

### 6.6 Compliance

Identify any relevant legal or regulatory requirements.

These may include:

- Product-specific permits.
- Food safety requirements.
- Consumer protection obligations.
- Labelling requirements.
- Professional licences.
- Intellectual property requirements.
- Tax obligations.
- Data protection responsibilities.

Requirements must be determined according to the actual business and product category. The parent company must not invent universal compliance requirements for every applicant.

## 7. Registration and Informal Business Considerations

The absence of CIPC company registration must not automatically disqualify an applicant.

South African businesses may operate in different legal forms, including sole proprietorships and informal trading arrangements.

The parent company must establish the applicant's actual legal and trading position and determine which obligations apply.

Relevant official guidance includes:

- CIPC business registration information: https://www.cipc.co.za/?page_id=10462
- SARS guidance for starting and registering a business: https://www.sars.gov.za/businesses-and-employers/small-businesses-taxpayers/starting-a-business-and-tax/registering/

The parent company should obtain appropriate legal and tax advice when an applicant's business structure or obligations are unclear.

## 8. Assessment Framework

Each applicant should be evaluated using a structured assessment.

The proposed scorecard uses a 100-point scale.

| Assessment area | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Entrepreneurial drive and commitment | 20 |
| Product or business viability | 20 |
| Customer and market understanding | 15 |
| Operational capability | 15 |
| Marketing and growth potential | 15 |
| Reliability and professional conduct | 10 |
| Alignment with platform standards | 5 |
| **Total** | **100** |

These weights are an internal proposal and should be reviewed after the pilot recruitment programme.

### 8.1 Entrepreneurial drive and commitment: 20 points

Evaluate:

- Motivation.
- Personal commitment.
- Willingness to take responsibility.
- Consistency.
- Ability to overcome challenges.
- Willingness to learn.
- Availability to operate the business.

### 8.2 Product or business viability: 20 points

Evaluate:

- Product or service quality.
- Clear value proposition.
- Demand indicators.
- Pricing assumptions.
- Cost structure.
- Differentiation.
- Production or sourcing feasibility.
- Commercial potential.

For idea-stage applicants, assess the strength of the proposed business and validation plan rather than existing sales.

### 8.3 Customer and market understanding: 15 points

Evaluate:

- Understanding of the target customer.
- Customer needs.
- Market size and accessibility.
- Competitor awareness.
- Customer feedback.
- Existing customer relationships, where applicable.
- Ability to reach potential customers.

### 8.4 Operational capability: 15 points

Evaluate:

- Production or sourcing arrangements.
- Product availability.
- Quality control.
- Fulfilment planning.
- Customer service readiness.
- Reliability.
- Ability to manage orders.

### 8.5 Marketing and growth potential: 15 points

Evaluate:

- Brand potential.
- Product presentation.
- Marketing opportunities.
- Existing audience, where applicable.
- Customer acquisition strategy.
- Growth objectives.
- Ability to participate in campaigns.

A large existing following is not mandatory.

### 8.6 Reliability and professional conduct: 10 points

Evaluate:

- Honesty.
- Communication.
- Responsiveness.
- Accountability.
- Accuracy of information.
- Willingness to follow agreed procedures.
- Treatment of customers and partners.

### 8.7 Alignment with platform standards: 5 points

Evaluate:

- Willingness to follow platform policies.
- Acceptance of security and privacy requirements.
- Cooperation with technical and creative workflows.
- Respect for other participating stores.
- Acceptance of the governance framework.

## 9. Mandatory Admission Gates

A high assessment score does not override serious compliance or integrity concerns.

The following must be treated as mandatory admission gates:

1. Identity and applicant authority can be reasonably verified.
2. No unresolved material fraud or deliberate misrepresentation.
3. Products and services are lawful and suitable for the platform.
4. The applicant has the necessary rights to sell products and use submitted brand assets.
5. A credible production, sourcing or fulfilment plan exists.
6. The applicant accepts the applicable platform, privacy and security requirements.
7. The applicant understands and accepts the commercial arrangement.
8. The applicant agrees to the Store Owner governance framework.

An applicant who fails a mandatory gate should not be appointed until the issue is resolved.

## 10. Proposed Assessment Outcomes

The following outcome bands are proposed for the initial recruitment programme.

| Score | Suggested outcome |
|---|---|
| 80–100 | Strong candidate, subject to verification and approval |
| 65–79 | Suitable for a structured pilot or conditional appointment |
| 50–64 | Development pathway before full appointment |
| Below 50 | Not currently ready for appointment |

These bands are internal guidance, not an automatic decision mechanism.

The assessment panel must consider the applicant's business stage, mandatory gates and supporting evidence.

A candidate with a strong idea but no trading history should not be penalised merely for lacking established sales.

## 11. Development and Pre-Launch Pathway

Applicants who show potential but are not ready for a full store launch may enter a development pathway.

This may include:

- Business concept refinement.
- Customer interviews.
- Product validation.
- Competitor research.
- Supplier identification.
- Pricing and margin exercises.
- Product sample development.
- Brand identity development.
- Initial marketing planning.
- Fulfilment planning.
- Legal and compliance checks.

The parent company may set specific milestones before approving a full storefront.

Examples include:

- Demonstrating interest from potential customers.
- Producing a viable product sample.
- Obtaining a credible supplier quotation.
- Completing a basic pricing exercise.
- Demonstrating a realistic fulfilment plan.

The purpose is to reduce avoidable business risk and help founders become launch-ready.

Participation in a development pathway does not guarantee admission or funding.

## 12. Conditional Admission

The parent company may approve an applicant subject to specific conditions.

Examples include:

- Completing product verification.
- Resolving brand ownership questions.
- Providing additional supplier information.
- Completing required training.
- Demonstrating fulfilment readiness.
- Correcting inaccurate product claims.
- Completing relevant compliance steps.

Conditions must be:

- Clearly documented.
- Reasonable.
- Relevant to the identified risk.
- Assigned a responsible person.
- Given a realistic completion period.

The applicant must know which conditions must be met before the store can launch.

## 13. Admission Decision

The admission decision should be made by an authorised person or assessment panel.

The decision record must include:

- Applicant ID.
- Proposed business.
- Business stage.
- Assessment date.
- Score by category.
- Evidence reviewed.
- Mandatory gate results.
- Identified risks.
- Conditions, if any.
- Decision.
- Reason for decision.
- Approving person.
- Next steps.

Possible decisions include:

- Approved.
- Approved with conditions.
- Development pathway.
- Deferred pending further information.
- Declined.

Applicants should receive a clear and respectful outcome.

## 14. Fairness and Conflict of Interest

Recruitment decisions must be based on documented criteria.

The assessment process should avoid:

- Favouritism.
- Discrimination.
- Personal conflicts of interest.
- Arbitrary requirements.
- Unexplained rejection.
- Preferential treatment without a legitimate business reason.

Where an assessor has a personal or financial relationship with an applicant, that relationship must be disclosed.

The parent company should maintain a record of material conflicts and how they were managed.

## 15. Store Owner Onboarding

Once approved, the applicant enters the formal onboarding process.

Onboarding should cover:

1. Identity and business verification.
2. Agreement execution.
3. Subscription or trial acceptance.
4. Store creation or assignment.
5. Brand identity collection.
6. Product and catalogue preparation.
7. Photography and media production planning.
8. Storefront configuration.
9. Payment and fulfilment setup.
10. Dashboard access.
11. Security and account setup.
12. Privacy and customer-data responsibilities.
13. Store Owner training.
14. Testing and quality assurance.
15. Launch approval.

The store must not be publicly launched until mandatory operational and compliance checks are complete.

## 16. Initial Review Period

New Store Owners should receive a structured review during their initial operating period.

The review should consider:

- Completion of onboarding.
- Product information accuracy.
- Storefront quality.
- Operational participation.
- Order handling.
- Customer communication.
- Fulfilment reliability.
- Compliance with platform standards.
- Support requirements.
- Business development progress.

The review should identify training and support needs.

It must not treat early sales volume as the only measure of success.

## 17. Recruitment Records and Data Protection

Applicant records must be collected and retained for legitimate recruitment and governance purposes.

The parent company must:

- Explain why information is collected.
- Collect only necessary information.
- Protect identity and financial documents.
- Limit access to authorised personnel.
- Maintain appropriate retention periods.
- Secure supporting files.
- Handle unsuccessful applications responsibly.
- Comply with applicable POPIA requirements.

Applicants must not be required to provide unnecessary sensitive personal information.

## 18. Recruitment Performance Monitoring

The parent company should periodically review recruitment outcomes.

Useful measures include:

- Number of applications.
- Applications by business stage.
- Verification completion rate.
- Approval rate.
- Conditional admission rate.
- Development pathway participation.
- Time from application to decision.
- Time from approval to launch.
- Store activation rate.
- Early operational issues.
- Store Owner retention.
- Reasons for rejection or withdrawal.

The objective is to improve selection quality and remove unnecessary barriers without lowering essential standards.

## 19. Recommended External Support Resources

The parent company may refer applicants to appropriate business development organisations where additional assistance is needed.

Useful starting points include:

- Small Enterprise Development and Finance Agency (SEDFA), including its small business support services: https://www.gov.za/st/about-government/small-business-development
- Eastern Cape Development Corporation business support model: https://www.ecdc.co.za/business-support
- Y Combinator application and founder guidance for idea-stage businesses: https://www.ycombinator.com/faq and https://www.ycombinator.com/howtoapply

These resources are examples of external support. They do not replace the parent company's own admission process.

## 20. Review and Improvement

The recruitment framework should be reviewed after the first pilot cohort.

The review should consider:

- Whether assessment criteria predict operational readiness.
- Whether informal businesses are being unfairly excluded.
- Whether idea-stage founders receive appropriate evaluation.
- Whether verification requirements are proportionate.
- Whether admission decisions are consistent.
- Whether onboarding delays are excessive.
- Whether the parent company has sufficient capacity to support approved applicants.

Scoring weights and admission thresholds may be adjusted based on evidence.

Any changes must be documented and applied consistently.

## 21. Final Standard

The parent company must select Store Owners based on integrity, commitment, business potential, operational readiness and willingness to work within the shared platform.

It must not confuse formal registration with business quality, existing sales with future potential, or social media popularity with commercial viability.

Established informal businesses, registered businesses and idea-stage founders must each receive an assessment appropriate to their stage.

The recruitment process must remain fair, evidence-based, transparent and aligned with the parent company's purpose of helping entrepreneurs build sustainable businesses.
