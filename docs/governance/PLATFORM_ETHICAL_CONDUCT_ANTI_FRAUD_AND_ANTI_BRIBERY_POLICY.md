## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes ethical conduct standards and controls intended to prevent fraud, bribery, corruption, dishonesty and abuse of authority across the platform's business operations.

The objective is to build a business environment in which customers, Store Owners, employees, suppliers and business partners can participate with confidence.

The policy protects the parent company, participating stores, customers, business relationships and the integrity of platform records.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to:

- The parent company and its leadership.
- Store Owners.
- Employees and contractors.
- Temporary staff and representatives.
- Suppliers and service providers.
- Marketing and creative partners.
- Business development partners.
- Anyone acting on behalf of the parent company or a participating store.

Where an external party operates under a separate agreement, the applicable contractual obligations must be read together with this policy.

## 3. Ethical conduct principles

All participants must act with:

1. Honesty: provide accurate information and avoid deliberate deception.
2. Integrity: act consistently with applicable agreements and responsibilities.
3. Fairness: avoid exploiting customers, suppliers or other participants.
4. Accountability: accept responsibility for decisions and actions.
5. Transparency: disclose material commercial arrangements and conflicts.
6. Respect: treat customers, colleagues and business partners professionally.
7. Confidentiality: protect information accessed through the platform.
8. Legal compliance: follow applicable South African laws and regulations.

## 4. Prohibited conduct

The following conduct is prohibited:

- Fraudulent transactions.
- False payment confirmations.
- Fabricated orders or sales.
- Manipulation of financial records.
- Theft or misuse of funds.
- Bribery or kickbacks.
- Undisclosed commissions.
- Misuse of customer information.
- Deliberate misrepresentation of products.
- Forged documents or approvals.
- Unauthorised access to accounts.
- Deliberate manipulation of stock records.
- Abuse of administrative privileges.
- Concealing material conflicts of interest.
- Retaliation against legitimate whistleblowers.
- Collusion intended to deceive customers or the parent company.

## 5. Fraud prevention

Fraud includes intentional deception intended to obtain an improper financial, commercial or operational benefit.

Examples include:

- Marking an unpaid order as paid.
- Submitting false proof of payment.
- Creating fictitious customers or orders to inflate performance.
- Manipulating refunds or settlement calculations.
- Recording stock that does not exist.
- Diverting customer payments.
- Using another participant's account without authorisation.
- Submitting false supplier invoices.
- Misrepresenting marketing expenditure.
- Claiming services were delivered when they were not.

The parent company must maintain appropriate controls to prevent, detect and investigate suspected fraud.

## 6. Payment and financial integrity

All financial activity must follow approved payment, reconciliation and settlement procedures.

Participants must not:

- Alter payment evidence.
- Falsify transaction references.
- Confirm payments without verification.
- Divert payments to unauthorised accounts.
- Make unauthorised deductions.
- Conceal refunds or chargebacks.
- Manipulate settlement records.
- Create duplicate claims for the same expense.

Financial adjustments must have a documented reason and appropriate authorisation.

Payment verification must be performed by authorised personnel using reliable evidence.

## 7. Bribery and corruption

Bribery involves offering, giving, requesting or receiving an improper benefit to influence a decision or action.

Bribery and corrupt conduct are prohibited in all platform-related activities.

Examples include:

- Paying someone to approve a supplier.
- Offering personal benefits to obtain preferential treatment.
- Accepting kickbacks for selecting a service provider.
- Paying for access to confidential customer or business information.
- Offering benefits to manipulate product approvals.
- Using personal relationships to bypass required controls.
- Offering inducements to influence financial reconciliation.

No participant may authorise or facilitate an improper payment on behalf of the parent company or a participating store.

## 8. Gifts, hospitality and benefits

Reasonable business hospitality may be acceptable where it is lawful, modest, transparent and unrelated to improper influence.

Participants must not accept or offer gifts or benefits that:

- Influence a commercial decision.
- Create an obligation to provide preferential treatment.
- Conceal a payment or commission.
- Are excessive or inappropriate.
- Violate applicable law or contractual requirements.

Cash gifts and cash-equivalent benefits must not be accepted as business courtesies.

Where a gift or hospitality arrangement could create a conflict, it must be disclosed to the appropriate responsible person.

The parent company should maintain a gift and hospitality register where the nature or value of business interactions justifies one.

## 9. Conflicts of interest

A conflict of interest occurs when personal, financial or other interests could improperly influence a participant's professional or business decisions.

Examples include:

- Selecting a supplier owned by a close relative without disclosure.
- Approving payments to a business in which the approver has an interest.
- Using confidential platform information for personal commercial gain.
- Giving preferential access to business opportunities because of personal relationships.
- Influencing Store Owner recruitment for undisclosed personal benefit.

Actual, potential and perceived conflicts must be disclosed.

A conflict does not automatically mean misconduct has occurred. It must be assessed and managed appropriately.

Possible controls include:

- Independent approval.
- Reassignment of decision-making authority.
- Additional review.
- Documented disclosure.
- Exclusion from a specific decision.

## 10. Supplier and procurement integrity

Supplier selection must be based on legitimate business considerations, including:

- Product or service suitability.
- Price and total cost.
- Quality.
- Reliability.
- Legal and regulatory compliance.
- Security and privacy requirements.
- Relevant experience.
- Conflicts of interest.

Supplier decisions must not be manipulated for personal benefit.

Undisclosed commissions, kickbacks and personal payments connected to supplier selection are prohibited.

Procurement records must accurately reflect the parties, agreed prices, approvals and services delivered.

## 11. Store Owner responsibilities

Store Owners must:

- Provide accurate business and product information.
- Handle customer payments honestly.
- Maintain accurate order and inventory records.
- Respect customer privacy.
- Disclose relevant conflicts.
- Avoid misleading marketing.
- Follow approved settlement procedures.
- Cooperate with legitimate investigations.
- Avoid using the platform to facilitate unlawful activity.

Store Owners remain responsible for the lawful operation of their own businesses and must meet obligations applicable to their products, services and commercial activities.

The parent company must not use this policy to claim control over independent business decisions that are legitimately reserved to Store Owners under their agreements.

## 12. Employee and administrator responsibilities

Employees and administrators must use their access only for authorised business purposes.

They must not:

- View private records out of curiosity.
- Modify financial records without authority.
- Create unauthorised accounts or privileges.
- Bypass approval procedures.
- Use customer or Store Owner data for personal purposes.
- Conceal errors or suspected misconduct.
- Share credentials.
- Manipulate audit records.
- Use administrative authority to settle personal disputes.

Privileged actions must be logged where technically and operationally appropriate.

## 13. Customer protection and honest marketing

Marketing and product information must be accurate and must not deliberately mislead customers.

Participants must not:

- Invent product claims.
- Falsify reviews or testimonials.
- Manipulate ratings.
- Misrepresent product origin.
- Conceal material product limitations.
- Advertise unavailable stock as available.
- Misrepresent delivery times.
- Create fake discounts.
- Use deceptive pricing practices.
- Impersonate customers or competitors.

Marketing claims must be supported by reasonable evidence.

## 14. Confidential information and misuse of access

Confidential information must only be accessed and used for legitimate authorised purposes.

This includes:

- Customer information.
- Store Owner business records.
- Financial information.
- Supplier agreements.
- Platform credentials.
- Security information.
- Internal commercial plans.
- Unpublished product and marketing information.

Participants must not sell, disclose, copy or exploit confidential information without a lawful and authorised basis.

Suspected unauthorised access or disclosure must be reported through the applicable security incident process.

## 15. Reporting suspected misconduct

Anyone who reasonably suspects fraud, bribery, corruption or serious unethical conduct should report it through an approved reporting channel.

Reports should include, where available:

- Description of the suspected conduct.
- Relevant parties.
- Approximate date and time.
- Supporting records.
- Potential financial or operational impact.
- Any immediate risk.

Reports should be handled confidentially and shared only with people who need the information to assess or investigate the matter.

A report does not need to contain conclusive proof before it can be submitted.

## 16. Whistleblower protection

The parent company must not tolerate retaliation against a person who raises a concern in good faith or participates in a legitimate investigation.

Retaliation includes:

- Threats or intimidation.
- Unjustified account restrictions.
- Unfair commercial treatment.
- Dismissal or disciplinary action for making a protected disclosure.
- Deliberate withholding of legitimate payments.
- Harassment or exclusion.

Reports must be assessed objectively.

Knowingly fabricated allegations or deliberate abuse of reporting channels may themselves constitute misconduct.

Nothing in this policy limits protections available under applicable whistleblower or protected disclosure legislation.

## 17. Investigation and evidence preservation

Suspected misconduct must be investigated fairly and proportionately.

Investigations should:

1. Assess the credibility and seriousness of the report.
2. Identify relevant records and individuals.
3. Preserve relevant evidence.
4. Restrict access to sensitive information.
5. Avoid conflicts of interest.
6. Give affected parties a reasonable opportunity to respond where appropriate.
7. Record findings and supporting evidence.
8. Recommend proportionate corrective action.

Evidence must not be destroyed, altered or concealed to obstruct an investigation.

Where the matter involves possible criminal conduct, the parent company should obtain appropriate legal advice and consider referral to the relevant authorities.

## 18. Corrective and enforcement action

Depending on the findings and applicable agreements, action may include:

- Guidance or additional training.
- Correcting inaccurate records.
- Removing unauthorised access.
- Requiring repayment of improperly obtained funds.
- Suspending a specific activity.
- Formal warnings.
- Contractual remedies.
- Store or account restrictions.
- Termination of a business relationship.
- Referral to law enforcement or regulators.

Actions must be supported by evidence and consistent with applicable agreements and law.

The parent company must distinguish between suspected misconduct and established findings.

Temporary protective restrictions must not automatically be treated as proof of wrongdoing.

## 19. Record keeping and auditability

Material disclosures, investigations and decisions must be documented.

Records should include:

- Conflict disclosures.
- Relevant gift and hospitality declarations.
- Suspected misconduct reports.
- Investigation assignments.
- Evidence references.
- Findings.
- Decisions.
- Corrective actions.
- Approval records.

Records must be protected against unauthorised modification and retained according to the applicable information lifecycle requirements.

## 20. Training and awareness

The parent company should provide appropriate guidance to employees, administrators and Store Owners.

Training should cover:

- Recognising fraud.
- Payment integrity.
- Conflicts of interest.
- Bribery and corruption.
- Supplier integrity.
- Customer protection.
- Confidentiality.
- Reporting channels.
- Whistleblower protection.
- Investigation procedures.

Training depth should reflect the participant's responsibilities and level of access.

## 21. Implementation requirements

The platform and its operational processes should support:

- Role-based access.
- Financial approval controls.
- Payment verification.
- Audit logging.
- Supplier approval records.
- Conflict disclosures.
- Complaint and misconduct reporting.
- Evidence preservation.
- Privileged account monitoring.
- Separation of sensitive responsibilities.

Technical controls must support, rather than replace, clear organisational accountability.

## 22. Review and maintenance

This policy should be reviewed annually and whenever material changes affect:

- Applicable anti-corruption legislation.
- Financial controls.
- Supplier relationships.
- Store Owner agreements.
- Administrative permissions.
- Reporting and investigation processes.
- Platform commercial arrangements.

Material changes must be communicated to affected participants.

## 23. Governing principle

The platform must operate on trust supported by verifiable controls.

No commercial opportunity, personal relationship, financial benefit or position of authority justifies fraud, bribery, corruption or deliberate dishonesty.

Every participant is responsible for protecting the integrity of the businesses and relationships connected to the platform.
