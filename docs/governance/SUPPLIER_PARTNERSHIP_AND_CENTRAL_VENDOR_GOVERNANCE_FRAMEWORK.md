## 1. Purpose

This framework defines how the parent company establishes, manages and governs strategic supplier relationships and commercial partnerships that may benefit multiple participating stores.

It distinguishes central supplier partnerships from independent Store Owner supplier relationships and establishes the controls required when the parent company negotiates shared commercial arrangements.

The objective is to improve supplier access, purchasing conditions, product availability and operational efficiency without taking control of independent Store Owner businesses or relationships without agreement.

## 2. Core Principles

1. Central supplier partnerships must create identifiable value for participating stores.
2. Store Owners retain control over their independent supplier relationships.
3. Parent-company negotiations must not automatically bind every participating store.
4. Shared supplier arrangements require clear participation and commercial terms.
5. Supplier approval must be based on documented assessment.
6. Commercial terms and negotiated benefits must be transparent to authorised participants.
7. Conflicts of interest must be disclosed and managed.
8. Supplier relationships must not create undisclosed financial incentives.
9. Product quality, safety and legal compliance take priority over favourable pricing.
10. Every central supplier arrangement must have an accountable owner and review process.

## 3. Types of Central Partnerships

The parent company may establish several types of supplier and commercial partnerships.

### 3.1 Product Supply Partnerships

Agreements with manufacturers, wholesalers, distributors and producers to make products available to participating stores.

Possible benefits include:

- Preferential pricing.
- Improved product availability.
- Volume discounts.
- Consistent product specifications.
- Better delivery arrangements.
- Access to exclusive product ranges where legitimately agreed.

### 3.2 Logistics Partnerships

Agreements with courier companies, fulfilment providers, warehousing businesses and delivery partners.

Possible benefits include:

- Negotiated delivery rates.
- Collection arrangements.
- Shared fulfilment infrastructure.
- Delivery tracking.
- Returns handling.
- Improved delivery coverage.

### 3.3 Creative and Production Partnerships

Agreements with photographers, videographers, studios, printers, designers and production suppliers.

Possible benefits include:

- Preferential production rates.
- Shared production days.
- Equipment access.
- Specialist creative services.
- Consistent technical standards.

### 3.4 Technology Partnerships

Agreements with hosting providers, software vendors, payment providers, analytics providers and other technology partners.

These arrangements must be assessed for security, privacy, reliability, cost and contractual suitability.

### 3.5 Business Support Partnerships

Agreements with business-development organisations, training providers, professional advisers and entrepreneurship support programmes.

These may help participating Store Owners improve their business operations and capabilities.

## 4. Partnership Ownership and Authority

The parent company owns and manages its central supplier partnership programme.

The responsible parent-company representative must have authority to:

- Identify potential partners.
- Conduct supplier assessments.
- Negotiate proposed terms.
- Coordinate internal reviews.
- Submit agreements for approval.
- Monitor partner performance.
- Manage disputes and renewals.

Only authorised representatives may enter into agreements on behalf of the parent company.

A partnership representative must not promise that an individual Store Owner will participate unless that Store Owner has agreed.

## 5. Store Owner Participation

Participation in a central supplier arrangement may be:

- Optional.
- Required for a specifically agreed service.
- Required for a documented platform-wide operational standard.
- Limited to selected stores.
- Subject to separate onboarding.

Before a Store Owner participates, the relevant terms must be communicated.

Where participation creates a financial obligation, contractual commitment or change to existing supplier arrangements, explicit agreement must be obtained.

The Store Owner must understand:

- Which supplier is involved.
- What products or services are covered.
- What pricing applies.
- Who pays the supplier.
- Who is responsible for delivery.
- What happens when goods are defective.
- How disputes are handled.
- Whether participation can be withdrawn.

## 6. Supplier Assessment

Before entering into a material central partnership, the parent company must assess the supplier.

The assessment should consider:

### 6.1 Business Legitimacy

- Supplier identity.
- Business details where applicable.
- Relevant registration.
- Authority to supply the products or services.
- Relevant licences and permits.

### 6.2 Product and Service Quality

- Product specifications.
- Quality assurance.
- Authenticity.
- Safety.
- Service capability.
- Warranty arrangements.
- Returns and replacement procedures.

### 6.3 Commercial Suitability

- Pricing.
- Payment terms.
- Minimum order quantities.
- Delivery costs.
- Contract duration.
- Volume commitments.
- Cancellation conditions.
- Financial stability where relevant.

### 6.4 Operational Capability

- Available capacity.
- Geographic coverage.
- Delivery reliability.
- Customer support.
- Stock availability.
- Business continuity arrangements.

### 6.5 Legal and Compliance Risk

- Applicable consumer protection requirements.
- Product-specific regulation.
- Privacy and data protection.
- Intellectual property.
- Employment and subcontracting considerations where relevant.
- Anti-fraud and anti-corruption controls.

The depth of assessment must be proportionate to the value and risk of the arrangement.

## 7. Partnership Approval

Material partnerships must receive documented approval before execution.

The approval record should include:

- Partnership name.
- Supplier identity.
- Partnership category.
- Commercial purpose.
- Expected benefits.
- Participating stores.
- Financial commitments.
- Contract duration.
- Risk assessment.
- Conflict-of-interest declarations.
- Approving authority.
- Approval date.

A proposed partnership must not be presented as approved before the required approvals are complete.

## 8. Commercial Negotiation

The parent company may negotiate shared terms on behalf of participating stores where authorised.

Negotiations may cover:

- Wholesale pricing.
- Volume discounts.
- Minimum order quantities.
- Delivery fees.
- Payment terms.
- Credit arrangements.
- Product availability.
- Service levels.
- Warranty support.
- Returns.
- Marketing support.
- Exclusive arrangements.

Negotiated benefits must be accurately represented.

If the supplier provides a discount or rebate to the parent company, the agreement must state how that benefit is treated.

The parent company must not advertise a saving to Store Owners that it cannot substantiate.

## 9. Financial Models

Central partnerships may use different financial arrangements.

### 9.1 Direct Store Purchasing

Each Store Owner contracts with and pays the supplier independently.

The parent company negotiates shared terms but does not become the buyer.

### 9.2 Parent Company Purchasing

The parent company purchases products or services and resells or allocates them to participating stores under an agreed commercial arrangement.

The arrangement must define:

- Ownership of purchased goods.
- Transfer of ownership.
- Pricing.
- Payment terms.
- Delivery responsibility.
- Risk of loss.
- Returns.
- Tax treatment.
- Financial reconciliation.

### 9.3 Coordinated Group Purchasing

The parent company coordinates a collective purchase while each participating store has a documented allocation.

The arrangement must specify:

- Participating stores.
- Individual quantities.
- Total order quantity.
- Unit pricing.
- Shared charges.
- Payment responsibility.
- Delivery allocation.
- Short-delivery treatment.
- Refund and credit allocation.

### 9.4 Referral or Commission Arrangement

The parent company may receive a referral fee or commission where explicitly agreed with the supplier.

The arrangement must be documented and disclosed where required.

A referral payment must not influence product recommendations in a way that misleads Store Owners or customers.

## 10. Supplier Exclusivity

Exclusive supplier arrangements require special review.

Before accepting exclusivity, the parent company must assess:

- Whether the arrangement benefits participating stores.
- Whether alternative suppliers are available.
- Whether product availability is reliable.
- Whether pricing remains competitive.
- Whether the restriction is commercially reasonable.
- Whether the arrangement creates unfair dependence.
- Whether individual Store Owners are affected.
- Whether competition or other legal requirements apply.

Exclusivity must not be imposed on a Store Owner through an informal announcement.

Any binding restriction must be included in an agreement accepted by the affected party and reviewed where necessary by legal advisers.

## 11. Conflicts of Interest

Potential conflicts must be declared before a partnership is approved.

Examples include:

- A decision-maker having a financial interest in a supplier.
- A supplier being owned by a related party.
- Personal commissions or referral benefits.
- Preferential allocation to a related business.
- A supplier relationship influencing product selection.
- Undisclosed gifts or incentives.

The parent company must record:

- The nature of the conflict.
- The person declaring it.
- The affected decision.
- The mitigation applied.
- The approving authority.

A person with an unmanaged material conflict must not make the final approval decision.

## 12. Supplier Performance Monitoring

Central partners must be reviewed periodically.

Monitoring may include:

- Delivery performance.
- Product quality.
- Service availability.
- Pricing consistency.
- Dispute frequency.
- Warranty performance.
- Customer complaints.
- Communication quality.
- Contract compliance.
- Financial reliability.

Material failures must trigger an improvement plan or further review.

The parent company may suspend or remove a supplier from its approved network where justified.

A central supplier restriction must be communicated to affected stores.

## 13. Partner Service Levels

Material agreements should define measurable service expectations.

These may include:

- Order acknowledgement.
- Quotation turnaround.
- Delivery timelines.
- Support response.
- Product replacement.
- Fault resolution.
- Reporting.
- Incident notification.

Service levels must be realistic and agreed with the supplier.

The parent company must not promise Store Owners a service level that the supplier has not committed to.

## 14. Data Sharing and Confidentiality

Supplier partnerships may require limited access to store, customer or transaction information.

Any data sharing must have a defined purpose and lawful basis.

The parties must agree:

- Information being shared.
- Purpose of processing.
- Permitted users.
- Security controls.
- Retention period.
- Confidentiality.
- Incident notification.
- Deletion or return of information.
- Restrictions on onward disclosure.

Customer information must not be shared with suppliers merely because they participate in a central partnership.

Only the minimum necessary information may be shared for an authorised transaction or service.

The arrangement must comply with applicable POPIA requirements.

## 15. Intellectual Property and Brand Use

A supplier must not use a Store Owner's brand, logo, product content or customer-facing material without appropriate permission.

The parent company must not grant a supplier rights over Store Owner assets beyond the permissions actually received.

Partnership agreements should specify:

- Approved brand assets.
- Permitted use.
- Channels.
- Duration.
- Geographic scope where relevant.
- Approval requirements.
- Withdrawal of permission.
- Treatment of material after termination.

The same principles apply to the parent company's own brand and intellectual property.

## 16. Partnership Marketing

The parent company may coordinate campaigns involving central suppliers and participating stores.

Campaigns must define:

- Participating stores.
- Supplier contributions.
- Products promoted.
- Campaign objective.
- Discount funding.
- Advertising expenditure.
- Creative responsibilities.
- Brand approvals.
- Content rights.
- Performance reporting.

Each participating store must remain identifiable as its own brand.

Supplier-funded promotion must not create misleading endorsements or undisclosed commercial relationships.

## 17. Partnership Disputes

Disputes may involve:

- Pricing disagreements.
- Supplier non-performance.
- Product defects.
- Late delivery.
- Incorrect invoices.
- Payment disputes.
- Contract interpretation.
- Data misuse.
- Intellectual property infringement.

The responsible parent-company representative must:

1. Record the dispute.
2. Identify affected stores.
3. Preserve relevant evidence.
4. Review the applicable agreement.
5. Contact the supplier.
6. Record the supplier's response.
7. Assess financial and operational impact.
8. Agree or escalate a resolution.
9. Communicate relevant outcomes to affected stores.

Material legal or financial disputes must be escalated to the appropriate adviser or authority.

## 18. Renewal and Termination

Every material partnership must have a documented review and renewal process.

Before renewal, assess:

- Performance.
- Cost.
- Store Owner feedback.
- Product or service quality.
- Compliance.
- Outstanding disputes.
- Commercial value.
- Alternative suppliers.

Termination planning must address:

- Outstanding orders.
- Unpaid invoices.
- Customer obligations.
- Stock in transit.
- Warranty commitments.
- Data return or deletion.
- Intellectual property permissions.
- Service continuity.
- Store Owner communication.

Terminating a central partnership must not automatically terminate a Store Owner's independent supplier relationship unless the relevant agreement provides a valid basis.

## 19. Supplier Relationship Records

The parent company must maintain a central partnership register.

Each record should include:

- Partnership ID.
- Supplier ID.
- Partnership category.
- Contracting parties.
- Agreement reference.
- Partnership owner.
- Approval status.
- Participating stores.
- Commercial model.
- Financial terms.
- Effective date.
- Review date.
- Expiry date.
- Conflict declarations.
- Risk assessment.
- Performance history.
- Dispute history.
- Termination details.

Records must be access-controlled and auditable.

## 20. Reporting and Governance Review

Management should review:

- Active central partnerships.
- Participating stores.
- Commercial benefits.
- Supplier performance.
- Partnership costs.
- Rebates and commissions.
- Conflicts of interest.
- Open disputes.
- Contract renewals.
- Supplier concentration.
- Compliance issues.

Store Owners should receive relevant information about partnerships that affect their operations or financial commitments.

Confidential supplier information must remain restricted to authorised users.

## 21. Testing Requirements

Where partnership functionality is implemented in the platform, test that:

1. Central partnerships are distinct from store-specific suppliers.
2. Store participation requires the appropriate authority.
3. Participation terms are recorded.
4. Supplier approval status is enforced.
5. Financial arrangements are traceable.
6. Shared purchasing allocations are store-specific.
7. Conflicts of interest are recorded.
8. Supplier restrictions are communicated.
9. Store-specific supplier relationships remain independent.
10. Supplier data access follows permissions.
11. Customer information is not exposed unnecessarily.
12. Partnership termination preserves outstanding obligations.
13. Audit history captures material decisions.
14. Reports do not expose unauthorised commercial information.

## 22. Implementation Guidance

Before implementation:

- Review the supplier onboarding and procurement standards.
- Review store-specific supplier records.
- Review financial settlement and reconciliation.
- Review Store Owner agreements.
- Review data protection controls.
- Review central marketing and creative production agreements.
- Identify existing supplier and partnership functionality.

Do not create a second supplier registry if the existing supplier architecture can support central partnership records and store participation relationships.

Any schema changes must include migrations, integrity constraints, appropriate indexes, rollback considerations and automated tests.

## 23. Governing Rule

The parent company may build strategic supplier partnerships that create value across the platform.

Those partnerships must remain transparent, commercially accountable and voluntary where individual store participation is optional.

The parent company coordinates shared opportunities. Store Owners retain their independent supplier relationships and make their own purchasing decisions within agreed platform and legal requirements.
