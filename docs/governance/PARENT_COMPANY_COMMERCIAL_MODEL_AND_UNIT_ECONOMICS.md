# Parent Company Commercial Model and Unit Economics

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the proposed commercial model through which the parent company provides e-commerce infrastructure, central creative production, operational support and optional marketing services to participating Store Owners.

The model must balance three objectives:

1. Give entrepreneurs access to professional digital commerce infrastructure without requiring them to build and maintain it independently.
2. Allow Store Owners to operate and grow their individual store identities with meaningful control over their brands and business decisions.
3. Keep the parent company financially sustainable so it can maintain, improve and expand the shared platform and services.

The commercial model must be transparent. Store Owners must understand what they pay, what is included, what costs extra and how any future transaction-based charges are calculated.

All prices in this document are preliminary planning examples. They are not approved final prices or binding offers.

## 2. Commercial Model

The proposed model is a hybrid of:

- A modest recurring subscription.
- Optional marketing and creative production services.
- A possible future transaction fee, introduced only when the platform can reliably record and reconcile qualifying transactions.

The subscription provides access to the core platform and defined support. Optional services allow Store Owners to purchase additional assistance according to their needs and budgets.

The parent company must not depend on unlimited unpaid labour or assume that every Store Owner will purchase additional services.

## 3. Subscription Structure

### 3.1 What the subscription covers

The subscription is intended to cover the agreed core service package, which may include:

- Storefront hosting and infrastructure allocation.
- Access to the assigned e-commerce storefront.
- Core catalogue and product management functionality.
- Standard checkout and order-management functionality supported by the platform.
- Routine platform maintenance.
- Security updates and infrastructure monitoring.
- Core platform improvements made available to the relevant subscription tier.
- Defined technical support.
- Basic operational reporting.
- Standard storefront media optimisation and delivery.
- A defined basic visual onboarding allowance, where included in the selected plan.

The subscription does not automatically include unlimited custom development, advertising, campaign management, photography, videography, graphic design or other professional services.

### 3.2 Subscription boundaries

Each subscription tier must have a written service description.

The description should identify:

- Included platform features.
- Hosting and storage allowances, where applicable.
- Support channels and support hours.
- Expected response targets.
- Included onboarding assistance.
- Included visual production allowance, if any.
- Reporting available to the Store Owner.
- Excluded services.
- Any usage limits or additional charges.

The parent company must not advertise a service as included if it is only available through an additional paid service.

### 3.3 Preliminary subscription examples

The following figures are planning examples only. They must be validated against actual infrastructure costs, support requirements, operating capacity and the affordability of the target market.

| Illustrative plan | Example monthly price | Intended positioning |
|---|---:|---|
| Pilot | R0 for 30 days | Limited evaluation period |
| Standard | R599 | Core storefront and standard support |
| Growth | R899 | Higher service allowance and additional support or reporting |

These examples do not establish final plan entitlements.

The parent company must not introduce a higher tier merely by changing the price. Each tier must provide a clearly documented difference in service or allowance.

## 4. Trial and Pilot Programme

### 4.1 Trial period

A participating Store Owner may be offered a 30-day trial.

The trial should allow both parties to evaluate:

- Storefront functionality.
- Product catalogue setup.
- Checkout and order workflows.
- Store Owner dashboard access.
- Operational support.
- Media quality and presentation.
- The Store Owner's readiness to operate the store.
- Whether the commercial arrangement is sustainable.

### 4.2 No automatic paid conversion

A free trial must not silently convert into a paid subscription.

Before the trial ends, the parent company must provide the Store Owner with the applicable subscription options and terms.

The Store Owner must explicitly accept the selected paid plan before billing begins.

If no paid plan is accepted, the parties must follow the agreed trial-expiry and store-suspension procedure.

### 4.3 Pilot cohort

The parent company should begin with a manageable pilot group rather than immediately onboarding a large number of Store Owners.

The pilot should measure:

- Actual cost per store.
- Onboarding time.
- Support time per store.
- Infrastructure usage.
- Creative production costs.
- Store activation and launch rates.
- Order activity.
- Store Owner satisfaction.
- Retention and willingness to pay.

Pilot findings must inform the final pricing structure.

## 5. Optional Marketing and Creative Services

The parent company operates a central marketing and creative function that supports participating stores.

This function includes both technical storefront media production and optional commercial marketing services. These are related but distinct activities and must be priced and recorded separately.

### 5.1 Central creative production

The central creative team is responsible for producing and preparing platform-ready visual assets.

This may include:

- Product photography.
- Product videos.
- Product detail imagery.
- Storefront banners.
- Category imagery.
- Other approved storefront media.

The team must follow the platform's technical requirements for dimensions, aspect ratios, file formats, compression, responsive variants, accessibility and visual quality.

The Store Owner contributes product knowledge, brand direction, styling preferences, references and approval.

### 5.2 Basic visual onboarding allowance

A subscription may include a limited basic visual onboarding allowance.

The allowance must specify:

- Number of products or assets included.
- Number of images per product, where relevant.
- Whether basic editing and optimisation are included.
- Whether video is included.
- Number of revisions.
- Production location and logistics.
- What happens when the allowance is exceeded.

The allowance must be realistic and financially sustainable.

The subscription must not be interpreted as unlimited photography, videography or creative production.

### 5.3 Additional production

Additional photography and video production may be charged through:

- A published rate card.
- A defined production package.
- A per-product or per-session fee.
- A written quotation for larger or specialised projects.

The Store Owner must approve the scope and price before production begins.

Where several stores participate in a shared production day, costs may be reduced through shared logistics. Each Store Owner must still receive a clear record of their own deliverables and charges.

### 5.4 Optional marketing services

Marketing services may include:

- Campaign strategy and planning.
- Social media content.
- Promotional graphics.
- Advertising creative.
- Copywriting.
- Campaign management.
- Product launches.
- Seasonal promotions.
- Brand refinement.
- Photography and video campaigns.
- Customer acquisition initiatives.

These services are optional unless a specific service is expressly included in a written plan or agreement.

### 5.5 Illustrative service pricing

The following ranges are preliminary examples for financial planning. They require market research, cost validation and approval before publication.

| Service | Illustrative price range |
|---|---:|
| Promotional graphic | R150–R350 |
| Promotional copywriting | R150–R400 |
| Campaign planning | R350–R750 |
| Social content package | R750–R1,500 |
| Campaign management | R750–R2,000 |
| Brand refinement | R1,000–R3,500 |
| Photography coordination | Quoted according to scope |

The final rate card must explain the deliverables, turnaround expectations, revision limits and exclusions for each service.

### 5.6 Advertising expenditure

Advertising spend is separate from the parent company's marketing or campaign-management fee.

Every campaign proposal must identify:

- The service fee.
- The proposed advertising budget.
- The platform on which advertising will run.
- The campaign duration.
- The intended audience.
- The campaign objective.
- The party responsible for funding the advertising account.
- The reporting that will be provided.

The parent company must not present advertising expenditure as its own service revenue.

No campaign should incur advertising expenditure without the required approval and funding arrangement.

## 6. Parent Company Marketing Function

The parent company may promote participating stores through its own marketing channels and campaigns.

This may include:

- Introducing participating Store Owners and their businesses.
- Promoting product categories across multiple stores.
- Seasonal campaigns.
- Individual store or product campaigns.
- Entrepreneur stories.
- Shared promotional events.
- Marketing the parent company's own platform and services.

Each participating store must remain recognisable as its own brand and business identity.

Marketing material must not imply that the parent company created, owns or independently operates a Store Owner's brand where that is not the agreed position.

Campaigns must follow documented consent, approval, attribution and content-rights procedures.

Where campaign costs are shared, the allocation method must be disclosed and recorded.

The parent company must report campaign activity and results accurately. It must not guarantee sales, reach, conversions or other outcomes that depend on external market conditions.

## 7. Future Transaction Fee

### 7.1 Conditional introduction

A transaction fee may be considered in a later phase.

It must not be charged until the platform can reliably identify, record and reconcile qualifying completed transactions.

The parent company must first establish that the platform can distinguish between:

- Completed and paid orders.
- Pending orders.
- Failed payments.
- Cancelled orders.
- Fully or partially refunded orders.
- Duplicate orders.
- Cash and EFT transactions.
- WhatsApp or manually recorded sales.
- Transactions processed through external payment providers.

### 7.2 Separate written acceptance

A transaction fee must not be introduced through an undocumented dashboard change or an assumed subscription update.

Before implementation, the Store Owner must receive a written schedule explaining:

- The fee percentage or fixed amount.
- The calculation basis.
- Which transactions qualify.
- How refunds and cancellations are handled.
- Whether delivery charges are included.
- How discounts and promotions affect the calculation.
- How payment-provider fees are treated.
- How cash, EFT and manually recorded transactions are treated.
- The reconciliation and reporting process.
- Payment and dispute procedures.
- The effective date.

The Store Owner must explicitly accept the transaction fee terms in writing before the fee is applied.

### 7.3 Illustrative future rate

A possible planning range is 0.5% to 1% of qualifying completed transactions.

This is not an approved fee.

The parent company must evaluate whether a transaction fee is necessary and fair after reviewing actual platform costs, payment processing arrangements, reporting reliability and the effect on Store Owner margins.

The fee must not be charged twice on the same transaction because of duplicated records or multiple fulfilment events.

## 8. Unit Economics

The parent company must understand the actual cost of operating each participating store.

Revenue alone does not establish profitability.

### 8.1 Direct cost per store

Direct costs may include:

- Hosting and infrastructure allocation.
- Database and storage usage.
- Image and video delivery.
- Email and messaging services.
- Payment or platform service costs borne by the parent company.
- Store-specific support labour.
- Onboarding labour.
- Included creative production.
- Monitoring and maintenance attributable to the store.

Shared central costs should be recorded separately rather than arbitrarily assigned to individual stores.

### 8.2 Contribution per store

The basic calculation is:

Contribution per store = Monthly subscription revenue - Direct monthly cost to serve that store.

Optional service revenue should be recorded separately, with its own delivery costs and contribution.

Transaction-fee revenue, if introduced, must also be tracked separately.

### 8.3 Illustrative calculation

The following example is hypothetical and does not represent measured operating costs.

| Item | Example |
|---|---:|
| Monthly subscription | R599 |
| Direct monthly cost per store | R200 |
| Monthly contribution per store | R399 |

If the parent company's fixed monthly operating overhead is R15,000:

Break-even store count = Fixed monthly overhead / Contribution per store.

R15,000 / R399 = approximately 38 stores.

This calculation assumes that each store generates the same contribution and that fixed overhead remains unchanged.

Actual break-even analysis must account for different usage levels, support requirements, subscription tiers, service revenue, taxes and unexpected costs.

### 8.4 Cost categories

The finance function should maintain separate cost categories for:

1. Shared platform infrastructure.
2. Store-specific infrastructure usage.
3. Development and engineering.
4. Security and compliance.
5. Customer and Store Owner support.
6. Onboarding.
7. Photography and videography.
8. Creative editing and production.
9. Marketing and campaign management.
10. Advertising expenditure.
11. Administration and governance.
12. Payment processing and financial operations.

This separation makes it possible to identify which services are profitable and which require pricing or process changes.

## 9. Financial Monitoring

The parent company should maintain a monthly commercial performance report.

At minimum, it should record:

- Active Store Owners.
- Active stores.
- Trial stores.
- Paid subscriptions.
- Subscription revenue.
- Optional service revenue.
- Transaction-fee revenue, if applicable.
- Direct cost per store.
- Contribution per store.
- Shared operating costs.
- Total monthly operating expenditure.
- Outstanding subscription payments.
- Refunds and credits.
- Store churn.
- Trial-to-paid conversion.
- Average support time per store.
- Creative production hours and costs.
- Marketing service delivery costs.

The report should distinguish recurring revenue from one-off service revenue.

A store that purchases a large once-off campaign must not be treated as proof that recurring subscription pricing is sustainable.

## 10. Pricing Validation and Review

Before final prices are approved, the parent company must:

1. Measure actual infrastructure costs.
2. Record onboarding and support time.
3. Measure creative production costs.
4. Evaluate the affordability of proposed prices for the target market.
5. Compare the scope of services with relevant market alternatives.
6. Model low, expected and high usage scenarios.
7. Calculate contribution margins.
8. Test break-even requirements.
9. Review the impact of VAT and other applicable taxes.
10. Obtain management approval.

Prices and service allowances should be reviewed periodically.

Any material price change must be communicated in advance and handled according to the Store Owner's agreement and applicable law.

A price change must not silently reduce an existing Store Owner's agreed service entitlements.

## 11. Store Owner Commercial Responsibilities

Each Store Owner must:

- Select and accept an applicable subscription plan.
- Pay agreed subscription charges by the due date.
- Approve optional service quotations before work begins.
- Approve advertising budgets before expenditure.
- Supply accurate product and business information.
- Maintain lawful product listings and required permissions.
- Cooperate with order, payment and fulfilment processes.
- Notify the parent company of material changes affecting store operations.
- Review invoices and raise disputes within the agreed process.

The parent company must provide clear invoices, service descriptions and payment records.

## 12. Parent Company Commercial Responsibilities

The parent company must:

- Provide the agreed platform and services.
- Maintain the infrastructure within the agreed service scope.
- Explain subscription limits and exclusions.
- Record subscription and service charges accurately.
- Obtain approval for optional paid work.
- Keep advertising spend separate from service fees.
- Provide transaction reports where applicable.
- Maintain transparent records of additional charges.
- Give reasonable notice of material service or pricing changes.
- Handle billing disputes through a documented process.
- Protect Store Owner and customer information.
- Avoid charging for services that were neither included in the subscription nor separately approved.

## 13. Ownership, Brand and Commercial Separation

Payment of a subscription or optional service fee does not transfer ownership of a Store Owner's brand, social media audience, customer relationships or underlying business to the parent company.

The Store Owner's brand and underlying intellectual property must be governed by the relevant brand and IP agreements.

The parent company's ownership of its platform, infrastructure, software and shared systems remains separate.

The commercial agreement must distinguish:

- Platform access.
- Store operation.
- Brand ownership and usage rights.
- Customer data responsibilities.
- Creative production rights.
- Marketing service delivery.
- Advertising account ownership.
- Payment collection and reconciliation.

A subscription must not be interpreted as a transfer of ownership of the Store Owner's independent brand identity.

## 14. Billing, Suspension and Disputes

The commercial agreements must define:

- Billing frequency.
- Payment methods.
- Invoice requirements.
- Due dates.
- Late-payment handling.
- Grace periods.
- Dispute procedures.
- Suspension conditions.
- Reinstatement procedures.
- Cancellation and notice periods.
- Refund and credit rules.
- Treatment of outstanding balances.

Suspension must be proportionate and must not result in arbitrary deletion of Store Owner data or historical business records.

Where a store is suspended or a Store Owner exits, the parent company must follow the documented data retention, export, handover and exit procedures.

## 15. Exit and Handover

The commercial model must support an orderly exit.

The agreement package should explain:

- How a Store Owner may terminate the arrangement.
- How much notice is required.
- Which charges remain payable.
- How optional service work in progress is handled.
- How customer orders already accepted are fulfilled.
- How outstanding payments and refunds are reconciled.
- How Store Owner data and permitted exports are provided.
- How brand and creative rights are handled.
- How access to the dashboard and other systems is withdrawn.
- How the store is prepared for a successor, if applicable.

The Store Owner's departure does not automatically terminate the existence of the store as a platform asset.

The parent company must preserve the store's historical records and attribution of previous Store Owners' contributions in accordance with applicable privacy, contractual and retention requirements.

## 16. Contractual Document Set

The commercial model should be implemented through a coordinated set of agreements and schedules.

The proposed document set includes:

1. Store Owner Agreement.
2. Subscription Agreement and Service Schedule.
3. Trial and Pilot Agreement.
4. Marketing Services Agreement.
5. Creative Production Terms.
6. Transaction Fee Schedule, if introduced.
7. Data Processing and Privacy Terms.
8. Brand and Intellectual Property Terms.
9. Advertising Budget and Campaign Approval Terms.
10. Exit, Data Export and Handover Terms.

These documents must be consistent with one another.

A Store Owner should not be required to accept a transaction fee, optional marketing package or additional production service merely to obtain the core subscription unless that requirement is clearly stated and separately accepted.

## 17. South African Legal and Tax Review

The final commercial agreements and pricing structure must be reviewed for compliance with applicable South African law.

Review areas include:

- Contract formation and enforceability.
- Consumer Protection Act applicability.
- Electronic Communications and Transactions Act requirements.
- Protection of Personal Information Act responsibilities.
- Value-added tax treatment.
- Income tax and invoicing obligations.
- Advertising and marketing requirements.
- Intellectual property and copyright.
- Payment processing and financial reporting.
- Sector-specific product and trading requirements.

VAT must not be added or described as applicable without confirming the parent company's VAT registration status and the correct tax treatment.

The parent company should obtain advice from a South African commercial attorney and tax practitioner before publishing final agreements or prices.

This document is an internal commercial planning standard, not legal or tax advice.

## 18. Implementation Sequence

The recommended implementation sequence is:

### Phase 1: Cost visibility

- Record platform and infrastructure costs.
- Track support and onboarding time.
- Track creative production costs.
- Establish a basic monthly commercial report.

### Phase 2: Pilot pricing

- Select a small pilot group.
- Apply the agreed trial process.
- Measure actual cost to serve.
- Test proposed subscription tiers.
- Gather Store Owner feedback.

### Phase 3: Commercial launch

- Approve final subscription prices.
- Publish service schedules and exclusions.
- Finalise the contract package.
- Establish billing and dispute procedures.
- Begin recurring commercial reporting.

### Phase 4: Optional services

- Publish the marketing and creative rate card.
- Establish quotation and approval workflows.
- Separate advertising budgets from service revenue.
- Measure profitability by service type.

### Phase 5: Transaction fee assessment

- Validate transaction recording and reconciliation.
- Test refunds, cancellations and manual payment scenarios.
- Prepare a transparent fee schedule.
- Obtain explicit written acceptance.
- Introduce the fee only after the required controls are operational.

## 19. Final Standard

The parent company's commercial model must make professional e-commerce infrastructure accessible while ensuring that the platform and its support functions remain financially sustainable.

The subscription pays for a defined core service. Additional marketing and creative work is separately scoped and priced. Any future transaction fee requires reliable reconciliation and explicit written acceptance.

The parent company must be transparent about costs, services, ownership, responsibilities and performance.

Store Owners must retain meaningful control over their assigned store identities and must never be misled about what they own, what they are paying for or what the parent company controls.
