## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes standards for recruiting, engaging, managing and developing people who perform work for the parent company.

The objective is to build a capable, accountable and professional workforce that can support the platform, participating stores and the company's long-term growth.

The policy distinguishes employees, independent contractors, consultants and external service providers. Their legal status must be determined by the actual working relationship and applicable law, not merely by the title used in an agreement.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to:

- Permanent employees.
- Fixed-term employees.
- Part-time employees.
- Temporary employees.
- Independent contractors.
- Consultants.
- Interns and trainees.
- Agency-provided personnel.
- Other individuals performing work on behalf of the parent company.

Store Owners are independent business participants and are not automatically employees or contractors of the parent company.

## 3. Workforce management principles

The parent company must follow these principles:

1. Fair treatment: individuals must be treated professionally and without unlawful discrimination.
2. Merit: recruitment and development decisions must consider relevant skills and business requirements.
3. Clarity: responsibilities, authority and expected deliverables must be documented.
4. Accountability: work must have identifiable owners and measurable outcomes.
5. Legal compliance: employment and contracting arrangements must comply with applicable law.
6. Confidentiality: access to business information must be controlled.
7. Development: people should have reasonable opportunities to improve relevant skills.
8. Proportionality: workforce structures must reflect the company's actual size and operating capacity.

## 4. Workforce planning

Before creating a role or engaging a contractor, management should identify:

- The business requirement.
- The work to be performed.
- Required skills and experience.
- Expected workload.
- Reporting relationship.
- Budget.
- Required access to systems and information.
- Whether the work is ongoing or project-based.
- Whether the work should be performed by an employee or an external service provider.

Workforce decisions must be based on genuine operational needs.

The company should avoid creating roles that duplicate existing responsibilities without a clear business reason.

## 5. Role descriptions

Every employee position should have a documented role description.

Where appropriate, contractor engagements should have a statement of work.

These documents should identify:

- Role or service title.
- Purpose.
- Main responsibilities.
- Required qualifications or skills.
- Reporting or communication arrangements.
- Decision-making authority.
- Performance expectations.
- Relevant confidentiality obligations.
- Access requirements.
- Scope limitations.

A role description must not be used to impose responsibilities that have not been properly agreed or lawfully assigned.

## 6. Recruitment and selection

Recruitment should follow a consistent and fair process.

The process may include:

1. Approval of the role and budget.
2. Preparation of a role description.
3. Publication or distribution of the opportunity.
4. Application review.
5. Relevant interviews or assessments.
6. Reference or qualification checks where justified and lawful.
7. Selection decision.
8. Written offer or engagement agreement.
9. Pre-engagement administration.
10. Onboarding.

Selection criteria must be relevant to the role.

The company must not use unlawful discriminatory criteria.

Where personal information is collected during recruitment, it must be handled according to applicable privacy requirements.

## 7. Employment and contractor classification

The parent company must correctly determine the legal nature of each working relationship.

The classification should consider the actual circumstances, including:

- Degree of control.
- Independence in performing the work.
- Working arrangements.
- Economic dependence.
- Integration into the business.
- Responsibility for equipment and expenses.
- Contractual obligations.
- Applicable statutory tests.

Calling a person a contractor does not automatically make the relationship an independent contracting arrangement.

Where classification is uncertain, the company should obtain appropriate employment and tax advice before engagement.

## 8. Employment agreements

Employees must receive written employment terms that comply with applicable South African labour legislation.

Depending on the role, the agreement should address:

- Job title and duties.
- Commencement date.
- Place or method of work.
- Working hours.
- Remuneration.
- Leave.
- Probation where lawful and applicable.
- Benefits where applicable.
- Confidentiality.
- Intellectual property.
- Notice and termination.
- Dispute procedures.
- Applicable policies.

The company must comply with statutory minimum conditions regardless of whether a particular term is included in an agreement.

## 9. Contractor agreements

Contractor engagements should be documented through an appropriate agreement.

The agreement should define:

- Scope of work.
- Deliverables.
- Fees and payment terms.
- Project timelines.
- Acceptance criteria.
- Responsibilities of each party.
- Confidentiality.
- Data protection.
- Intellectual property ownership or licensing.
- Security obligations.
- Subcontracting restrictions where relevant.
- Termination.
- Dispute resolution.

Contractors must not receive broader system access than necessary to perform their agreed work.

Contractor arrangements must not be used to avoid employment obligations where the actual relationship is legally one of employment.

## 10. Onboarding

Every new employee or contractor must complete onboarding appropriate to their responsibilities.

Onboarding should cover:

- Company structure.
- Role responsibilities.
- Reporting arrangements.
- Applicable policies.
- Security awareness.
- Confidentiality.
- Data protection.
- Acceptable system use.
- Communication procedures.
- Escalation channels.
- Required tools and access.

Access must only be granted after the necessary approvals.

The onboarding process should record completed requirements and outstanding actions.

## 11. Performance and accountability

Employees and contractors must understand the expected outcomes of their work.

Performance expectations should be based on:

- Role responsibilities.
- Agreed deliverables.
- Work quality.
- Reliability.
- Professional conduct.
- Relevant performance indicators.
- Compliance with applicable policies.

Performance reviews should be proportionate to the nature and duration of the engagement.

Contractors should primarily be assessed against agreed contractual deliverables and service obligations, subject to the actual legal relationship.

The company must not use performance metrics to encourage unsafe shortcuts, dishonest reporting or poor customer treatment.

## 12. Working standards and professional conduct

People working for the parent company must:

- Act honestly.
- Communicate professionally.
- Respect colleagues and business partners.
- Protect confidential information.
- Follow lawful instructions.
- Use company resources responsibly.
- Disclose conflicts of interest.
- Report serious misconduct.
- Avoid harassment, intimidation and discrimination.
- Maintain appropriate professional boundaries.

The applicable Ethical Conduct, Anti-Fraud and Anti-Bribery Policy must also be followed.

## 13. Training and development

The parent company should identify training requirements according to operational needs.

Relevant areas may include:

- Platform operations.
- Software development.
- Cybersecurity.
- Privacy and POPIA.
- Customer support.
- Financial reconciliation.
- Marketing.
- Creative production.
- Product management.
- Leadership.
- Risk management.

Training should be documented where it is required for a role or access permission.

The company should encourage skills development that improves both individual capability and business performance.

## 14. Working time, leave and availability

Employee working hours, overtime, rest periods and leave must comply with applicable labour legislation and the employment agreement.

Contractor availability and delivery expectations must be defined in the relevant statement of work.

The company must not assume that an independent contractor is subject to employee working-time arrangements merely because regular communication or delivery milestones are required.

Remote and flexible working arrangements should define reasonable expectations for communication, availability and information security.

## 15. Remuneration and payments

Employee remuneration must be administered according to employment agreements and applicable statutory requirements.

Contractor payments must follow the agreed contractual terms.

The company should maintain appropriate records for:

- Approved remuneration.
- Contractor invoices.
- Payment approvals.
- Deductions where legally permitted.
- Reimbursements.
- Tax-related administration.
- Payment dates.

No unauthorised person may change remuneration or approve payments outside their assigned authority.

## 16. Workplace health, safety and wellbeing

The parent company must meet applicable workplace health and safety obligations.

Relevant measures may include:

- Safe working conditions.
- Appropriate equipment.
- Ergonomic guidance.
- Incident reporting.
- Risk assessments.
- Emergency procedures.
- Reasonable workload management.
- Appropriate support for remote workers.

The company's responsibilities must be assessed according to the actual work arrangement and applicable legislation.

## 17. Confidentiality and information security

Employees and contractors must protect information accessed through their work.

This includes:

- Customer information.
- Store Owner information.
- Financial records.
- Supplier agreements.
- Source code.
- Credentials.
- Business plans.
- Security documentation.
- Internal communications.
- Unpublished marketing material.

Information must only be accessed for authorised work purposes.

Credentials must not be shared.

Suspected data exposure, unauthorised access or loss of company equipment must be reported immediately through the relevant incident process.

## 18. Intellectual property and work product

Employment and contractor agreements must clearly address intellectual property rights.

The company should identify:

- Work created during the engagement.
- Existing intellectual property brought into the engagement.
- Third-party materials.
- Ownership or licensing arrangements.
- Permitted portfolio use.
- Confidentiality restrictions.
- Source file and deliverable handover requirements.

The company must not assume that all intellectual property created by an independent contractor automatically belongs to it.

Ownership and usage rights must be documented in accordance with applicable law.

Store Owner brand assets, product materials and creative contributions must be treated according to the relevant Store Owner agreements and creative production standards.

## 19. Conflicts of interest and outside work

Employees and contractors must disclose material conflicts that could affect their responsibilities.

Examples include:

- Working for a direct competitor in a conflicting capacity.
- Having a financial interest in a supplier.
- Using company resources for undisclosed private work.
- Misusing confidential information for another business.
- Influencing procurement for personal benefit.

Outside work must be assessed according to the applicable agreement, actual conflict and legal requirements.

A disclosure must be assessed fairly and must not automatically be treated as misconduct.

## 20. Workplace complaints and grievances

Employees and contractors must have an appropriate channel to raise concerns.

Complaints may involve:

- Unfair treatment.
- Harassment.
- Discrimination.
- Unsafe conditions.
- Unclear responsibilities.
- Payment disputes.
- Conflicts of interest.
- Misconduct.
- Breaches of company policy.

Complaints must be handled confidentially, impartially and without retaliation.

Employment grievances must follow the applicable employment procedures and statutory rights.

Contractor disputes must also be handled according to the relevant agreement and applicable law.

## 21. Disciplinary and corrective processes

Where misconduct or poor performance is alleged, the company must follow a fair and appropriate process.

For employees, disciplinary action must comply with applicable labour legislation and procedural fairness requirements.

For contractors, remedies must follow the contract and applicable law.

Possible outcomes may include:

- Guidance.
- Training.
- Performance improvement measures.
- Formal warnings where appropriate.
- Restricted access.
- Contractual remediation.
- Disciplinary action.
- Termination where lawfully justified.

Allegations must not be treated as established facts before a fair assessment.

## 22. Offboarding and termination

When an employee or contractor leaves, the company must complete an appropriate offboarding process.

This should include:

- Confirmation of the final working date.
- Return of company property.
- Transfer of work and documentation.
- Handover of active responsibilities.
- Revocation of system access.
- Credential rotation where necessary.
- Final remuneration or invoice processing.
- Confidentiality reminders.
- Record retention.
- Exit administration.

Access must be removed promptly when the individual no longer has a legitimate need for it.

Termination must follow the applicable agreement and legal requirements.

## 23. Records and privacy

Workforce records must be collected and retained only for legitimate purposes.

Records may include:

- Recruitment information.
- Agreements.
- Role descriptions.
- Training records.
- Performance documentation.
- Payment records.
- Leave records.
- Disciplinary records.
- Offboarding records.

Access must be restricted to authorised personnel.

Retention periods must follow applicable employment, tax, privacy and information governance requirements.

## 24. Implementation requirements

The parent company should maintain:

- An approved organisational structure.
- Role descriptions.
- Recruitment procedures.
- Employment agreements.
- Contractor agreements.
- Onboarding checklists.
- Access approval records.
- Training records.
- Performance review procedures.
- Grievance procedures.
- Offboarding checklists.

Workforce processes should be reviewed as the company grows and introduces new departments or services.

## 25. Review and maintenance

This policy should be reviewed annually and whenever material changes affect:

- Labour legislation.
- Company structure.
- Workforce size.
- Contractor arrangements.
- Data protection obligations.
- Intellectual property arrangements.
- Workplace safety.
- Operational responsibilities.

Any legal or contractual changes must be reflected in the relevant agreements and procedures.

## 26. Governing principle

The parent company must build its workforce on clear agreements, fair treatment, professional conduct and accountability.

Employees and contractors must understand what is expected of them, what authority they hold and how their work contributes to the business.

Growth must never depend on unclear working arrangements, exploitation, unlawful practices or disregard for the rights of the people who help operate the platform.
