## 1. Purpose

The parent company MUST operate fair, practical business challenges and competitions that develop Store Owners' entrepreneurial capabilities.

Activities SHOULD encourage measurable business improvement, innovation, responsible competition, collaboration and knowledge sharing.

Participation MUST remain voluntary.

## 2. Challenge Types

The programme MAY organise challenges covering:

- Customer acquisition and retention.
- Marketing and brand development.
- Sales growth and conversion.
- Customer experience.
- Operational efficiency.
- Product presentation and merchandising.
- Financial planning and cash-flow management.
- Digital adoption and technology.
- Business innovation.
- Community collaboration.
- Personal entrepreneurial development.

Challenges SHOULD be designed around practical business problems rather than participation for its own sake.

## 3. Challenge Definition

Every challenge MUST have an approved brief containing:

- Unique challenge identifier.
- Title and purpose.
- Skills or business outcomes being developed.
- Eligible participants.
- Entry requirements.
- Start and closing dates.
- Submission format.
- Assessment criteria and weighting.
- Judges and approval authority.
- Prize or recognition details.
- Dispute and appeal process.
- Publication and knowledge-sharing arrangements.

Rules MUST be available to participants before submissions open.

Material rule changes after opening MUST be communicated to all affected participants.

## 4. Participation

Eligible Store Owners MUST be able to access the challenge brief through the application.

The platform MUST record:
- Participant membership.
- Associated store where relevant.
- Submission date.
- Submission version.
- Supporting evidence.
- Consent for publication, where applicable.

The system MUST distinguish individual participation from team participation.

Team entries MUST identify all participating members and the agreed contribution arrangement.

## 5. Assessment and Judging

Every competition MUST use published criteria.

Judging criteria MAY include:
- Practical effectiveness.
- Innovation.
- Customer value.
- Financial sustainability.
- Execution quality.
- Measurable improvement.
- Learning and reflection.
- Responsible business practices.

Judges MUST declare relevant conflicts of interest.

A judge MUST NOT assess an entry where a material conflict could compromise impartiality.

Scores and written feedback MUST be retained.

The final result MUST be approved by the designated authority before publication.

## 6. Fairness and Integrity

The parent company MUST apply the same published rules to all eligible participants.

The framework MUST prohibit:
- Fabricated results or supporting evidence.
- Manipulated customer activity.
- Plagiarism presented as original work.
- Undisclosed conflicts of interest.
- Unauthorised access to competitors' submissions.
- Retaliation against participants who raise concerns.

The programme administrator MUST investigate reported irregularities through an auditable process.

## 7. Prizes and Recognition

Every challenge MUST disclose its prize or recognition arrangements before participation begins.

Prizes MAY include:
- Certificates or public recognition.
- Training opportunities.
- Mentorship.
- Approved marketing exposure.
- Business development support.
- Sponsored services or equipment.
- Monetary or other material prizes, where separately authorised.

Prize costs MUST be approved in advance.

The parent company MUST NOT promise prizes or services that have not been authorised and funded.

Recognition MUST distinguish achievement in a specific challenge from an overall assessment of a Store Owner's business.

## 8. Results and Feedback

After approval, results SHOULD include:
- Challenge name.
- Winners or recognised participants.
- Assessment criteria.
- Relevant feedback.
- Key lessons.
- Next steps.

Participants SHOULD receive constructive feedback.

The platform MUST preserve the original submission and final assessment.

Public recognition MUST respect participant consent and confidentiality.

## 9. Knowledge Sharing

Every completed challenge SHOULD produce reusable learning material.

The programme administrator SHOULD capture:
- The business problem.
- The approach taken.
- Implementation steps.
- Resources required.
- Results and supporting evidence.
- Difficulties encountered.
- Lessons and recommendations.

A participant's business results MUST NOT be presented as guaranteed outcomes for other businesses.

Case studies MUST be reviewed and approved before publication.

## 10. Disputes and Appeals

Participants MUST have a documented route to challenge:
- Eligibility decisions.
- Procedural irregularities.
- Undeclared conflicts of interest.
- Incorrect scoring or result recording.
- Breaches of published rules.

Appeals MUST be reviewed by an authorised person who was not solely responsible for the disputed decision.

The outcome and reasons MUST be recorded.

## 11. Application Requirements

The application MUST support:
- Challenge publication.
- Eligibility information.
- Registration or entry.
- Submission and supporting files.
- Submission deadlines.
- Judging assignments.
- Scoring and feedback.
- Result approval.
- Recognition records.
- Appeal records.
- Approved knowledge-base publication.

All actions MUST use unified organisational RBAC and audit services.

## 12. Acceptance Criteria

The framework is satisfied when:

1. Challenges have approved and published briefs.
2. Participants can access consistent rules.
3. Entries and supporting evidence are recorded.
4. Judging follows documented criteria.
5. Conflicts of interest are declared and handled.
6. Results require authorised approval.
7. Prizes are disclosed and funded before commitment.
8. Appeals are independently reviewed.
9. Lessons can be published for non-participants.
10. Private business information remains protected.
