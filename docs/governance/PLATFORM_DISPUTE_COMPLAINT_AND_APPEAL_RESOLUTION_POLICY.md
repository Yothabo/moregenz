## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes a fair, consistent and traceable process for receiving, investigating and resolving disputes and complaints involving the platform.

It aims to protect customers, Store Owners, the parent company and other legitimate platform participants while ensuring that concerns are handled objectively.

Disputes must be assessed using relevant evidence, applicable agreements, platform records and legal requirements.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to disputes and complaints involving:

- Customers.
- Store Owners.
- The parent company.
- Employees and contractors.
- Suppliers and service providers.
- Marketing and creative service arrangements.
- Platform access and operational decisions.
- Payments, settlements and financial records.
- Products, orders, delivery, returns and refunds.
- Store identity, brand use and ownership rights.
- Platform rules and enforcement decisions.

This policy does not replace statutory consumer remedies, legal proceedings, payment-provider dispute processes or regulatory complaint mechanisms.

## 3. Core resolution principles

All complaints and disputes must be handled according to the following principles:

1. Fairness: each affected party must receive a reasonable opportunity to present its position.
2. Impartiality: decisions must be made by people without an unmanaged conflict of interest.
3. Evidence: conclusions must be supported by relevant information.
4. Confidentiality: information must only be shared with authorised parties.
5. Proportionality: responses must reflect the seriousness and impact of the issue.
6. Timeliness: complaints must be acknowledged and handled within published service targets.
7. Accountability: decisions, reasons and actions must be documented.
8. Non-retaliation: legitimate complaints must not result in punishment or discrimination.
9. Legal compliance: applicable South African laws and contractual rights must be respected.

## 4. Types of disputes

### 4.1 Customer disputes

Examples include:

- Incorrect products received.
- Missing or damaged orders.
- Delivery delays.
- Product quality concerns.
- Incorrect charges.
- Refund disagreements.
- Payment verification problems.
- Misleading product information.
- Privacy and personal information complaints.

### 4.2 Store Owner disputes

Examples include:

- Incorrect financial settlements.
- Disagreement over platform charges.
- Store access restrictions.
- Product or catalogue decisions.
- Marketing service disagreements.
- Creative production disagreements.
- Brand identity and intellectual property concerns.
- Store ownership history and succession disputes.
- Enforcement actions.
- Disagreements about platform functionality or support.

### 4.3 Parent company and supplier disputes

Examples include:

- Service delivery failures.
- Contractual disagreements.
- Payment disputes.
- Data processing concerns.
- Intellectual property disputes.
- Security or confidentiality breaches.

## 5. Complaint submission

Complaints should be submitted through an available official support channel.

Where supported, the platform should provide a structured complaint workflow.

A complaint should capture:

- Complainant identity and contact method.
- Relevant store or order.
- Complaint category.
- Description of the issue.
- Date the issue occurred.
- Supporting evidence.
- Requested resolution.
- Any immediate risk requiring urgent attention.

The platform must not require unnecessary personal information to accept a complaint.

Anonymous complaints may be accepted where appropriate, although the ability to investigate or provide feedback may be limited.

## 6. Complaint acknowledgement

The responsible team should acknowledge receipt within the published support response target.

Acknowledgement should confirm:

- That the complaint was received.
- A reference number.
- The responsible team or contact point.
- Any additional information required.
- The expected next update.

An acknowledgement is not an admission of fault or a final decision.

## 7. Classification and prioritisation

Complaints should be classified according to severity and business impact.

### Critical

Issues involving immediate safety risks, suspected serious security incidents, significant personal information exposure, fraud or widespread platform disruption.

These must be escalated immediately through the applicable incident or emergency process.

### High

Issues involving significant financial harm, widespread customer impact, serious operational disruption or potentially unlawful conduct.

These should receive priority investigation.

### Normal

Routine order, product, service, payment or operational disagreements.

These should follow the standard complaint process.

### Low

General feedback, suggestions and non-urgent improvement requests.

These may be handled through normal support and product feedback workflows.

Classification must be reviewed if new evidence changes the seriousness of the matter.

## 8. Evidence and investigation

Investigations should consider relevant evidence, including:

- Order records.
- Payment and reconciliation records.
- Customer communications.
- Store Owner communications.
- Delivery evidence.
- Product descriptions and images.
- Platform activity logs.
- Agreements and published policies.
- Supplier records.
- Marketing approvals.
- Relevant system events.

Evidence must be obtained lawfully and accessed only by authorised personnel.

Records must not be altered, deleted or selectively presented to influence an investigation.

Where a material record is unavailable, the investigator must document that limitation.

## 9. Investigation process

The responsible person should:

1. Review the complaint and available evidence.
2. Identify the relevant parties.
3. Determine which policies, agreements or legal obligations apply.
4. Request additional information where necessary.
5. Give affected parties a reasonable opportunity to respond.
6. Assess the evidence objectively.
7. Identify findings and any unresolved uncertainty.
8. Recommend an appropriate resolution.
9. Record the decision and its reasons.
10. Communicate the outcome to the relevant parties.

The investigator should not make assumptions based solely on a party's commercial status, popularity or relationship with the parent company.

## 10. Resolution options

Depending on the circumstances, possible outcomes include:

- Explanation or clarification.
- Correction of inaccurate information.
- Payment verification.
- Financial adjustment.
- Refund or replacement.
- Order cancellation.
- Product removal or correction.
- Service remediation.
- Access restoration.
- Additional support or training.
- Formal warning.
- Contractual enforcement.
- Referral to an appropriate external process.
- No further action where the complaint is unsupported.

Any financial adjustment must follow the applicable financial controls and approval requirements.

A proposed resolution must not remove a customer's statutory rights or override an enforceable contractual obligation.

## 11. Customer complaint resolution

Customer complaints should be handled with particular attention to consumer protection obligations.

The responsible Store Owner should normally address issues relating to the products and orders supplied by that store.

The parent company may coordinate or intervene where:

- The platform caused or materially contributed to the issue.
- The Store Owner cannot be reached.
- There is a serious customer protection concern.
- The matter affects multiple stores.
- A legal or safety obligation requires intervention.

The platform must distinguish between the parent company's technology services and the Store Owner's responsibility for the products or services they supply.

This distinction must not be used to avoid responsibilities that legally belong to the parent company.

## 12. Store Owner disputes

Store Owners must have access to a documented process for challenging decisions that materially affect their business.

This includes disputes concerning:

- Financial calculations.
- Settlement deductions.
- Platform charges.
- Access restrictions.
- Marketing decisions.
- Creative production decisions.
- Product restrictions.
- Store identity and branding.
- Contract interpretation.
- Enforcement measures.

Where a decision affects a Store Owner's rights or commercial interests, the parent company should provide a clear explanation of the decision and the applicable basis.

## 13. Conflicts of interest

A person must not independently investigate and decide a dispute where they have a material personal, financial or operational interest in the outcome.

Where a conflict exists:

- The conflict must be disclosed.
- Another authorised person should handle the investigation or decision.
- The reason for reassignment should be recorded.

If the complaint concerns senior management or the person normally responsible for dispute resolution, an independent reviewer should be appointed where reasonably possible.

## 14. Appeal process

A party may request an appeal where:

- Material evidence was overlooked.
- New evidence has become available.
- The applicable policy was incorrectly interpreted.
- The investigation was procedurally unfair.
- The decision was disproportionate.
- A conflict of interest may have affected the outcome.

An appeal should identify the original decision, the grounds for appeal and any supporting information.

Where practical, the appeal should be reviewed by someone who was not responsible for the original decision.

The appeal reviewer may:

- Uphold the original decision.
- Amend the decision.
- Reverse the decision.
- Request further investigation.
- Refer the matter for independent review.

The appeal outcome must include the reasons for the decision.

## 15. Interim protective measures

The parent company may take temporary action while a serious dispute is being investigated.

Examples include:

- Temporarily restricting a compromised account.
- Pausing a disputed campaign.
- Restricting access to affected information.
- Temporarily suspending a dangerous product listing.
- Preserving relevant transaction or system records.

Interim measures must be necessary, proportionate and reviewed regularly.

They must not be used as a substitute for completing the investigation.

Where a measure materially affects a Store Owner, the parent company should communicate the reason and expected review process unless doing so would create a legitimate security or legal risk.

## 16. Financial disputes

Financial complaints must be checked against authoritative records.

This includes:

- Order totals.
- Payment-provider transactions.
- Verified manual payments.
- Refunds.
- Chargebacks.
- Platform fees.
- Approved service charges.
- Settlement calculations.
- Previous adjustments.

Disputed amounts must be identified clearly.

The parent company must not make arbitrary deductions or change settlement figures without a documented contractual or legal basis.

Where only part of a settlement is disputed, the undisputed portion should be handled according to the applicable settlement terms.

## 17. Privacy and confidentiality complaints

Complaints involving personal information must be escalated to the responsible privacy or information governance function.

The investigation must consider:

- The information involved.
- The people affected.
- The purpose and legal basis of processing.
- Who accessed or received the information.
- Whether unauthorised disclosure occurred.
- Required containment and notification steps.

Where a personal information security compromise may have occurred, the applicable incident response and POPIA notification obligations must be followed.

## 18. External escalation

Internal complaint handling does not prevent a customer, Store Owner or other affected party from using an applicable external dispute resolution or legal process.

Depending on the matter, external escalation may include:

- The relevant payment provider.
- The Information Regulator.
- The National Consumer Commission.
- An appropriate industry ombud.
- Law enforcement.
- A competent court or tribunal.
- Independent mediation or arbitration where agreed or legally applicable.

The parent company must not misrepresent internal dispute resolution as a mandatory replacement for statutory remedies.

## 19. Communication and record keeping

All material complaints and disputes should have a traceable record containing:

- Complaint reference.
- Date received.
- Parties involved.
- Classification.
- Assigned investigator.
- Evidence reviewed.
- Communications.
- Findings.
- Decision.
- Resolution actions.
- Appeal details.
- Closure date.

Records must be retained according to the applicable data retention and information lifecycle requirements.

Sensitive information must not be included in general support notes where restricted records are more appropriate.

## 20. Service targets

The parent company should publish realistic service targets for:

- Complaint acknowledgement.
- Initial assessment.
- Investigation updates.
- Resolution.
- Appeal acknowledgement.
- Appeal review.

Targets must reflect the complexity and seriousness of the issue.

If a matter cannot be resolved within the expected period, the responsible team should provide an update explaining the delay and the next expected step.

Service targets must not be presented as guaranteed legal deadlines unless they are legally prescribed.

## 21. Protection against retaliation

No customer, Store Owner, employee or supplier may be penalised merely for raising a legitimate complaint or participating in an investigation.

Retaliation may include:

- Unjustified account restrictions.
- Unfair commercial treatment.
- Threats or intimidation.
- Deliberate withholding of legitimate settlements.
- Unjustified removal of approved store functionality.
- Unfair exclusion from business opportunities.

This protection does not prevent proportionate action against proven misconduct, fraud or deliberate abuse of the complaint process.

## 22. Implementation requirements

The platform should support:

- Complaint submission.
- Complaint reference numbers.
- Classification and priority.
- Assignment to responsible teams.
- Evidence attachment.
- Internal investigation notes.
- Customer and Store Owner communication.
- Resolution tracking.
- Appeal submission.
- Audit history.
- Role-based access.
- Reporting on complaint trends.

Complaint records must be isolated according to the permissions of the relevant parties.

## 23. Review and maintenance

This policy should be reviewed annually and whenever material changes affect:

- Consumer protection obligations.
- Store Owner agreements.
- Financial settlement processes.
- Privacy and information security.
- Platform enforcement.
- Customer support operations.
- Dispute resolution arrangements.

All material revisions must be documented and communicated to affected participants.

## 24. Governing principle

Every participant must have a fair opportunity to raise concerns, present evidence and receive an understandable decision.

The parent company must protect the integrity of the platform without placing itself above customers, Store Owners, contractual obligations or applicable law.

Dispute resolution must be transparent, proportionate, evidence-based and accountable.
