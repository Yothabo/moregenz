# Platform Data Governance and Information Lifecycle Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes how the parent company governs information created, collected, processed, stored, shared and deleted across the multi-store platform.

The platform handles information belonging to several distinct groups:

- The parent company.
- Store Owners.
- Customers.
- Suppliers.
- Employees and authorised service providers.
- Marketing and creative partners.
- Payment and fulfilment providers.

The objective is to ensure that information is accurate, appropriately protected, accessible only to authorised parties and retained only for legitimate business, operational or legal purposes.

## 2. Core Principles

1. Every important data category must have an accountable owner.
2. Data must have a documented purpose.
3. Access must follow least-privilege principles.
4. Store-specific information must remain logically isolated.
5. Customer information must be protected across all stores.
6. Personal information must be processed lawfully and transparently.
7. Data quality must be maintained throughout its lifecycle.
8. Retention periods must be defined and justified.
9. Deletion must be controlled and verifiable.
10. Data governance must support business continuity and accountability.

## 3. Data Governance Roles

### 3.1 Parent Company

The parent company establishes platform-wide data governance rules and remains responsible for the systems and processing activities under its control.

Responsibilities include:

- Defining data standards.
- Maintaining the data inventory.
- Managing platform access.
- Establishing retention rules.
- Maintaining security controls.
- Managing data quality.
- Coordinating data incidents.
- Reviewing third-party data processing.
- Maintaining governance records.

### 3.2 Information Owner

An Information Owner is responsible for the business purpose, meaning and authorised use of a particular information category.

Examples include:

- Customer account information.
- Store records.
- Product catalogue information.
- Financial records.
- Marketing records.
- Supplier records.

The Information Owner must ensure that the information is used consistently with approved business purposes.

### 3.3 System Owner

The System Owner is responsible for the technical environment that stores or processes information.

Responsibilities include:

- Access controls.
- System configuration.
- Security.
- Availability.
- Backup arrangements.
- Technical retention controls.
- System integrity.

### 3.4 Store Owner

A Store Owner is responsible for the accuracy and appropriate use of information they are authorised to manage within their assigned store.

This may include:

- Product descriptions.
- Store-specific pricing.
- Inventory information.
- Store operating information.
- Store-specific customer service records.
- Marketing content submitted by the Store Owner.

A Store Owner must not access another store's restricted information merely because both stores operate on the same platform.

### 3.5 Information Officer

The parent company must identify and appoint the appropriate Information Officer and Deputy Information Officer arrangements required under applicable South African law.

The Information Officer must oversee relevant privacy and information governance responsibilities.

## 4. Data Inventory

The parent company must maintain a data inventory.

Each data category should record:

- Data category.
- Description.
- Business purpose.
- Information Owner.
- System Owner.
- Source.
- Storage location.
- Classification.
- Personal information status.
- Permitted users.
- Sharing restrictions.
- Retention rule.
- Disposal method.
- Legal or contractual requirements.

The inventory must be updated when new information categories, systems or processing activities are introduced.

## 5. Data Classification

Information must be classified according to its sensitivity and potential impact if disclosed, altered, lost or misused.

### 5.1 Public

Information intentionally made available to the public.

Examples:

- Published product descriptions.
- Public storefront pages.
- Approved store branding.
- Public promotional material.
- Public business contact details.

Public information must still be accurate and approved for publication.

### 5.2 Internal

Information intended for authorised internal business use.

Examples:

- Internal operating procedures.
- General business documentation.
- Non-sensitive project plans.
- Internal administrative instructions.

Unauthorised public disclosure may create operational or reputational risk.

### 5.3 Confidential

Information that could harm a person, Store Owner or the parent company if disclosed without permission.

Examples:

- Supplier agreements.
- Commercial pricing arrangements.
- Unpublished financial reports.
- Store performance information.
- Internal marketing plans.
- Non-public business strategies.
- Contractual negotiations.

Access must be limited to authorised users.

### 5.4 Restricted

Highly sensitive information requiring stronger controls.

Examples:

- Authentication credentials.
- Security secrets.
- Payment-related sensitive information.
- Customer personal information requiring strict protection.
- Identity documents.
- Security incident evidence.
- Sensitive financial records.
- Information protected by legal privilege.

Restricted information must use appropriate encryption, access restrictions, logging and secure storage.

## 6. Data Ownership and Control

The parent company must distinguish between:

- Legal ownership.
- Intellectual property rights.
- Responsibility for processing.
- Custody of records.
- Permission to access or use information.
- Contractual rights.

Storing information in the platform does not automatically transfer ownership or intellectual property rights to the parent company.

### 6.1 Store Information

Store identity, branding and business information must be governed by the applicable Store Owner agreement.

The parent company retains the platform infrastructure and its underlying technical systems.

Store Owners retain the rights established in their agreements over their own brand assets and business contributions.

### 6.2 Customer Information

Customer information must be governed by the platform's central customer identity architecture, privacy policy and applicable processing agreements.

A customer's account may support purchases across several stores.

That does not grant every Store Owner unrestricted access to the customer's complete purchase history.

### 6.3 Product Information

The platform must distinguish:

- Global product catalogue records.
- Store-specific product listings.
- Store-specific prices.
- Store-specific inventory.
- Store-specific merchandising content.

A shared catalogue record does not make every store's operational information public to other stores.

### 6.4 Financial Information

Financial records must be maintained with sufficient separation and traceability to identify:

- Parent-company transactions.
- Store-specific sales.
- Store-specific expenses.
- Supplier payments.
- Customer payments.
- Refunds.
- Fees.
- Settlements.
- Reconciliation adjustments.

## 7. Personal Information Governance

Personal information must be processed in accordance with applicable privacy law, including the Protection of Personal Information Act (POPIA).

The parent company must document:

- The purpose of processing.
- The applicable lawful basis or justification.
- Categories of personal information.
- Categories of data subjects.
- Recipients and service providers.
- Security safeguards.
- Retention requirements.
- Cross-border processing where applicable.
- Data subject rights procedures.

Personal information must not be collected simply because it may be useful in the future.

Collect only information reasonably necessary for the stated purpose.

## 8. Data Access and Permissions

Access must be granted according to role, responsibility and legitimate business need.

The platform should distinguish permissions for:

- Parent-company administrators.
- Technical administrators.
- Finance personnel.
- Customer support personnel.
- Marketing personnel.
- Creative production personnel.
- Store Owners.
- External suppliers.
- Service providers.

Access must be limited to the information required for the user's responsibilities.

### 8.1 Store-Level Access

Store Owners must only access information authorised for their assigned stores.

### 8.2 Central Access

Central administrators may access cross-store information where required for legitimate platform operations, governance, support, security or financial administration.

Such access must be controlled and auditable.

### 8.3 Temporary Access

Temporary access must have:

- An approved purpose.
- An authorised requester.
- A defined scope.
- An expiry or review date.
- An audit record.

Access must be revoked when the purpose ends.

## 9. Data Quality Standards

Important business information must be accurate, complete, consistent and traceable.

Data quality controls should address:

- Missing information.
- Duplicate records.
- Invalid values.
- Incorrect relationships.
- Outdated information.
- Conflicting records.
- Unauthorised changes.

Where possible, validation should occur when information is created or updated.

Material corrections must preserve the required audit history.

## 10. Data Lifecycle

All information must follow a defined lifecycle.

### 10.1 Creation or Collection

Record information only for an authorised purpose.

Identify:

- Source.
- Purpose.
- Classification.
- Responsible owner.
- Required validation.

### 10.2 Storage

Store information in approved systems.

Apply:

- Access controls.
- Encryption where appropriate.
- Backup protection.
- Retention rules.
- Integrity controls.

### 10.3 Use

Information must only be used for authorised purposes.

Secondary uses must be assessed before implementation.

### 10.4 Sharing

Information may only be shared with authorised recipients under an appropriate legal, contractual or operational basis.

### 10.5 Archiving

Information that is no longer required for active operations but must be retained may be moved into controlled archival storage.

### 10.6 Disposal

Information must be securely deleted or anonymised when retention is no longer justified and no legal hold or other valid requirement applies.

## 11. Retention Schedule

The parent company must maintain a formal retention schedule.

The schedule should identify:

- Information category.
- Business purpose.
- Applicable legal requirements.
- Contractual requirements.
- Retention start event.
- Retention period.
- Archival requirements.
- Disposal method.
- Responsible owner.

Retention periods must be determined from applicable laws, contractual obligations and legitimate business needs.

Do not assign one universal retention period to every information category.

Financial records, customer account information, marketing consent, security logs, product content and creative assets may require different retention rules.

## 12. Archiving

Archived information must remain protected.

The parent company must define:

- Archival eligibility.
- Storage location.
- Access permissions.
- Integrity verification.
- Retrieval procedure.
- Retention period.
- Disposal procedure.

Archiving must not be used to avoid deletion obligations.

Personal information in archived records remains subject to applicable privacy requirements.

## 13. Data Deletion

Deletion must be controlled.

Before deletion, determine:

- Who requested it.
- Whether the requester has authority.
- Which information is affected.
- Whether the information is subject to a legal retention requirement.
- Whether a legal hold applies.
- Whether related records must be retained.
- Whether deletion affects other stores or customers.

Deletion must not silently destroy financial, audit or legal records that must remain available.

Where appropriate, use anonymisation or restricted archival storage instead of deleting the entire associated business record.

Record material deletion actions.

## 14. Customer Data Rights

The parent company must maintain procedures for handling applicable data subject requests.

Requests may include:

- Access to personal information.
- Correction of inaccurate information.
- Deletion where legally permitted.
- Objection to certain processing.
- Withdrawal of consent where consent is the applicable basis.
- Complaints about processing.

Requests must be authenticated and handled within applicable legal timeframes.

The platform must distinguish between:

- A customer's central account.
- Store-specific transaction records.
- Marketing preferences.
- Financial and legal records.
- Information held by external providers.

Deleting an account must not automatically erase records that must lawfully be retained.

## 15. Store Owner Departure and Data Continuity

When a Store Owner leaves:

- Their platform access must be reviewed and revoked as appropriate.
- Store operations must be handed over.
- Store history must remain intact.
- Previous contributions must remain correctly attributed.
- Customer and financial records must remain protected.
- Brand and intellectual property rights must follow the applicable agreement.
- Personal information must not be transferred to an unauthorised successor.

The departure of a Store Owner must not result in the destruction of the store's historical records.

## 16. Third-Party Data Processing

Before sharing information with an external provider, assess:

- Purpose.
- Information categories.
- Provider access.
- Security safeguards.
- Data location.
- Sub-processors.
- Retention.
- Deletion.
- Breach notification.
- Contractual responsibilities.
- Cross-border transfer requirements.

Where required, enter into appropriate written operator or data processing agreements.

The parent company must not assume that a provider's general privacy policy satisfies all contractual requirements.

## 17. Data Transfers Across Borders

International storage or processing must be assessed under applicable South African privacy requirements.

Before introducing cross-border processing, document:

- Information being transferred.
- Destination country.
- Receiving organisation.
- Processing purpose.
- Applicable legal safeguards.
- Contractual protections.
- Security measures.
- Any required authorisation or consent.

A provider's international infrastructure must not be treated as automatically compliant.

## 18. Data Integrity and Auditability

Important changes must be traceable.

Audit records should identify:

- Actor.
- Action.
- Record affected.
- Timestamp.
- Previous value where appropriate.
- New value where appropriate.
- Reason or reference.
- Relevant store.
- Related request or transaction.

Audit records must be protected against unauthorised alteration.

Sensitive information must not be copied unnecessarily into audit logs.

## 19. Data Incident Management

Suspected data loss, exposure, corruption or unauthorised access must be reported through the platform incident response process.

The parent company must:

1. Identify the affected information.
2. Determine the scope.
3. Contain the incident.
4. Preserve evidence.
5. Assess affected data subjects.
6. Determine legal and contractual obligations.
7. Coordinate notifications where required.
8. Restore or correct information where appropriate.
9. Record decisions and actions.
10. Implement corrective measures.

The Information Officer must be involved where privacy or personal information obligations arise.

## 20. Data Governance and Reporting

Management should periodically review:

- Data inventory completeness.
- Data classification.
- Access permissions.
- Retention compliance.
- Deletion requests.
- Data quality issues.
- Third-party processing.
- Cross-border transfers.
- Data incidents.
- Outstanding governance actions.

The review should identify gaps and assign corrective actions.

## 21. Platform Implementation Requirements

The platform should support the governance model through:

- Data ownership metadata where appropriate.
- Store-level data relationships.
- Role-based permissions.
- Access audit records.
- Retention configuration.
- Controlled archival.
- Deletion workflows.
- Data subject request tracking.
- Processing activity records.
- Third-party integration records.
- Data quality validation.

Do not introduce unnecessary data duplication simply to satisfy governance reporting.

Use existing authoritative records wherever possible.

## 22. Testing Requirements

Verify that:

1. Data categories have assigned owners.
2. Classification is applied consistently.
3. Store Owners cannot access unauthorised store information.
4. Central access is permission-controlled.
5. Personal information is excluded from unnecessary logs.
6. Retention rules can be applied.
7. Legal holds prevent inappropriate deletion.
8. Deletion workflows preserve required financial records.
9. Customer account deletion respects applicable retention obligations.
10. Store Owner departure preserves store history.
11. Third-party access is restricted to approved purposes.
12. Data subject requests can be tracked.
13. Audit records capture material changes.
14. Cross-store customer privacy boundaries remain intact.
15. Backup and archival processes preserve data integrity.

## 23. Review and Ownership

The parent company is responsible for maintaining this policy.

The policy must be reviewed:

- At least annually.
- When new data categories are introduced.
- When processing purposes change.
- When new third-party providers are introduced.
- When privacy laws or guidance change.
- After a material data incident.
- When the platform architecture changes significantly.

Legal and privacy advisers should review the policy and its supporting procedures.

## 24. Governing Rule

Information is a business asset, but it is not unrestricted property.

The parent company must govern information according to its purpose, legal obligations, contractual rights and the interests of the people and businesses represented in the platform.

Every information category must have a clear reason for existing, a responsible owner, appropriate protection and a defined lifecycle.
