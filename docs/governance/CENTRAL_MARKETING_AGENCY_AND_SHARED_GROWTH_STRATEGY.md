# Central Marketing Agency and Shared Growth Strategy

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the parent company's central marketing agency function and its role in supporting the growth of participating stores.

The parent company is not only a technology provider. It also operates a shared marketing and creative function designed to help Store Owners build visibility, attract customers and grow their businesses.

The marketing function must serve two related objectives:

1. Build awareness and demand for the parent company's platform and services.
2. Promote participating stores and their individual products, services and brands.

These objectives must remain distinguishable.

The parent company must never use its central marketing position to misrepresent a Store Owner's brand, claim another person's work or imply that every participating store is simply a product line owned by the parent company.

## 2. Marketing Operating Model

The parent company operates a central marketing agency serving participating stores.

The agency may provide:

- Central marketing strategy.
- Campaign planning.
- Creative direction.
- Photography and video coordination.
- Promotional graphics.
- Copywriting.
- Social media content.
- Paid advertising management.
- Product launch campaigns.
- Seasonal campaigns.
- Store introduction campaigns.
- Brand positioning support.
- Marketing performance reporting.

The central function allows stores to access marketing capabilities that may otherwise be too expensive or difficult to organise independently.

The parent company may also sell additional marketing services to Store Owners under separate service agreements.

## 3. Two Distinct Marketing Objectives

### 3.1 Parent company marketing

The parent company must market its own platform, infrastructure and services.

Its marketing may communicate:

- What the platform provides.
- How entrepreneurs can participate.
- How stores are supported.
- Available e-commerce capabilities.
- Central creative production services.
- Marketing services.
- Business support opportunities.
- Success stories, where properly approved.
- Opportunities for new Store Owners.

Parent company marketing must not imply that the parent company personally created or owns the independent brands operating on the platform.

### 3.2 Store marketing

Store marketing focuses on an individual Store Owner's business.

It may communicate:

- The store's identity.
- Its products and services.
- Its unique value proposition.
- Product launches.
- Promotions.
- Customer benefits.
- Brand story.
- Store Owner journey.
- Customer testimonials.
- Availability and purchasing information.

The store must remain clearly identified as the business being promoted.

### 3.3 Shared campaigns

The parent company may coordinate campaigns involving several stores.

Examples include:

- Clothing campaigns.
- Fragrance campaigns.
- Local business promotions.
- Seasonal shopping campaigns.
- Gift guides.
- Entrepreneur showcases.
- Category-specific promotions.
- Multi-store product collections.

Every participating store must receive appropriate attribution.

Shared campaigns must not create confusion about which store sells a product, fulfils an order or is responsible for customer service.

## 4. Store Brand Independence

Each participating store must maintain its own brand identity.

The parent company's marketing function must respect:

- Store name.
- Logo.
- Brand colours.
- Brand voice.
- Product positioning.
- Target audience.
- Visual identity.
- Approved promotional messaging.
- Store Owner's creative direction.

The parent company may provide recommendations and professional assistance, but it must not arbitrarily replace a Store Owner's brand identity.

### 4.1 Attribution

Marketing material must clearly identify the relevant store.

Depending on the campaign format, attribution may include:

- Store name.
- Store logo.
- Storefront link.
- Product attribution.
- Store Owner credit, where approved.
- Relevant purchasing or contact information.

### 4.2 No false ownership claims

The parent company must not:

- Present a Store Owner's brand as its own retail brand.
- Claim ownership of another person's product designs.
- Claim credit for work it did not create.
- Imply that the parent company is the manufacturer where it is not.
- Present a Store Owner's existing audience as a parent-company asset.
- Reuse a Store Owner's content outside agreed permissions.

The parent company may explain that it provides the infrastructure or marketing support behind a store, provided the wording accurately reflects the relationship.

## 5. Campaign Categories

The central marketing team should maintain a structured campaign portfolio.

### 5.1 Store introduction campaigns

Purpose: Introduce a participating store to potential customers.

Possible content:

- Store introduction.
- Brand story.
- Product range.
- Store Owner background, where approved.
- Main customer proposition.
- Storefront link.

### 5.2 Product campaigns

Purpose: Promote specific products or collections.

Possible content:

- Product photography.
- Product videos.
- Product demonstrations.
- Product benefits.
- Product availability.
- Promotional offers.
- Purchasing information.

### 5.3 Category campaigns

Purpose: Promote products across multiple stores within a shared category.

Examples:

- Clothing.
- Fragrance.
- Beauty.
- Home and lifestyle.
- Printing and customised products.
- Gifts and accessories.

Each product must retain its store attribution.

### 5.4 Seasonal campaigns

Purpose: Coordinate marketing around relevant shopping periods and events.

Examples:

- Valentine's Day.
- Mother's Day.
- Father's Day.
- Black Friday.
- Festive shopping.
- Back-to-school periods.
- Local events.
- Industry-specific occasions.

Campaign dates and commercial terms must be confirmed before publication.

### 5.5 Entrepreneur campaigns

Purpose: Highlight the people and businesses behind participating stores.

Possible content:

- Founder stories.
- Business journeys.
- Product development.
- Challenges overcome.
- Local entrepreneurship.
- Business milestones.

The Store Owner must approve the use of their personal story, image and statements.

### 5.6 Parent company campaigns

Purpose: Build awareness of the parent company's services and attract new business participants.

These campaigns should focus on the infrastructure and support model rather than claiming ownership of participating stores.

## 6. Campaign Planning Workflow

Every material campaign should follow a documented process.

### Stage 1: Campaign proposal

The marketing team records:

- Campaign name.
- Campaign objective.
- Campaign category.
- Participating stores.
- Target audience.
- Key message.
- Proposed channels.
- Campaign period.
- Required creative assets.
- Proposed budget.
- Expected deliverables.
- Responsible team member.

### Stage 2: Store participation

Where a campaign involves a Store Owner's business, the team must obtain the required participation approval.

The Store Owner should be informed about:

- Campaign concept.
- Product selection.
- Brand presentation.
- Promotional messaging.
- Advertising budget.
- Campaign duration.
- Expected deliverables.
- Required participation.
- Relevant service charges.

### Stage 3: Creative production

The central creative team prepares the required assets.

This may involve:

- Product photography.
- Product video.
- Promotional graphics.
- Copywriting.
- Social media layouts.
- Advertising formats.
- Landing page assets.

Production must follow the relevant creative production standards and rights agreements.

### Stage 4: Review and approval

Before publication, verify:

- Correct store attribution.
- Correct product information.
- Correct pricing.
- Product availability.
- Approved claims.
- Brand presentation.
- Usage rights.
- Advertising budget.
- Destination links.
- Campaign dates.

The Store Owner must approve material store-specific content where required by the agreement.

### Stage 5: Publication and execution

The campaign is published through the approved channels.

The marketing team must record:

- Publication dates.
- Platforms used.
- Advertising expenditure.
- Creative versions.
- Campaign changes.
- Relevant performance data.

### Stage 6: Reporting

After the campaign, the team prepares a report covering:

- Work completed.
- Channels used.
- Advertising expenditure.
- Reach and impressions.
- Engagement.
- Clicks.
- Storefront visits.
- Enquiries, where measurable.
- Orders or conversions, where reliably attributable.
- Key observations.
- Recommendations.

The report must distinguish measured results from estimates.

## 7. Campaign Consent and Approval

The parent company must obtain appropriate approval before using a Store Owner's brand, products, personal story or other protected material in campaigns.

Approval requirements should distinguish between:

- General permission for routine store promotion.
- Approval of specific campaigns.
- Approval of personal images and stories.
- Approval of discounts and special offers.
- Approval of advertising expenditure.
- Approval of content for external use.
- Approval of third-party partnerships.

A general marketing relationship must not be treated as unlimited permission to use every asset in every context.

### 7.1 Approval records

The system should record:

- Campaign ID.
- Store ID.
- Store Owner.
- Approval request.
- Material presented.
- Approval status.
- Approving person.
- Approval date.
- Approved usage.
- Expiry or withdrawal conditions, where applicable.

## 8. Advertising Budget and Financial Controls

Advertising expenditure must be separate from marketing service fees.

Every paid campaign must identify:

1. Advertising platform.
2. Advertising account.
3. Funding party.
4. Approved budget.
5. Campaign duration.
6. Service fee.
7. Payment arrangements.
8. Reporting method.

The parent company must not spend a Store Owner's advertising budget without the required authorisation.

Where the parent company funds a shared campaign, the allocation method must be documented.

### 8.1 Shared budget allocation

Shared campaigns may use:

- Equal contributions.
- Agreed category-based contributions.
- Proportional budget allocation.
- Parent-company-funded promotion.
- Another expressly agreed arrangement.

The method must be explained before the campaign begins.

A Store Owner must not be charged an undisclosed portion of a shared advertising budget.

### 8.2 Advertising account access

Where campaigns run through Store Owner-owned accounts, access must follow secure authorisation procedures.

The parent company should use delegated access rather than requesting personal passwords.

Account ownership and access must be addressed in the relevant agreement.

## 9. Organic and Paid Marketing

The marketing function must distinguish organic activity from paid advertising.

### 9.1 Organic marketing

Organic marketing may include:

- Social media posts.
- Community engagement.
- Product features.
- Store introductions.
- Educational content.
- Email campaigns.
- Search-oriented content.
- Organic partnerships.

Organic activity still has a delivery cost, even when no advertising budget is spent.

### 9.2 Paid marketing

Paid marketing may include:

- Social media advertisements.
- Search advertising.
- Display campaigns.
- Retargeting, where lawful and appropriately consented.
- Sponsored placements.
- Paid influencer partnerships.

Paid activity must follow the approved budget and reporting process.

## 10. Central Creative Production

The marketing agency works with the parent company's central creative production team.

The creative team is responsible for producing technically suitable media for the platform and approved marketing channels.

The production workflow must account for:

- Product-specific photography.
- Product video.
- Storefront image containers.
- Responsive image requirements.
- File formats.
- Compression.
- Image quality.
- Visual consistency.
- Accessibility.
- Usage rights.

The Store Owner contributes creative direction and product knowledge.

The marketing team may request additional campaign assets beyond the basic storefront media allowance.

Additional production must be scoped and charged according to the relevant agreement.

The marketing function must not assume that a subscription includes unlimited campaign photography or video.

## 11. Content Ownership and Usage Rights

Every campaign must use content for which the parent company has appropriate rights.

The relevant agreements must distinguish:

- Store Owner-owned brand assets.
- Parent-company-created materials.
- Commissioned photography and video.
- Third-party assets.
- Licensed fonts, music and graphics.
- Model and property releases.
- Customer testimonials.
- User-generated content.
- AI-assisted creative material.

The agreement must state who owns each asset and what permissions each party receives.

The parent company must not assume that payment for a marketing service automatically transfers copyright or ownership.

Campaign usage rights must identify:

- Permitted channels.
- Geographic scope, where relevant.
- Duration.
- Editing permissions.
- Reuse permissions.
- Portfolio or case-study permissions.
- Post-exit usage.

## 12. Marketing Services and Pricing

The parent company may offer additional marketing services under a separate service agreement.

Possible services include:

- Campaign strategy.
- Campaign management.
- Promotional graphics.
- Copywriting.
- Social media content packages.
- Product launch campaigns.
- Brand refinement.
- Photography and video production.
- Advertising management.
- Marketing reporting.

Each service must have:

- Defined scope.
- Deliverables.
- Price.
- Payment terms.
- Turnaround expectations.
- Revision limits.
- Exclusions.
- Approval process.

Advertising expenditure must remain separate from the service fee.

The parent company must not promise guaranteed revenue, sales or audience growth.

## 13. Store Owner Participation

Store Owners should be encouraged to participate in their marketing.

They may contribute:

- Product knowledge.
- Brand direction.
- Campaign ideas.
- Product samples.
- Creative references.
- Existing content.
- Customer insights.
- Testimonials.
- Product demonstrations.
- Campaign feedback.

The parent company should provide guidance and professional support without removing the Store Owner's voice or identity.

Store Owners must supply accurate information and honour approved campaign commitments.

## 14. Marketing Performance and Attribution

The marketing team must maintain consistent measurement standards.

### 14.1 Campaign-level metrics

Possible measures include:

- Reach.
- Impressions.
- Engagement.
- Click-through rate.
- Cost per click.
- Cost per thousand impressions.
- Video views.
- Landing page visits.
- Enquiries.
- Conversion rate.
- Customer acquisition cost, where measurable.
- Revenue attributed to a campaign, where reliable.

### 14.2 Store-level metrics

Store reporting may include:

- Storefront visits.
- Product views.
- Add-to-cart activity.
- Checkout initiation.
- Completed orders.
- Repeat purchase activity.
- Campaign-attributed conversions.
- Customer acquisition trends.

Metrics must be interpreted carefully.

An increase in traffic does not automatically mean an increase in sales.

A campaign should not receive credit for a sale merely because a customer visited an advertisement at some point.

Attribution methods and measurement windows must be documented.

### 14.3 Reporting transparency

Store Owners should be able to understand:

- What the marketing team did.
- What content was produced.
- Where it was published.
- How much advertising expenditure was used.
- What results were measured.
- What limitations affected measurement.
- What improvements are recommended.

## 15. Marketing Technology and Data

The parent company may use analytics, advertising and marketing tools to operate campaigns.

The use of these tools must comply with applicable privacy and data protection requirements.

The platform must distinguish:

- Platform operational analytics.
- Store-specific analytics.
- Advertising platform data.
- Customer personal information.
- Marketing consent records.
- Campaign reporting.

Store Owners should only receive data they are authorised to access.

Customer information must not be shared between stores merely because the parent company operates the shared platform.

Marketing consent must be captured and respected according to the applicable purpose and communication channel.

## 16. AI-Assisted Marketing

The parent company may use AI tools to support:

- Copywriting.
- Content planning.
- Creative ideation.
- Product descriptions.
- Campaign analysis.
- Audience research.
- Reporting.
- Search and AI discovery optimisation.

AI-generated or AI-assisted material must be reviewed before publication.

The marketing team remains responsible for:

- Factual accuracy.
- Product claims.
- Brand consistency.
- Rights clearance.
- Privacy.
- Advertising compliance.
- Human approval requirements.

AI tools must not be given unnecessary access to confidential Store Owner information or customer personal data.

The use of AI must not create false testimonials, fabricated customer experiences or misleading product claims.

## 17. Marketing Conflicts and Fairness

The parent company may promote multiple stores operating in similar categories.

The marketing team must use fair and documented allocation practices.

Relevant considerations include:

- Campaign objectives.
- Store readiness.
- Product availability.
- Audience relevance.
- Approved budgets.
- Creative requirements.
- Business priorities.
- Previous campaign commitments.

The parent company must not secretly favour one Store Owner while representing shared marketing opportunities as equally available.

Where a campaign has limited capacity, selection criteria should be documented.

## 18. Campaign Records and Dashboard Integration

The platform should eventually support campaign management records.

A campaign record may contain:

- Campaign ID.
- Campaign name.
- Campaign category.
- Participating stores.
- Campaign owner.
- Objective.
- Target audience.
- Channels.
- Creative assets.
- Approval records.
- Budget.
- Service fees.
- Advertising expenditure.
- Start date.
- End date.
- Status.
- Performance results.
- Final report.

Store Owners should be able to view campaigns involving their store and access the reports permitted by their agreement.

The parent company should retain central oversight of shared campaigns.

## 19. Complaints and Campaign Corrections

The marketing function must have a process for handling:

- Incorrect product information.
- Incorrect pricing.
- Misleading claims.
- Unapproved creative material.
- Incorrect store attribution.
- Copyright complaints.
- Customer complaints.
- Advertising account issues.
- Campaign overspending.
- Data or privacy incidents.

Material errors must be corrected promptly.

Where a campaign creates a legal, customer or financial risk, the parent company may pause publication while the matter is investigated.

The decision and corrective action must be recorded.

## 20. Implementation Sequence

### Phase 1: Marketing governance

- Establish central marketing responsibilities.
- Define campaign approval rules.
- Establish brand attribution standards.
- Define advertising budget controls.
- Establish content rights procedures.

### Phase 2: Campaign operations

- Create campaign templates.
- Establish creative workflows.
- Define reporting formats.
- Establish store participation records.
- Create a campaign approval process.

### Phase 3: Commercial services

- Publish optional marketing services.
- Define service rates.
- Establish quotations and approvals.
- Separate service fees from advertising expenditure.
- Measure service delivery costs.

### Phase 4: Platform integration

- Introduce campaign records.
- Connect participating stores to campaigns.
- Add campaign approval workflows.
- Add Store Owner campaign visibility.
- Add performance reporting.

### Phase 5: Optimisation

- Measure campaign effectiveness.
- Improve creative performance.
- Evaluate customer acquisition costs.
- Review store participation.
- Refine shared marketing allocation.

## 21. Final Standard

The parent company's marketing agency must act as a shared growth engine for participating stores while independently promoting the parent company's own platform and services.

Every store must remain identifiable as its own brand and business.

Campaigns must have clear consent, attribution, creative rights, budget approval and performance reporting.

The parent company may provide strategy, production and campaign execution, but it must not claim another person's brand, work, audience or business achievements.

The objective is to build a platform where the success of participating stores contributes to the sustainability and growth of the parent company, while the parent company's shared resources create genuine value for each participating Store Owner.
