# Platform Governance

**Status:** DRAFT
**Version:** 0.1
**Last updated:** 2026-11-07

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the governance framework for the centrally operated multi-store retail platform.

It establishes how platform authority is exercised, how responsibilities are divided between the Platform Owner and participating stores, how decisions are made, and how accountability is maintained.

The framework is intended to support consistent platform operations while preserving the operational autonomy of participating stores.

## 2. Governance Objectives

Platform governance must:

- Establish clear authority and accountability.
- Protect the interests of the platform and participating stores.
- Define the boundaries between platform-level and store-level decisions.
- Ensure that decisions affecting stores are explainable and recorded.
- Protect store data, commercial information and operational independence.
- Establish fair and consistent processes for resolving disputes.
- Support responsible financial and operational oversight.
- Provide controlled processes for onboarding, suspension and exit.
- Support sustainable platform growth without unnecessary bureaucracy.

## 3. Governance Principles

### 3.1 Clear authority

Every material platform and store decision must have an identifiable authority.

Authority must come from an assigned role, approved delegation or documented governance process.

### 3.2 Proportionate oversight

Platform oversight must be proportionate to the platform's responsibilities, the risks involved and the agreements governing each store.

Routine store operations should not require unnecessary platform approval.

### 3.3 Store operational autonomy

Store Admins remain responsible for ordinary operations within their stores and their assigned authority.

The platform must not assume control over ordinary store decisions merely because it provides shared infrastructure or business support.

### 3.4 Accountability

Material decisions, approvals, interventions and exceptions must be attributable to the responsible person or authorised process.

### 3.5 Transparency

Stores must receive relevant information about decisions that materially affect their operations, access, commercial arrangements or participation.

Confidential platform information and other stores' private information must remain protected.

### 3.6 Consistency and fairness

Comparable situations should be handled consistently.

Differences in treatment must have a legitimate and explainable reason.

### 3.7 Separation of responsibilities

Platform governance, store administration, financial recording and technical administration must have clearly defined responsibilities.

Where practical, sensitive actions should not be controlled entirely by one person.

### 3.8 Human accountability

Automated metrics, performance scores and system recommendations may support decisions but must not independently determine significant commercial or operational interventions.

## 4. Governance Structure

### 4.1 Platform Owner

The Platform Owner holds overall responsibility for the centrally operated platform.

Responsibilities include:

- Establishing platform strategy and direction.
- Approving platform-wide policies.
- Appointing and authorising platform-level administrators.
- Overseeing participating store relationships.
- Approving material platform changes.
- Establishing platform commercial arrangements.
- Overseeing platform finances and obligations.
- Reviewing platform performance and risks.
- Authorising significant platform interventions.
- Ensuring appropriate governance records are maintained.

The Platform Owner's authority must be exercised within applicable agreements, legal obligations and approved platform policies.

### 4.2 Platform Super Admin

The Platform Super Admin performs authorised platform-level administrative functions.

Responsibilities may include:

- Creating and configuring stores.
- Managing store lifecycle operations.
- Administering platform-level access.
- Configuring shared platform capabilities.
- Reviewing store-level administrative activity where authorised.
- Managing platform-wide operational settings.
- Coordinating platform support and administration.

The Platform Super Admin role does not automatically grant unrestricted authority to alter every store's commercial, financial or operational records.

Sensitive actions must follow the applicable permission, approval and audit requirements.

### 4.3 Store Admin

Each participating store has an authorised Store Admin responsible for its ordinary administration.

Responsibilities include:

- Managing store operations.
- Managing store employees and delegated permissions.
- Managing store products and commercial listings within authorised boundaries.
- Managing store inventory and stock movements.
- Managing store orders and fulfilment.
- Managing store customers within applicable privacy rules.
- Maintaining store financial records.
- Reviewing store performance.
- Responding to platform governance requests.
- Maintaining accurate store information.

A Store Admin cannot grant permissions beyond the authority delegated to that role.

### 4.4 Store Employees

Store employees perform assigned operational responsibilities.

Their authority is determined by permissions delegated by the Store Admin and enforced by the platform.

Employees must not use access granted for one responsibility to perform unrelated privileged actions.

### 4.5 External Parties

External service providers, professional advisers and business partners may participate in platform or store activities under approved arrangements.

Their access and authority must be limited to the purpose of their engagement.

External parties do not acquire platform governance authority merely through a service relationship.

## 5. Authority and Decision Boundaries

### 5.1 Platform-level decisions

Platform-level authority covers decisions concerning the shared platform and its centrally operated services.

Examples include:

- Platform strategy and development priorities.
- Shared technical infrastructure.
- Platform-wide security requirements.
- Store onboarding criteria.
- Platform-wide operating policies.
- Platform-owned provider relationships.
- Platform commercial arrangements.
- Platform-level financial obligations.
- Platform-wide service restrictions.

### 5.2 Store-level decisions

Store-level authority covers ordinary operations within an individual participating store.

Examples include:

- Store employee scheduling.
- Employee task allocation.
- Store-specific product selection.
- Store pricing and commercial terms within applicable agreements.
- Store inventory management.
- Order fulfilment.
- Store customer service.
- Store operating procedures.
- Store-level marketing participation.
- Store operating expenses.

### 5.3 Shared decisions

Some matters affect both the platform and a participating store.

Examples include:

- Changes to shared services affecting store operations.
- Store participation in platform marketing campaigns.
- Shared provider arrangements.
- Stock or capital assistance.
- Material changes to store commercial arrangements.
- Store-specific integrations with shared platform services.

The applicable agreement, policy and decision authority must determine whether platform approval, store approval or mutual agreement is required.

### 5.4 Reserved decisions

Certain decisions must remain with the designated authority and must not be assumed by another role.

Reserved decisions may include:

- Changes to platform ownership or control.
- Approval of platform-wide governance policies.
- Material changes to platform commercial terms.
- Significant platform financial commitments.
- Exceptional restrictions affecting a participating store.
- Approval of material stock or capital assistance.
- Changes to another store's ownership or administrative authority.

The specific approval requirements must be documented before these decisions are implemented.

### 5.5 No implied authority

Access to platform data, administrative tools or operational reports does not automatically grant authority to make decisions about that data or the business it represents.

Technical capability must not be treated as business approval.

## 6. Delegation of Authority

### 6.1 Delegation principles

Authority may be delegated where the delegating role is permitted to do so.

Delegation must be explicit, limited and traceable.

### 6.2 Platform delegation

The Platform Owner may delegate platform administrative responsibilities to authorised Platform Super Admins or other approved platform personnel.

Delegation must specify its intended scope.

### 6.3 Store delegation

Store Admins may delegate ordinary store responsibilities to employees through the platform's permission system.

Delegated permissions must remain within the Store Admin's own authority.

### 6.4 Limits of delegation

Delegation must not:

- Transfer ownership without an approved ownership process.
- Grant access to another store without authority.
- Bypass platform security controls.
- Grant platform-level privileges through store administration.
- Override financial approval requirements.
- Remove required audit records.
- Authorise actions prohibited by applicable agreements or policies.

### 6.5 Delegation review

Delegated access must be reviewed when responsibilities change, employment ends, an administrator changes or a security concern arises.

Unnecessary access must be revoked promptly.

### 6.6 Emergency delegation

Temporary emergency authority may be granted where necessary to protect platform or store operations.

It must have a defined purpose, scope, duration and approving authority.

Emergency delegation must be recorded and reviewed after the immediate situation has been addressed.

## 7. Governance Records and Accountability

### 7.1 Recordkeeping

Material governance decisions must be recorded in an appropriate platform-controlled record.

Records should identify:

- The decision or action.
- The responsible person.
- The approving authority, where applicable.
- The affected platform or store.
- The date and time.
- The reason and relevant context.
- Supporting evidence or references.
- Any conditions or limitations.
- The resulting action or follow-up.

### 7.2 Decision traceability

A decision must be traceable from its request or proposal through approval, implementation and any subsequent review.

### 7.3 Policy records

Approved governance policies must have identifiable versions, effective dates, owners and approval records.

Superseded policies should remain available for historical reference where appropriate.

### 7.4 Exceptions

Exceptions to approved governance requirements must be documented with their scope, justification, approving authority and review conditions.

An exception must not silently become a permanent policy.

### 7.5 Access to governance records

Access to governance records must be restricted according to role, business need, confidentiality and applicable privacy requirements.

Stores may access records relevant to their own participation and operations, subject to legitimate confidentiality restrictions.

### 7.6 Accountability reviews

Material decisions and interventions should be reviewed where necessary to establish whether they were authorised, proportionate and consistent with applicable policies.


## 8. Store Onboarding Governance

### 8.1 Onboarding authority

The Platform Super Admin may initiate store creation through the authorised platform administration process.

Store onboarding must follow platform-approved eligibility, verification and configuration requirements.

### 8.2 Store information

The onboarding process must capture the information necessary to establish the store's identity, ownership or operating authority, contact details and operational configuration.

Information requirements must be proportionate to the intended store relationship.

### 8.3 Store participation approval

A store must not be treated as an approved participating business merely because a technical store record has been created.

The platform must distinguish technical provisioning from business approval and activation.

### 8.4 Agreements and commercial terms

Applicable participation agreements, commercial arrangements, responsibilities and obligations must be documented before the relevant activities begin.

Unresolved commercial terms must not be silently assumed.

### 8.5 Store administrator appointment

The platform must establish the initial authorised Store Admin through a controlled process.

The appointment and resulting access must be recorded.

### 8.6 Store provisioning

Store creation must initiate the required technical provisioning process, including the store-specific integration package described in the Product Catalogue and Integration Architecture documents.

Provisioning must not expose privileged platform credentials or grant unrestricted access to shared services.

### 8.7 Onboarding completion

Onboarding completion must confirm that required business approvals, store configuration, administrative access and operational prerequisites have been addressed.

Incomplete onboarding must remain distinguishable from an active, approved store.

## 9. Store Lifecycle Governance

### 9.1 Lifecycle management

Participating stores must be governed through a controlled lifecycle.

The lifecycle may include:

- Proposed.
- Under review.
- Approved.
- Provisioning.
- Active.
- Temporarily restricted.
- Suspended.
- Exiting.
- Deactivated.

The final lifecycle states and transition rules must be established through the Store Architecture.

### 9.2 Activation

A store may be activated after the applicable onboarding and approval requirements have been satisfied.

Activation must be authorised and recorded.

### 9.3 Operational restrictions

Restrictions may be applied where necessary to protect platform integrity, customers, stores or other legitimate interests.

Restrictions must have a defined scope, reason and authorised decision-maker.

### 9.4 Suspension

Suspension must be distinguished from ordinary operational restrictions and emergency protective measures.

Where practical, the affected store must receive the reason, effective date, scope and available review process.

### 9.5 Emergency protective action

Immediate protective action may be necessary where there is a credible risk involving security, fraud, customer harm, financial loss or platform integrity.

Emergency action may be taken by an authorised role within its defined emergency authority.

The action must be recorded and reviewed promptly.

### 9.6 Store exit

A store may exit through an agreed process or an applicable contractual or policy mechanism.

The process must address outstanding orders, customer obligations, financial settlements, stock, access, integrations and retained records.

### 9.7 Deactivation and historical preservation

Deactivation must not automatically delete historical transactions, audit records, financial obligations or records that must be retained.

Data retention and deletion must follow applicable legal obligations and approved retention policies.

## 10. Financial Governance

### 10.1 Financial separation

Platform finances and store finances must remain separately identifiable.

Financial reporting must distinguish platform revenue, platform expenses, store revenue, store expenses and amounts attributable to shared arrangements.

### 10.2 Financial authority

Financial commitments, payments, adjustments, settlements and assistance must be performed by authorised persons within documented limits.

### 10.3 Commercial arrangements

Commission arrangements, service charges, shared costs and other commercial terms must be documented and approved by the appropriate parties.

No default commission rate or settlement arrangement is established by this governance document.

### 10.4 Stock and capital assistance

Stock or capital assistance must follow a documented assessment and approval process.

The assessment should consider:

- The purpose of the assistance.
- The amount or stock involved.
- The expected benefit.
- Repayment or settlement expectations, where applicable.
- Risks and supporting evidence.
- The responsibilities of each party.
- Approval authority.
- Monitoring and review requirements.

Performance scores alone must not automatically approve or reject assistance.

### 10.5 Financial records

Financial decisions and transactions must be supported by appropriate records, evidence and authorisation.

Corrections must preserve the original transaction history and record the reason for the adjustment.

### 10.6 Reconciliation

Platform and store financial obligations must be reconciled at appropriate intervals.

Disputed or unexplained differences must be investigated before adjustments or settlements are authorised.

### 10.7 Professional oversight

Accounting, tax and legal requirements must be assessed with appropriately qualified South African professionals where necessary.

This document does not determine the legal or tax status of participating stores.

## 11. Performance and Oversight Governance

### 11.1 Purpose of performance oversight

Performance oversight exists to support informed decisions, identify operational challenges and improve participating store outcomes.

It must not be used as an unexplained or automatic mechanism for imposing penalties.

### 11.2 Performance information

Performance reports should use reliable and relevant information from authoritative platform domains.

Metrics must identify their source, calculation method, reporting period and relevant limitations.

### 11.3 Fair comparisons

Store comparisons must account for relevant differences, including:

- Operating history.
- Product categories.
- Product availability.
- Inventory levels.
- Store capacity.
- Marketing exposure.
- Agreed targets.
- Relevant operational circumstances.

### 11.4 Store context

Store Admins must have an appropriate opportunity to provide context where performance results do not reflect relevant operating circumstances.

Material disagreements should be recorded and considered during reviews.

### 11.5 Performance interventions

Improvement plans, operational recommendations and support measures must have identifiable objectives and responsible parties.

Any intervention affecting commercial terms, financial assistance, access or store participation must follow its separate approval requirements.

### 11.6 Human decision-making

Automated metrics and recommendations may inform governance decisions.

They must not independently impose significant financial, commercial or operational consequences.

## 12. Platform Policies and Compliance

### 12.1 Policy authority

Platform-wide policies must have an identifiable owner and approval authority.

Policies must define their intended scope and the stores, roles or services to which they apply.

### 12.2 Policy communication

Material policy changes must be communicated to affected stores and administrators before their effective date where reasonably practical.

Urgent security or legal changes may require immediate implementation, followed by appropriate communication.

### 12.3 Policy acknowledgement

Where acknowledgement is required, the platform should record the relevant person, policy version and acknowledgement date.

Acknowledgement must not be treated as a substitute for a legally required agreement or consent.

### 12.4 Compliance responsibilities

Platform and store responsibilities must be distinguished.

The platform is responsible for its own obligations and shared services.

Each store remains responsible for obligations assigned to it by applicable law, agreements and its operating arrangements.

### 12.5 Legal and regulatory review

Policies involving privacy, consumer protection, electronic communications, employment, taxation, financial transactions or other regulated matters must receive appropriate professional review.

### 12.6 Non-compliance

Suspected non-compliance must be assessed using relevant evidence, applicable policy and the seriousness of the issue.

Responses should be proportionate and consistently applied.

### 12.7 Policy exceptions

Exceptions must be authorised, documented and reviewed.

An exception must not override applicable law or contractual obligations.

## 13. Conflicts of Interest and Dispute Resolution

### 13.1 Conflicts of interest

People involved in platform or store decisions must disclose material conflicts of interest where those conflicts could affect impartial decision-making.

### 13.2 Decision independence

Where practical, a person with a material conflict should not be the sole approver of the affected decision.

An alternative authorised decision-maker should be identified.

### 13.3 Store disputes

Disputes between the platform and a participating store should follow a documented resolution process.

The process should identify:

- The parties involved.
- The issue being disputed.
- Relevant agreements and policies.
- Supporting evidence.
- The responsible reviewer.
- Proposed resolution.
- Any agreed corrective action.
- The final outcome.

### 13.4 Internal escalation

Where a dispute cannot be resolved at the operational level, it should be escalated to the appropriate platform authority.

A person should not be required to approve their own disputed decision where an independent reviewer is reasonably available.

### 13.5 Financial disputes

Financial disputes must be investigated using authoritative transaction records, supporting evidence and applicable agreements.

Disputed amounts must not be altered or settled without appropriate authority.

### 13.6 Technical disputes

Technical disputes involving service availability, access, data integrity or integrations should be assessed using relevant system records and technical evidence.

### 13.7 Resolution records

Material disputes and their outcomes must be documented.

Records should distinguish allegations, verified facts, decisions and unresolved issues.

### 13.8 External resolution

Where internal resolution is unsuccessful or inappropriate, the parties may use applicable contractual, professional or legal dispute-resolution mechanisms.

## 14. Governance Reviews and Continuous Improvement

### 14.1 Review purpose

Governance reviews assess whether the platform's authority structures, policies and controls remain effective and appropriate.

### 14.2 Review areas

Reviews may include:

- Platform policy effectiveness.
- Store participation and lifecycle decisions.
- Delegated access.
- Financial oversight.
- Store performance governance.
- Security and privacy controls.
- Disputes and complaints.
- Exceptions and emergency actions.
- Operational risks.
- Outstanding governance decisions.

### 14.3 Review frequency

Review frequency must reflect operational risk, business needs and applicable obligations.

Specific review schedules remain subject to approval.

### 14.4 Corrective actions

Reviews may produce recommendations, corrective actions or policy changes.

Each material action should have an owner, expected outcome and appropriate completion tracking.

### 14.5 Governance improvement

Lessons from incidents, disputes, audits and operational experience should inform improvements to platform governance.

Changes must follow the applicable approval and documentation process.


## 15. Governance Risks and Controls

### 15.1 Governance risk management

The platform must identify and manage risks arising from unclear authority, inadequate oversight, conflicts of interest and inconsistent decision-making.

### 15.2 Unauthorised decisions

Material decisions made outside an individual's authority must be investigated and addressed through the applicable governance and access-control processes.

### 15.3 Concentration of authority

The platform should identify situations where one person can initiate, approve and execute a sensitive action without independent oversight.

Appropriate approval separation should be introduced where practical and proportionate to risk.

### 15.4 Store interference

Platform oversight must not be used to interfere unnecessarily with ordinary store operations.

Any material intervention must have an identifiable authority, legitimate purpose, defined scope and supporting record.

### 15.5 Inconsistent treatment

Differences in store treatment must be explainable through relevant circumstances, agreements, policy requirements or documented decisions.

### 15.6 Information misuse

Platform and store information must only be accessed and used for authorised purposes.

Confidential business information must not be used to create unfair advantages or expose another store's private commercial information.

### 15.7 Governance control failures

Failures in governance controls must be recorded, assessed and addressed through corrective action.

Where the failure creates an immediate risk, appropriate protective measures may be taken within authorised emergency powers.

## 16. Governance Communication

### 16.1 Communication principles

Governance communication must be accurate, relevant, timely and appropriate to the intended recipient.

### 16.2 Platform-wide communication

Platform-wide governance changes and announcements must be communicated through approved platform channels.

### 16.3 Store-specific communication

A store must receive information relevant to its own participation, responsibilities, obligations and operational decisions.

### 16.4 Decision notifications

Where a governance decision materially affects a store, the affected Store Admin should receive the decision, its effective date and any applicable next steps.

### 16.5 Confidential communication

Sensitive governance matters must be communicated only to authorised recipients.

Communication must not expose confidential information belonging to another store or individual.

### 16.6 Communication records

Material governance notices, acknowledgements and decision communications should be retained where they provide evidence of a decision, obligation or required action.

## 17. Governance Data and Information Management

### 17.1 Governance records

Governance information must be stored in appropriate controlled records.

Records may include policies, approvals, delegations, decisions, exceptions, disputes, reviews and corrective actions.

### 17.2 Record ownership

The platform is responsible for maintaining platform-level governance records.

Store-specific governance records must be associated with the relevant store and protected by appropriate access controls.

### 17.3 Data integrity

Governance records must preserve their history and support traceability.

Changes to material records must identify the responsible person, timestamp and reason where applicable.

### 17.4 Retention

Retention periods must be defined according to record purpose, applicable legal requirements, contractual obligations and approved platform policies.

### 17.5 Access and disclosure

Access to governance information must follow role-based permissions, confidentiality requirements and legitimate business need.

Disclosure to external parties must be authorised and appropriately recorded where necessary.

### 17.6 Historical records

Store deactivation or administrator changes must not automatically remove governance records needed for accountability, dispute resolution, financial reconciliation or legal compliance.

### 17.7 Privacy

Personal information contained in governance records must be limited to what is necessary for the governance purpose.

Handling, retention, disclosure and deletion must follow applicable privacy requirements.

## 18. Governance of Shared Services and External Relationships

### 18.1 Shared service governance

Shared platform services must operate under centrally approved policies and controls.

The platform must communicate material service changes that affect participating stores.

### 18.2 Store-specific configurations

Store-specific configurations must remain within supported platform capabilities.

A store must not gain authority to alter shared infrastructure or another store's configuration through its own administrative access.

### 18.3 External provider relationships

Provider relationships must have identifiable ownership and responsibility.

The platform must distinguish platform-managed provider arrangements from provider arrangements managed independently by a store.

### 18.4 Provider access

External provider access must be limited to the services and information required for the provider's approved purpose.

Access must be reviewed and revoked when no longer required.

### 18.5 Provider changes

Material provider changes must be assessed for security, operational continuity, cost, contractual obligations and effects on participating stores.

### 18.6 Service interruptions

Where a shared service interruption materially affects stores, the platform should communicate relevant impact, mitigation and recovery information.

### 18.7 Store-specific impact

A shared service decision must consider whether its effects differ across stores.

A change that is suitable for one store must not automatically be assumed suitable for every participating store.

## 19. Governance Approval and Change Control

### 19.1 Approval requirements

Governance decisions must follow the approval authority applicable to their subject, scope and risk.

### 19.2 Approval evidence

Approval records should identify the approver, decision, date, scope and relevant supporting information.

### 19.3 Material changes

Material changes to platform ownership, governance structure, commercial arrangements, store participation requirements or platform-wide policies require formal review and approval.

### 19.4 Operational changes

Routine operational changes may be delegated to the responsible platform or store role where permitted by approved policies.

### 19.5 Emergency changes

Emergency changes may be implemented under authorised emergency procedures where delay would create unacceptable risk.

The reason, authority, scope and implementation must be recorded and reviewed afterwards.

### 19.6 Change communication

Affected stores and administrators must receive relevant information about approved changes before implementation where reasonably practical.

### 19.7 Implementation verification

Material governance changes must be checked after implementation to confirm that the intended authority, policy or control is effective.

## 20. Governance Constraints

The following constraints apply:

- Platform authority and store authority must remain distinguishable.
- Store Admins retain ordinary operational autonomy within their authorised responsibilities.
- Store-level delegation must not create platform-level privileges.
- Platform administration must not automatically imply unrestricted control over store finances or operations.
- Material interventions must be authorised, proportionate and recorded.
- Store participation and technical provisioning must remain distinct.
- Financial arrangements must be documented rather than assumed.
- Performance metrics must not independently determine significant commercial consequences.
- Sensitive decisions should have appropriate separation of responsibilities.
- Emergency actions must be limited to legitimate protective purposes and reviewed.
- Store data and confidential commercial information must remain appropriately isolated.
- Governance records must remain traceable and protected.
- Legal and regulatory obligations take precedence over internal policy.
- No governance provision should be interpreted as creating a legal relationship that has not been established through appropriate agreements.

## 21. Open Governance Decisions

The following matters require further specification and approval.

| Decision area | Open question |
|---|---|
| Governance ownership | Who will formally own and maintain platform governance policies? |
| Platform delegation | Which platform responsibilities may be delegated, and to whom? |
| Approval limits | What financial and operational approval limits will apply? |
| Store eligibility | What criteria must a business satisfy before participation? |
| Participation agreements | Which agreements must be completed before activation? |
| Store suspension | What circumstances and approval requirements justify suspension? |
| Emergency authority | Which roles may initiate emergency protective action? |
| Independent review | Who reviews disputes involving the Platform Owner or Platform Super Admin? |
| Financial oversight | What independent review or reconciliation arrangements are required? |
| Governance reviews | What review schedule and reporting format will be adopted? |
| Policy acknowledgement | Which policies require formal acknowledgement? |
| Record retention | What retention periods apply to each governance record category? |
| Store exit | What contractual and operational exit procedures will apply? |
| External advisers | Which professional advisers will support legal, tax and accounting matters? |
| Dispute resolution | Which formal dispute-resolution mechanisms will be adopted? |

These decisions must be resolved through appropriate business, legal, financial and operational review.

## 23. Governance Document Management

### 23.1 Document ownership

The designated platform governance owner is responsible for maintaining this document.

### 23.2 Approval

This document must be reviewed and approved by the authorised business owner before being treated as an approved governance policy.

### 23.3 Version control

Material changes must be reflected in the document version and change history.

### 23.4 Review

The document should be reviewed when platform ownership, participating store relationships, governance responsibilities or applicable obligations materially change.

### 23.5 Consistency

Changes must be considered alongside related business and architecture documents during the consolidated documentation review.

### 23.6 Exceptions

Any approved exception to this document must be recorded with its justification, authority, scope and review conditions.

### 23.7 Implementation alignment

Actual platform governance processes must be aligned with the approved version of this document.

Unimplemented proposals and unresolved decisions must remain clearly distinguished from active policy.
